Open the ledger: four concrete cases

Economic words become useful when they resolve into a perimeter, instrument, counterparty, date, flow and failure mode.

27.9% and 7.6% can both be true

Eurostat reported Denmark's general-government gross debt at 27.9% of GDP at end-2025. Danmarks Nationalbank reported central-government debt of DKK 235 billion, equal to 7.6% of GDP.

same country → different government perimeter → different debt object → different valid ratio

Use: always ask who is consolidated into the measure before treating two debt numbers as contradictory.

Eurostat · Danmarks Nationalbank

Debt has a maturity clock

The Danish debt-management report describes individual financing instruments, including long- and short-maturity borrowing, and reports DKK 5 billion in central-government interest costs for 2025.

funding need → bond/loan → currency/rate → maturity → refinancing → interest cost

Use: a debt stock becomes a risk profile only when the instrument structure and timing are visible.

Fiscal-capacity owner

A bank can be asset-rich and liquidity-poor

The Federal Reserve's review of Silicon Valley Bank describes interest-rate risk, concentrated uninsured deposits, governance/risk-management weaknesses and a rapid run.

rate sensitivity + funding concentration → liquidity pressure → loss signal → withdrawals → failure

Use: total assets are not enough. Market value, timing and funding behavior can dominate near-term survival.

Energy dependence reaches the budget through transmission

EU energy import dependency was 57% in 2024. That is an energy-system observation—not a debt measure. Fiscal exposure appears only when price/supply effects hit taxes, transfers, subsidies, public investment or state-linked balance sheets.

import dependence → shock → household/firm cost → policy response → fiscal flow / liability

Use: follow the mechanism instead of treating adjacent indicators as the same thing.
