The Eddas

The Atlas stores two complete reusable English editions locally: Henry Adams Bellows's 1923 translation of the Poetic Edda and Rasmus Björn Anderson's 1897 edition of the Younger / Prose Edda. The text itself is preserved separately from interpretation.

Poetic Edda

A collection of mythological and heroic poems transmitted in medieval Icelandic manuscripts and edited through later scholarship. This edition contains the complete Bellows translation, including the major mythological poems and the heroic material surrounding the Völsung and related cycles.

Structure: cosmology, wisdom poetry, divine narrative, prophecy, heroic legend, genealogy, tragedy.

Prose Edda

Snorri Sturluson's mythological and poetic handbook, represented here through the 1897 Rasmus Björn Anderson edition. Its major components include Gylfaginning, Brage's Speech, Skáldskaparmál and Háttatal.

Structure: mythological narrative, cosmology, gods, poetic diction, kennings and metres.

CROSS-TEXT READING

Major axes

Creation

Völuspá, Vafþrúðnismál and Gylfaginning give different textual forms of primordial order, emergence and the making of the world.

Yggdrasil

Völuspá, Grímnismál, Vafþrúðnismál and Gylfaginning can be read together to compare poetic and prose presentations of the world-tree and cosmic geography.

Wisdom

Hávamál, Vafþrúðnismál, Fáfnismál and Sigrdrífumál approach knowledge through counsel, contest, prophecy and instruction.

Ragnarök

Völuspá and Gylfaginning are especially useful for comparing poetic prophecy with Snorri's prose synthesis, while other poems contribute episodes and motifs.

ATLAS METHOD

Text first, interpretation second

The stored files are the primary textual layer. The structure map in structure.json supplies navigation and subject axes; it does not silently rewrite the text. Comparative claims belong in separate research records and must distinguish textual parallel, structural analogy, historical influence, contradiction and unknown.
