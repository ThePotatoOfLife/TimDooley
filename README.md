# The Potato of Life — Living Atlas

This repository is the source archive and research engine for the Tim Dooley / Potatoverse project.

It is deliberately being built as more than a themed website. The aim is a **living knowledge graph** in which the mythic architecture, philosophical interpretations, historical material and observable world are connected without pretending they have identical evidentiary status.

## The architecture

The main unfolding is:

**Tim Dooley → Source → Separation → Ladder → Door → Son → Tree of Life → Tree of Strife → Potato → World → North Programme**

The tree is the route through the archive. The graph is the underlying reality of the archive: children can connect across branches.

## Kingdom Atlas

The Kingdom Atlas is the broad relationship ontology. It is not just a list of countries. It is where the archive records:

- who owns what;
- who controls what;
- who owes whom;
- who supplies whom;
- which institution regulates whom;
- where infrastructure physically sits;
- where money flows;
- where energy comes from and where it goes;
- where contracts and procurement connect public and private actors;
- which technologies depend on which external inputs;
- where labour and skills are concentrated;
- where geopolitical and security relationships overlap with economic ones.

The relationship families include genealogical, institutional, financial, political, religious, intellectual, geographic, cultural, technological, information, social, symbolic, functional and mythological relationships.

## European Economic Graph

The empirical layer is intended to become an observable map of Europe's economic system rather than a collection of country summaries.

Its growing node families include:

- states and subnational governments;
- EU institutions and monetary institutions;
- companies and ownership chains;
- banks, funds and capital markets;
- public debt and bonds;
- taxes, expenditure, pensions and healthcare;
- public procurement;
- ports, roads, rail, electricity grids, pipelines and subsea cables;
- energy generation, storage, fuels and industrial demand;
- manufacturing and industrial capacity;
- trade and supply chains;
- semiconductor and technology dependencies;
- cloud and AI compute;
- universities and research systems;
- labour markets, skills and demographic structure;
- strategic dependencies and bottlenecks.

The first priority is not to declare what should be done. It is to map what actually exists, who is connected to it, how much it costs, what depends on it, and what evidence supports the relationship.

## Real-world anchors already in the data

The repository currently contains empirical nodes for the EU, Denmark, the Kingdom of Denmark, Greenland, France, Belgium, the European energy system and finance/public debt. The expanded registry now also makes room for the North Atlantic, North Sea, Arctic, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Iceland, Canada, the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Turkey and the Netherlands, plus infrastructure and technology systems.

The repository's current fiscal baseline uses Eurostat's Q4 2025 government-finance data: EU government debt 81.7% of GDP, France 115.6%, Belgium 107.9% and Denmark 27.9%. It also records the EU single market and Energy Union as real institutional systems rather than abstract themes.

## Research queue

The queue is intentionally concrete. It should grow into actual datasets and relationship records:

1. **France expenditure structure** — map spending by function, level of government and programme; identify duplication before proposing reductions.
2. **Belgium institutional structure** — map federal, regional and community competences against administrative spending and service delivery.
3. **European energy network** — generation, interconnectors, storage, LNG, gas pipelines, nuclear, renewables, demand and industrial clusters.
4. **North Sea infrastructure** — offshore wind, oil and gas, ports, cables, vessels, fisheries and coastal industry.
5. **North Atlantic infrastructure** — Greenland, Iceland, Denmark, Norway, Britain and Canada through ports, cables, shipping, energy and research.
6. **Public procurement** — major purchasing categories, central purchasing bodies, framework agreements and cross-border opportunities.
7. **Strategic technology** — semiconductors, cloud, AI compute, industrial software, telecom equipment and critical inputs.
8. **Industrial base** — machinery, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, vehicles, aerospace, energy-intensive industry and productive capacity.
9. **Labour and skills** — participation, shortages, migration, training, productivity and regional concentration.
10. **Ownership and control** — corporate parents, subsidiaries, funds, state ownership, infrastructure operators and beneficial-control relationships.

## Evidence standard

The repository explicitly separates:

- observed facts;
- calculated values;
- estimates;
- scenarios;
- historical claims;
- interpretations;
- mythology;
- creative material.

A symbolic relationship can be preserved as part of the Tim Dooley architecture without being presented as empirical proof. Conversely, an empirical node should be anchored to a source, date and jurisdiction whenever possible.

## The rule

**Relationships first.**

A country is a node. A debt is a node. A company is a node. A symbol is a node. A cable is a node. A port is a node. A contract is a node. A research institute is a node.

But the real object of study is the relationship between them.

That is how the project stops being a generic mythology page and becomes a living atlas: every concept can acquire children, every child can acquire records, and every record can connect outward into the real world.
