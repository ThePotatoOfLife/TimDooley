How to read this political corpus

This page describes Tim Dooley's recovered political statements and programme ideas; it does not ask the reader to adopt them. The archive keeps several modes separate because a direct position, a policy proposal, a forecast, a research question and a symbolic world-model should not be treated as the same kind of political commitment.

Explicit position

A recoverable statement of support, opposition, preference or self-description attributed directly to Tim.

Policy proposal

A suggested intervention. It still requires legal authority, financing, implementation design and outcome testing.

Programme design

A larger architecture such as North, European capability, digital sovereignty or institutional reform; broader than a single policy.

Prediction / forecast

A claim about what may happen. It should be evaluated against the earliest wording, information available at the time and later events.

Research lead

A relationship or institution Tim wants investigated. Interest in a hypothesis is not evidence that the hypothesis is true.

Symbolic model

North/West/East/South, biblical or mythic political language can organize interpretation without becoming a literal treaty map.

Political development matters. Tim's recovered positions are multidimensional and incomplete. The archive preserves unresolved areas instead of inferring answers, and distinguishes Tim's own self-label “radically centered” from later archive synthesis labels.

Recovered political material

The politics page should show the corpus, not merely promise one

This section exposes representative entries from the canonical political compendium directly in the page. They are presented as Tim-attributed self-description, position, proposal, programme design, forecast or research lead according to the archive record. They are not endorsements, and they do not become current law or institutional fact merely because they appear in the project.

~2015 · explicit position · civil liberty / drug policy

Drug policy begins with harm differentiation rather than one moral category

The recovered corpus records cannabis advocacy and later opposition to treating all drug use as one criminal or moral category. The position emphasizes prevention, rehabilitation and harm differentiation, while explicitly distinguishing psychedelics and other substances from heroin, fentanyl, heavy narcotics and sedatives. The archive does not generalize this into an unrestricted all-drug legalisation doctrine.

15 January 2026 · explicit position · sovereignty

Greenland and Danish sovereignty are treated as boundaries, not tradable abstractions

The compendium records Tim framing sovereignty, country and soil as non-negotiable under external pressure. Strategic access, security or economic arrangements may still be questions for negotiation, but the attributed position does not treat sovereignty itself as an ordinary transferable asset.

February–May 2026 · programme design · Europe / digital capability

European capability becomes a systems programme

Across several 2026 entries, North and European autonomy move from symbolic orientation toward practical questions: defence burden-sharing, interoperable cloud and compute, encrypted data exchange, multiple vendors, energy security, production and institutional memory. The important archive distinction is that this is a programme design, not evidence that the proposed institutions or alliances already exist.

2 June 2026 · explicit position · state capacity

The recurring distinction is capable versus wasteful government, not simply big versus small

The recovered statement set combines preference for an efficient state with support for healthcare, education, welfare, infrastructure, defence, technology, industry and energy security. That creates a tension the page should preserve rather than flatten into a conventional left/right label: the attributed view asks whether public capacity produces durable outcomes and whether spending can be justified, measured and corrected.

24 June 2026 · self-description

“Radically centered” is the clearest recovered political self-label

The political compendium treats this as Tim's own label. Later archive phrases such as “Nordic civic developmentalism” or “radical pragmatic center” are synthesis terms created to describe patterns across the corpus; they should not be silently substituted for the self-description.

27 August 2026 · explicit position · mixed political economy

Public services and markets are both retained

The recovered material supports public schools, healthcare/hospitals, unions, public prisons and a social safety net while also retaining markets, private property, entrepreneurship and contribution expectations. The page should therefore model the position as a mixed political economy rather than forcing it into a single doctrinal category.

2 September 2026 · policy proposal · institutional audit

“Reverse Jenga” is a sequential audit idea, not an instruction to demolish the state

The proposal is to examine public entities, programmes, NGOs, companies or branches for measurable value and then simplify, merge, repurpose, pause or remove structures sequentially. The core mechanism is reversibility and testing: change one load-bearing element, observe consequences, and avoid assuming a replacement will work merely because the existing structure is disliked.

2026 · forecast / prediction cluster

Forecasts belong in Prediction Time, not inside the position list

Claims about U.S.–Europe separation, troop retrenchment, bond refinancing pressure or North/Greenland developments are stored as forecasts with their own timing and resolution requirements. The politics page may explain why they matter to the worldview, but whether they were specific, prior and later matched belongs to the prediction audit rather than political self-description.

What remains unresolved

The canonical compendium deliberately keeps major topics open rather than inferring a position from ideology. The unresolved list currently includes abortion, detailed LGBTQ/trans law, death penalty, exact gun-control regime, universal basic income, minimum wage, rent control, full tax code, electoral-system design, central-bank mandate, NATO end-state, Ukraine territorial settlement, Russia sanctions end-state, nuclear doctrine, China/Taiwan doctrine, Israel–Palestine final-status questions, asylum quotas and a full climate/energy deadline framework.

Editorial rule: an unresolved topic stays unresolved until a recoverable source exists. A political page becomes more trustworthy by showing negative space rather than filling it with inference.
