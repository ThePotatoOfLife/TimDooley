Jain traditions

LIBERATION THROUGH
NON-HARM & DISCIPLINE

Jain history combines a sacred succession of Tirthankaras with a historically recoverable Mahavira-associated community and later gradual sectarian differentiation.

Jain tradition remembers twenty-four Tirthankaras in the present cosmic cycle. Historical scholarship treats Mahavira as the clearest ancient historical anchor, while preserving the tradition's longer sacred chronology as a different kind of claim.

Historical clocks

Do not invent a single schism year

6th–5th centuries BCE

Mahavira-associated historical community

Mahavira becomes the major historically recoverable teacher of the ancient Jain community; Jain sacred chronology extends beyond this window.

early centuries CE

Śvetāmbara / Digambara differentiation

The durable sectarian distinction develops over time. Later narratives give dramatic explanations, but the historical split should be rendered as gradual.

late antiquity onward

Textual and monastic stabilization

Canonical histories, monastic rules, commentaries, pilgrimage networks and regional institutions develop differently within the two major traditions.

Major traditions

Shared foundations, different disciplines and canons

Śvetāmbara

One of the two major Jain traditions. It preserves a canonical textual tradition not accepted in the same form by Digambaras, and its mendicant traditions include white-robed monastics.

Digambara

One of the two major Jain traditions. Digambara monastic ideals, canonical history and views of liberation differ in important ways from Śvetāmbara positions.

Sthānakavāsī

A later Śvetāmbara reform current emphasizing non-image temple practice and scriptural/ethical discipline.

Terāpanth

A later reform lineage within the Śvetāmbara Sthānakavāsī field, with distinctive institutional discipline and leadership history.

The strongest shared grammar is ahiṃsā, karma, disciplined conduct, non-attachment and liberation—not a creator-god cosmology. That makes Jainism an important counterexample when comparing world religions through “God” alone.
