Christianity · historical branch map

ONE ROOT,
MANY COMMUNIONS

Councils, communion breaks, reform movements and revivals change the shape of Christianity without giving every branch one simple birthday.

“Christianity” is useful as a family name, but it becomes misleading when it hides the actual branches. Churches disagree about authority, Christology, scripture and tradition, sacraments, baptism, Eucharist, saints and images, church government, salvation, worship and the end of history. Those differences are easier to understand when they are placed on the historical branch where they developed.

How to read the dates: 431, 451, 1054, 1517 and similar dates are usually high-information rupture or consolidation markers. They are not claims that the communities on either side suddenly appeared that year.

Compare by question

What actually differs?

Authority

How do scripture, tradition, councils, bishops, papacy, confessions, congregations and direct spiritual experience relate?

Church

Universal communion, autocephalous churches, episcopal province, presbytery, congregation, connection or movement?

Baptism

Infants or professing believers? Sacrament, covenant sign, ordinance, initiation, testimony—and what does it accomplish?

Eucharist

Real presence, sacramental mystery, spiritual presence, memorial emphasis, frequency and who presides?

Salvation

How are grace, faith, works, justification, sanctification, perseverance, theosis and holiness related?

Worship

Liturgy, icons, preaching, spontaneous prayer, silence, music, charismatic gifts, calendar and sacred space.

These questions are better than asking which denomination is “closest to the Bible.” Each tradition has its own account of scriptural interpretation, historical continuity and authority.
