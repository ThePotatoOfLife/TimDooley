Christianity · Reformation families

REFORM DOES NOT
BRANCH ONCE

The sixteenth- and seventeenth-century Western ruptures generate several distinct Christian families rather than one thing called “Protestantism.”

The Reformation disputes touch authority, justification, sacramental theology, liturgy, church government, political institutions and the meaning of reform itself. The branches overlap historically but do not descend from one single reformer.

Lutheran traditions

1517 controversy1530 Augsburg ConfessionWittenberg / German lands
Authority

Scripture as the primary doctrinal norm within a confessional tradition.

Core emphasis

Justification by grace through faith, Word and sacrament, strong baptismal/Eucharistic theology.

Not simply

“Catholicism minus pope.” Lutheran theology has its own positive confessional architecture.

Reformed & Presbyterian traditions

1520s–1540s formationZürich / GenevaScots Confession 1560
Family

Zwinglian, Calvinian and later Reformed trajectories; Presbyterianism is one governance/confessional family inside the wider Reformed world.

Emphases

God's sovereignty, covenant theology in many branches, disciplined preaching and confessional/synodical life.

Variation

Reformed churches differ on worship, sacraments, polity and later liberal/evangelical developments.

Anglican traditions

1534 institutional rupture1559 settlementEngland → global communion
Continuity

Retains episcopacy and much liturgical continuity while undergoing Reformation-era doctrinal transformation.

Range

Includes catholic, evangelical, broad-church and charismatic currents.

Key texture

Book of Common Prayer tradition, episcopal polity and a historically wide internal theological range.

Anabaptist traditions

1525 Zürich marker1527 SchleitheimRadical Reformation
Baptism

Believer baptism becomes a defining boundary against infant-baptizing churches.

Discipleship

Community ethics, church discipline and separation from coercive state religion become major themes; many later branches emphasize nonviolence.

Family

Mennonite, Amish and related traditions develop through distinct histories.

Baptist traditions

1609 Amsterdam1612 LondonGeneral / Particular streams
Origins

Modern Baptist history is strongly rooted in English Separatist/Independent contexts; a simple “Baptists came from Anabaptists” line is too crude.

Emphases

Believer baptism, congregational church life, conversion and religious liberty are common recurring themes.

Diversity

Baptist traditions include Calvinistic and non-Calvinistic, evangelical, progressive and many national/convention structures.

“Sola scriptura” does not mean every Protestant branch reads scripture identically, and “Protestant” does not imply one shared sacramental or church-order system.
