Christianity · revival / holiness / restoration

MOVEMENTS THAT
CROSS THE OLD BRANCHES

Some later Christian movements are denominations; others are currents that flow through several denominations at once.

This is where a simple family tree becomes insufficient. Evangelical, holiness and charismatic identities often cross older denominational boundaries, while Methodist, Nazarene, Adventist and restorationist bodies form more specific institutions.

Methodist / Wesleyan traditions

1729 Holy Club → 1738 Aldersgate → 18th-century revival → later Methodist denominations

Methodism begins as an Anglican revival associated especially with John and Charles Wesley. Grace, disciplined discipleship, sanctification/holiness, preaching, hymnody and social concern become recurring emphases.

Religious Society of Friends / Quakers

1647 Fox ministry → 1652 major expansion → later meetings and branches

Friends emerge in the English dissenting/Seeker field with emphasis on immediate divine guidance, equality before God, distinctive silent/unprogrammed worship in many branches, truthfulness and peace testimony in important historical streams. Modern Friends range from explicitly evangelical Christian to liberal/universalist communities.

Evangelical current

18th-century revival field → 19th/20th-century missions and institutions → global cross-denominational identities

Evangelicalism is better treated as a movement/current than as one denomination. Common markers include conversion, biblical authority, the cross and active evangelism, but definitions and institutional expressions vary substantially.

Wesleyan-Holiness & Nazarene

19th-century holiness movement → multiple denominations → Nazarene union marker 1908

Holiness movements intensify Wesleyan sanctification language, often emphasizing entire sanctification/perfect love. The Church of the Nazarene is one major institutional heir, formed through unions of several holiness bodies.

Stone-Campbell / Restoration Movement

early 19th-century restoration currents → Stone/Campbell union 1831–32 → later distinct bodies

The movement seeks restoration of New Testament Christianity rather than merely another confessional denomination. Later trajectories include the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), Churches of Christ and Christian Churches/Churches of Christ.

Adventist traditions

1830s–1844 Millerite expectation → post-1844 reorganization → Seventh-day Adventist Church organized 1863

Adventism grows from intense Second-Coming expectation. Seventh-day Adventism develops distinctive seventh-day Sabbath, sanctuary, health, education and apocalyptic traditions, while “Adventist” remains historically broader than the SDA Church alone.

Pentecostal & charismatic Christianity

Holiness/revival background → Topeka 1901 → Azusa Street 1906 → global Pentecostal expansion → charismatic renewal across older churches

Pentecostal traditions emphasize the present work of the Holy Spirit, spiritual gifts, healing, mission and experiential worship. The later charismatic movement carries similar practices into Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox and Protestant settings, showing why not every religious current can be drawn as a clean daughter denomination.
