COMMUNITY,
SUCCESSION & LAW

Islamic history branches through succession, theology, legal schools, institutions and mystical currents. Sunni, Shi'i, Ibadi and Sufi are not four equivalent denominations.

The most important distinction is category. Sunni and Shi'i are broad historical traditions. The four Sunni madhhabs are legal schools. Twelver, Ismaili and Zaydi are Shi'i lineages. Ibadi Islam follows a separate early trajectory. Sufism crosses larger branch boundaries rather than sitting beside them as a third sect.

A sacred-origin clock, distinct from later institutional history.

A major community and institutional refoundation marker.

An important background to later Ibadi and other early dissenting trajectories.

Karbala and the death of Husayn

A central Shi'i martyrdom and memory marker.

765
Death of Ja'far al-Sadiq

A major succession differentiation point in later Imami/Ismaili histories.

874 / 941
Twelver occultation transitions

Minor and Greater Occultation become defining theological-institutional markers for Twelver Shi'ism.

1094
Nizari / Musta'li Ismaili split

A durable succession division within Ismaili Shi'ism.

Different structures inside Islam

Sunni IslamMajority historical tradition whose formative institutions and theology develop over centuries rather than at one founding date.broad historical tradition
Shi'i IslamTraditions centered on authority/succession through the Prophet's family, with several major imamate lineages.broad historical tradition
Ibadi IslamDistinct early Islamic tradition historically related to but not adequately reducible to the polemical category “Kharijite.”early Islamic communion
Hanafi / Maliki / Shafi'i / HanbaliSunni legal schools (madhhabs), not denominations. Muslims can share Sunni identity while following different legal schools.legal schools
Twelver Shi'ismLargest Shi'i lineage, structured around twelve Imams and the occultation of the twelfth Imam.Shi'i lineage
Ismaili Shi'ismImamate lineage with major later branches including Nizari and Musta'li traditions.Shi'i lineage
Zaydi Shi'ismShi'i lineage associated historically with Zayd ibn Ali and later communities especially in Yemen.Shi'i lineage
Sufism / tasawwufMystical and devotional currents crossing Sunni, Shi'i and other Muslim contexts; later tariqas/orders institutionalize from roughly the 12th–13th centuries onward.cross-cutting mystical current
Theology schoolsAsh'ari, Maturidi, Athari and other theological currents deserve separate treatment from jurisprudence and denomination labels.theological schools
