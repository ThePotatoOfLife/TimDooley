Comparative learning surface

Trees, Worlds, Wells & Ways

Learn Yggdrasil, Kabbalah, Christianity, Buddhism, Daoism and Potatoism in their own grammar—then compare the functions that really overlap.

Different traditions keep returning to a surprisingly small set of difficult questions: Where is the center? What connects different levels of reality? How is wisdom gained? Why does life become disordered? What is a threshold? What does descent mean? Can what is broken be repaired? The similarities are useful precisely when the differences remain visible.

Comparison rule: this page is a translation surface, not a claim that all religions secretly teach one doctrine. Historical influence must be evidenced separately. A modern analogy is labeled as a modern analogy.

Historical clocks

The traditions do not enter history at the same time—or in the same way

Some have no single founding date; some begin as communities before their canonical texts are written; some survive primarily in manuscripts copied centuries after the practices they describe. The timeline therefore keeps emergence, text, institution, rupture and later reception as separate clocks.

JudaismIsraelite/Judahite religious formation c. 1200–586 BCE; Second Temple period c. 516 BCE–70 CE; rabbinic textual consolidation continues into late antiquity; medieval Kabbalah crystallizes especially in the 12th–13th centuries.
ChristianityJesus movement in the 30s CE; earliest surviving Pauline writings in the 50s; most New Testament writings broadly c. 50–100 CE; councils and later branches belong to later clocks.
BuddhismHistorical Buddha / early sangha broadly 5th–4th centuries BCE; Aśokan patronage 3rd century BCE; Pali canon written in Sri Lanka in the 1st century BCE; Mahayana currents emerge across a broad later window.
DaoismClassical Daoist textual formation in the 4th–3rd centuries BCE; organized Celestial Masters movement from 142 CE; later Shangqing and Lingbao revelations/ritual systems develop in late antiquity.
Norse traditionsPre-Christian Germanic/Scandinavian practice field is much older than the surviving manuscripts; Viking Age c. 800–1050 CE is a key historical horizon; Prose Edda c. 1220–1222; Codex Regius of the Poetic Edda c. 1270.
PotatoismThe project currently retains 25 December 2020 as its declared symbolic seed date, with later 2022–2026 development tracked separately from that origin marker.

Old Norse cosmology

Yggdrasil is not scenery. It is infrastructure.

The Eddic world tree is a better model when it is understood as an inhabited, vulnerable and maintained ecology. Gods assemble near it. Wells sit at its roots. Norns shape lives near the Well of Urðr. Creatures move through it and damage it. Odin seeks wisdom through both the tree and the well. The structure joins differentiated realms without turning them into one undivided place.

Upper domainsAsgard and other divine/light-associated realms in later conventional orientation. Authority, divine society, vision and order.
Midgard · inhabited middleThe human world: embodied life, ordinary society, work, conflict, kinship and consequence.
Boundary worldsJötunheim, Vanaheim, Álfheim and craft-associated realms remind us that the Norse cosmos is differentiated by kinds of beings, powers and relations—not a simple heaven/earth/hell stack.
Deep / death / elemental domainsHel, Niflheim and Muspelheim belong to distinct death, cold/mist and fire complexes. They should not be flattened into one “hell.”

Odin's eye · Mímir's well

Odin gives or leaves an eye at Mímir's well in the wisdom complex preserved in Völuspá and Snorri's Gylfaginning. The structural lesson is costly seeing: knowledge changes the knower and may require surrender rather than mere collection.

Compare with Eye / knowledge only as a function. Do not turn every sacred eye into Odin.

Odin on the tree · Hávamál

In Hávamál, Odin hangs on a windswept tree for nine nights, wounded by a spear and offered to himself, before taking up the runes. This is an ordeal-of-knowledge motif: descent into extremity produces a new capacity to read and act.

Norns · Urðr, Verðandi, Skuld

Fate sits close to root and water. That matters: destiny is not only issued from a summit. The Eddic material places life-allotment within an ecology of source, memory, law and becoming.

Bifröst

The rainbow bridge is a route between differentiated domains, not a merger of them. As a modern systems analogy it resembles an interface: passage is possible because boundaries remain.

Ragnarök and renewal

Ragnarök is catastrophe, conflict and death, but the mythic horizon also includes survival and renewed world imagery. The relevant comparator is not “apocalypse equals apocalypse,” but breakdown → remainder → reconstitution.

Other Norse figures worth knowing

ThorStorm, protection, giant-conflict, embodied force and boundary defense. A useful protection/power comparator, not simply “Norse Zeus.”
FreyjaLove, desire, fertility, seiðr and a share in the slain. Her functions cross categories that modern summaries often separate.
FreyrFertility, prosperity, peace and sacred kingship motifs; especially useful when comparing Garden, yield and good-season symbolism.
HeimdallWatcher and boundary guardian associated with Bifröst. A strong threshold/attention figure.
TýrLaw, oath and the binding of Fenrir; useful for cost-bearing order and the price of constraint.
LokiBoundary-breaker, helper, adversary and catalyst across stories. Better understood as a difficult relational role than a simple devil-equivalent.
BaldrBeloved divine figure whose death becomes a major pre-Ragnarök rupture and later renewal motif.
FriggQueenly, familial and foreknowledge-associated figure; distinct from Freyja despite later confusions.
HelBoth ruler-name and death-realm complex in Norse sources. Hel is not equivalent to later Christian Hell.

About the “nine worlds”: Old Norse sources refer to nine worlds, but they do not hand us one universally agreed canonical nine-box list. Modern charts are useful orientation aids, not literal Eddic maps.

Modern symbolic reading of the Norse worlds

This is deliberately a present-day analogy layer, not a reconstruction of medieval belief.

Asgard · ordered authority

Institutions, ideals, governance, high-level coordination and the narratives a society places “above” ordinary life.

Midgard · lived world

Embodiment, work, family, politics, social relations, material constraint and ordinary consequence.

Jötunheim · disruptive outside

Wilderness, rival powers, boundary pressure, the not-fully-domesticated and whatever does not fit the center's order.

Vanaheim · fertility & exchange

Ecology, wealth, reproduction, peace-making and reciprocal relations.

Álfheim · luminous culture

Beauty, imagination, refinement and the difficult category of “light” as cultural/symbolic value.

Niðavellir / dwarf-world · making

Craft, infrastructure, engineering, hidden labor and objects whose power comes from skilled construction.

Niflheim · cold inertia

Stagnation, obscurity, low-energy states, storage, fog and conditions in which movement is difficult.

Muspelheim · heat / rupture

Energy, combustion, conflict, irreversible transformation and systems pushed beyond containment.

Hel · death / memory

Loss, ancestry, irreversibility, what remains after active participation ends, and the social work of remembering the dead.

Jewish scripture & mysticism

Tree, ladder, throne, sefirot, shells and repair

For the wider historical lineages—Second Temple, rabbinic, Karaite, Hasidic and modern Jewish movements—see Jewish history & movements →

Jewish material contributes several different structures that should not be collapsed into one diagram. Genesis gives the Trees of Life and Knowledge. Jacob dreams a ladder or stairway joining earth and heaven. Ezekiel supplies throne/chariot vision. Later Jewish mysticism develops heavenly palaces, sefirot, multiple worlds, angelologies and—especially in later Kabbalah—kelippot/qelipot, “shells” or “husks” associated with concealment, impurity and captured vitality.

Biblical layer

Tree of Life, Tree of Knowledge, Eden, cherubim, Jacob's ladder, prophetic visions and angels as messengers. There is no tenfold Qliphoth demon-tree in Genesis.

Kabbalistic layer

Ein Sof and the ten sefirot provide a language of hidden infinity and differentiated divine manifestation. The familiar Tree diagram belongs to a long development rather than one timeless biblical blueprint.

Lurianic repair layer

Tzimtzum, vessels, breaking, sparks, kelippot and tikkun create a powerful repair drama: vitality can become trapped in broken structures and religious action participates in restoration.

Later Hermetic / occult layer

The neat tenfold “Qliphoth tree” with fixed shell names, demon rulers, tunnels and one-to-one adverse correspondences is especially a later Western esoteric system. It should be studied, but not backdated into the Torah or treated as the single Jewish position.

Angels and demons without the internet-chart shortcut

Jewish angels begin as messengers and agents in biblical texts; rabbinic and mystical traditions elaborate many ranks and names. Likewise, demonological material develops across periods. The useful structure is not “angel tree versus demon tree” but mediation, concealment, action, consequence and repair. Different Jewish sources organize those categories differently.

SefirotKeter, Chokhmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod and Malkhut: a tenfold vocabulary of divine manifestation whose meanings and diagrammatic arrangements vary by school and period.
Da'at“Knowledge” sometimes appears as an additional/hidden position in diagrams without becoming an ordinary eleventh sefirah. Useful as a knowledge-junction comparison.
Kelippot / QelipotShell/husk language for concealment, impurity or captured vitality in mystical traditions. Lurianic versions connect the problem to broken vessels and trapped sparks.
TikkunRepair/restoration. This is one of the strongest cross-project comparisons because repair is an action and process, not merely a cosmic label.
Mal'akh / angels“Messenger” is the basic biblical function; later Jewish thought develops much richer hierarchies and metaphysics.
Four worldsAtzilut, Beriah, Yetzirah and Assiyah provide a later mystical structure for degrees or modes of divine manifestation and creation.

Christianity

Tree, Cross, Door, Way, Vine, resurrection and New Jerusalem

For the internal Christian branching—ancient communions, Catholic/Orthodox trajectories, Reformation families, revivals and denominations—see the Christianity branch map →

This lens remains deliberately compact. Open the Bible comparison atlas for the source-text side →

Christianity inherits Jewish scripture but rereads it through Jesus, the cross and resurrection. That creates several powerful comparison points without making Christianity “the same system” as Kabbalah or Norse religion: Christ as Door and Way; the cross as death-to-life axis and, in later Christian symbolism, a life-giving tree; vine/branches as relational life; resurrection as transformed return; and Revelation's New Jerusalem with the Tree of Life restored.

Door / WayPassage is relational and ethical, not simply spatial. This is the closest Christian neighbor to the project's Door vocabulary.
Cross / TreeA death instrument becomes, in Christian interpretation and later imagery, a sign of life through self-giving death and resurrection.
Vine / branchesConnected life, fruit and dependence become an organic social-theological model rather than a world map.
KingdomGod's reign/order is both present and anticipated; it is not merely another celestial “realm” on a cosmographic chart.
ResurrectionReturn is not just recurrence. Christian resurrection claims transformed life after death and anchors the new-creation horizon.
New JerusalemCity, throne, river and Tree of Life are reunited in Revelation's closing vision of restored creation.

Buddhist traditions

Not a creator-tree system: awakening, dependence, realms and release

For Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana, Chan/Zen, Pure Land and Tibetan transmission history, see Buddhist transmission map →

Buddhism is especially valuable because it resists forcing every cosmology into a Father/Creator template. The Bodhi tree marks awakening, Mount Meru organizes cosmography in many Buddhist traditions, samsaric realms model conditioned existence, dependent origination explains arising through relation, and emptiness denies that things possess isolated permanent self-nature. Tibetan bardo traditions add a sophisticated transition-state model, but bardo should not be projected onto all Buddhism.

Bodhi treeTree as place of awakening rather than cosmic trunk or divine genealogy.
Mount MeruWorld-center/cosmographic organization. Useful beside Axis and sacred-mountain models, but not itself a salvation ladder.
Dependent originationThings arise through conditions. This strongly challenges isolated-node thinking and encourages relation/process models.
Śūnyatā / emptinessEspecially in Mahāyāna thought, emptiness concerns lack of independent inherent existence—not a literal empty void.
Samsara / realmsConditioned cycles and realms are tied to karma, perception and suffering, not merely places on a fantasy map.
Nirvana / awakeningRelease is not “moving to the top of the tree.” It is liberation from the conditions that sustain suffering and delusion.

Daoist traditions

The Way may be a better comparator than the Tree

For Celestial Masters, Shangqing, Lingbao, Quanzhen and Zhengyi history beyond the Daodejing lens, see Daoist textual & religious lineages →

Daoism helps the project avoid making every tradition vertical. The Dao is Way/process/source language rather than a throne at the top of a hierarchy. The Daodejing repeatedly values water, valley, return, simplicity, softness, non-forcing and the usefulness of empty space. A hub works because of its empty center; a vessel works because it can contain; a room works because doors and windows open it.

Dao · WaySource/process that exceeds final naming. Useful beside “Source,” but not a personal Father-equivalent.
Wu weiNon-forcing or action without coercive excess; not simple passivity.
WaterYielding, low-seeking, adaptive and persistent. An unusually strong flow metaphor.
ValleyLow place as generative receptivity, reversing the assumption that spiritual importance always means height.
Vessel / roomBoundaries become useful through the opening or capacity they preserve. This directly sharpens House and Door thinking.
ReturnReturn/reversal is a recurring Daoist movement: growth does not simply mean permanent outward expansion.

Project-native layer

Potatoism stays nearby as the system doing the comparing

Potatoism should not absorb the traditions above. It can instead declare its own operators clearly—Father/Source, Son/Door, Spirit/flow, Tree, Axis, Ladder, House, Seed, Garden, Knowledge, Strife, repair—and ask what each external system reveals or challenges.

Father / Source / HouseProject language for source-facing containment, orientation and generativity.
Son / Door / VesselThreshold, manifestation and passage language inside the project's own theology.
Spirit / flowMovement, relation and transmission rather than another static object.
Tree / Axis / LadderLiving connectivity, orientation and movement between differentiated levels.
Knowledge → Life / StrifeKnowledge is treated as a fork: what is learned can become generative, or remain unresolved and reproduce capture/conflict.
Seed / Garden / repairBurial, dormancy, rooting, growth, cultivation, fruit and restorative work form the project's generative cycle.

These are Potatoverse meanings. Similarities with Odin, Kabbalah, Christianity, Buddhism or Daoism are comparisons, not external validation of Potatoist theological claims.

The central lesson: similar symbols often solve different problems. Yggdrasil organizes a mythic ecology. The sefirotic Tree models divine manifestation. The Christian Cross/Tree centers death and resurrection. The Bodhi tree marks awakening. Daoism may prefer Way, water and empty capacity to any tree at all. Potatoism can learn from all of them without erasing them.
