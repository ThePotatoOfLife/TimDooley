Buddhism · transmission map

PATHS OF
TRANSMISSION

Buddhist history is better understood as networks of monastic, textual and regional transmission than as one clean founder-to-denomination tree.

Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana are useful large categories, but they do not function exactly like Christian denominations. Chan/Zen, Pure Land and Tibetan traditions grow through translation, monastic institutions, ritual, philosophy and regional transmission.

Major families

What kind of branch is it?

Theravada

Living monastic/textual traditions rooted especially in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia; Pali canon and monastic lineage are central.

Mahayana

Large family of texts, ideals and practices, including bodhisattva traditions and many East Asian schools; not one centralized institution.

Vajrayana

Tantric/esoteric Buddhist methods, texts and initiatory traditions found especially in Tibetan, Himalayan and some East Asian settings.

Chan / Zen / Seon / Thiền

Related but regionally distinct meditation and monastic traditions with Chinese Chan as a major historical source field.

Pure Land

Devotional/textual family centered on Amitabha/Amitayus and rebirth in a Pure Land; appears across East Asia with later specific Japanese schools.

Tibetan schools

Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug, Jonang and others need their own transmission histories rather than being collapsed into “Vajrayana.”

Buddhist “schools” can mean philosophical lineages, monastic transmission, ritual systems, regional institutions or devotional networks. The site should show the type rather than reuse “denomination” for everything.
