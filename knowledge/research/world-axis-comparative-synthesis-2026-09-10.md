# World Axis Comparative Synthesis

## Purpose

This note compares recovered Tim/Potatoverse North-West-East-South material with current observable geopolitical, institutional and geographic structures. It is not a claim that the project quadrants are official geopolitical blocs. The aim is to identify where the project model has strong relational structure, where it overlaps observable systems, where it contradicts them, and how the Atlas should represent uncertainty.

## Core finding

The four fields work best as **relationship fields**, not exclusive country buckets.

A country may simultaneously be:

- project-North through a recovered Tim formulation,
- project-West through another relational axis,
- project-South through a maritime/geographic role,
- and empirically BRICS, NATO, EU, ASEAN or Pacific-connected.

The Atlas should therefore model field assignment as a typed claim with provenance, status and confidence rather than as a single permanent color.

## North

North is the strongest and most recoverable project field.

The clearest 2026 formulation is the recovered `Canada -> Turkey minus England` arc, with Canada, Greenland, Iceland, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Germany, Netherlands, Belgium, France, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Scotland, Ireland, Ukraine and Türkiye explicitly named. England was pending, Russia excluded, Australia shared, and Mesopotamian/Iraq-Syria links contemplated later. This history is preserved in `data/north-axis-membership-history.json`.

Observable structures partly corroborate the relational coherence without defining it. NATO currently has 32 members and connects Canada, the Nordic states, much of Europe, the UK and Türkiye. The EU has 27 members and provides an even denser institutional/economic layer across continental Europe and Ireland. Ukraine remains an EU candidate rather than a member; accession negotiations are active and additional negotiating clusters opened in June and July 2026.

The mismatches are analytically important:

- Canada is NATO but not EU.
- Ireland is EU but not NATO.
- Türkiye is NATO but not EU.
- Greenland and the Faroe Islands have distinct constitutional and European institutional relationships.
- Ukraine is a candidate/accession state rather than a current EU member.
- Russia is geographically Arctic/European but explicitly excluded from the current recovered North formulation.

**Insight:** North is best understood as a project-defined North Atlantic / European / Arctic / strategic corridor whose observable strength comes from several overlapping institutions and functional chains, not a disguised NATO or EU map.

## West

Recovered material makes the United States and Israel the clearest project-West poles. Wider material places most of the Americas in a Western field unless specifically pulled toward East/North, and gives the UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and South Korea strong Western relationships.

Observable alliances support some of that structure but also expose its limits. Australia, the UK and United States are bound through AUKUS; Australia and the United States also retain a longstanding security alliance. Canada, the UK, Australia and New Zealand are deeply tied to the US-centered security architecture. None of this means those countries cannot also occupy North or South project fields.

South America is especially useful as a test. MERCOSUR links Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay and Bolivia in the same integration framework. Yet the recovered project model places Brazil closer to East/BRICS while treating much of the rest of South America as West. That is not an error if the Atlas stores **different relationship types** instead of trying to make institutional integration and project-axis identity identical.

**Insight:** West is not simply “NATO” or “the Americas.” It is a U.S.-centered Atlantic/security/cultural field with important cross-axis states and strong internal exceptions.

## East and BRICS

The strongest recovered East anchors are China, Russia and India, with Pakistan, Vietnam and parts of mainland Southeast Asia also repeatedly placed East. Iran, Brazil, South Africa, Egypt and Ethiopia appear in East through later BRICS-adjacent project language.

Official BRICS information materially sharpens the model. BRICS currently has eleven members: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, United Arab Emirates, Ethiopia, Indonesia and Iran. It also has partner countries including Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan and Vietnam.

The official BRICS description is crucial: BRICS presents itself as a political and diplomatic coordination forum for countries of the **Global South**, with cooperation spanning politics/security, economy/finance, and people-to-people relations. It is not an official “East” alliance.

Therefore:

- Saudi Arabia can be empirically BRICS while remaining project-West/Center-linked.
- Indonesia can be BRICS while remaining project-South/East overlapping.
- South Africa can be BRICS/East and geographically/project-South.
- Brazil can be BRICS/East while maintaining major Western-hemisphere and MERCOSUR relations.

**Insight:** BRICS should visually strengthen the East layer without ever becoming the definition of East. The Atlas should show BRICS as an empirical red backbone over which project-East is a separate interpretive field.

## South

South remains the least settled project field and should be treated accordingly.

Recovered material points most strongly to Australia, New Zealand, maritime Southeast Asia, the Pacific, the Indian Ocean and parts of southern Africa. Papua New Guinea is a plausible current candidate but lacks a recovered direct Tim statement and should remain provisional.

Observable regional structures provide useful corroboration. New Zealand's foreign ministry describes the Pacific Islands Forum as the Pacific region's premier political and economic policy organisation, with 18 members. New Zealand and Papua New Guinea are both PIF members, and New Zealand explicitly describes Papua New Guinea as a country connecting the Pacific and Southeast Asia. This strongly supports treating PNG as a **bridge node** in an emerging South field, but it does not prove a Timic South assignment.

The African Union provides another useful correction. The AU has 55 member states and formally divides them into Central, Eastern, Northern, Southern and Western African regions. This means “Africa = South” is analytically weak. Southern Africa is a real geographic regional layer; Africa as a whole is not.

**Insight:** South should initially be rendered as a maritime / Pacific / Indian-Ocean / southern-African relational field with visibly lower confidence outside its recovered core. It should grow by recovered statements and observable functional ties, not by filling blank space.

## Southeast Asia as a bridge zone

ASEAN now has eleven members after Timor-Leste joined on 26 October 2025. The region contains countries assigned East, South or both in recovered project material.

This is not a contradiction to eliminate. It is evidence that East and South should behave as overlapping gradients. Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam are particularly useful bridge nodes because their project assignments, ASEAN membership and BRICS participation/partnership create multiple simultaneous edges.

## Middle East / Center

Recovered material repeatedly treats the Middle East as a meeting arena rather than a clean quadrant. Iraq/Mesopotamia has North/Father/root relevance; Israel is West/covenant-linked; Iran is East/BRICS-linked; Saudi Arabia carries West, Islam, oil and Center overlays; Türkiye is a North strategic endpoint while also bridging Europe, the Black Sea and the Middle East.

**Insight:** Do not force a single Middle-East color. Preserve a Center/hinge concept and allow countries to inherit multiple axis relations.

## Rendering implications

The map should distinguish at least five states of project assignment:

1. **Primary / recovered** — direct or repeatedly recovered Tim assignment.
2. **Overlap** — clearly cross-axis or shared.
3. **Hinge** — boundary case whose relationships point in multiple directions.
4. **Provisional** — current hypothesis or newly suggested assignment without recovered source.
5. **Future / reconnection** — explicitly not current membership but retained as a contemplated future relation.

Primary states should render more strongly; overlap/hinge more softly; provisional and future states should be faint and clearly labeled on hover.

## Immediate actions adopted

- Keep North, West, East and South as project-interpretive fields.
- Keep BRICS as a separate empirical layer.
- Add field confidence/provenance vocabulary.
- Render primary assignments more strongly than provisional/future assignments.
- Preserve Russia as East plus North-future/reconnection, not current North.
- Preserve Papua New Guinea as provisional South.
- Preserve South Africa as East/BRICS plus South overlap.
- Preserve Australia as a North-shared, West-connected and South node.
- Preserve Brazil as an East/BRICS and West-hinge node.
- Treat Southeast Asia as an East-South bridge zone.
- Treat the Middle East as a multi-axis center/hinge arena rather than forcing a quadrant.
- Avoid assigning all of Africa to South.

## Research priorities

1. Recover exact dated Tim statements for Papua New Guinea and wider Pacific South.
2. Recover direct country-by-country South assignments before expanding South further across Africa.
3. Recover whether “South America is West” was a literal country list or a regional heuristic.
4. Recover exact cross-axis statements for Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, Brazil, Bolivia and South Africa.
5. Recover England/Scotland/UK transitions with exact dates and wording.
6. Keep all future taxonomy changes versioned rather than rewriting earlier snapshots.
