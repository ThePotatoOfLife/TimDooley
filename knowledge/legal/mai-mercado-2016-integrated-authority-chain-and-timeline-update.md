# Mai Mercado / Christiania 2016 — Integrated Authority-Chain and Timeline Update

## Status

This file is the **cross-system integration update** for the entire Mercado subsystem after the newly clarified police-door / no-contact timeline and the retrospective legal research on tilhold, §123, evidence preservation, and the prosecution objectivity principle.

It should be read together with the canonical casefile, full timeline, false-flag audit, remand/evidence-access audit, referral-chain audit, second-trial dossier, and retrospective-defense file.

The purpose is to prevent those records from continuing to treat the later `consequences / not a threat, but a guarantee` statement as a detached second threat. The correct reconstruction now includes the state-intervention sequence that preceded it.

---

# 1. Updated master causal chain

The central timeline is now:

1. **17 June 2016 — Mercado publicly posts** celebratory, deliberately forceful language about police `gennemsmadrer` hash stalls at Christiania, while foregrounding her newborn.
2. **17–20 June — multi-speaker Facebook conflict develops.** Many commenters participate; hostile and violent statements originate from more than one speaker.
3. **Institutional escalation begins.** Public reporting preserves at least two referral pathways: another Facebook user reportedly contacts police, while courtroom reporting says Folketingets sikkerhedsafdeling reported threats after contact from Mercado.
4. **Police attend the Son’s private home.** The Son’s firsthand account says officers tell him not to contact Mercado again.
5. **Legal status of that instruction remains unresolved.** It may have been informal advice/admonition, a police warning, or a formal tilhold. The project has not yet recovered a written tilhold decision or service record.
6. **The Son rejects the asymmetry.** From his perspective, Mercado had initiated a public political confrontation, possessed institutional power, and remained publicly able to speak while police now physically appeared at his home and attempted to terminate his side of the interaction.
7. **The Son sends a further message.** The core reported wording is: `Politiet er lige kommet og har sigtet mig. Hvis det får konsekvenser for mig, får det også konsekvenser for dig - det er ikke en trussel, men en garanti.` Longer judgment-index snippets appear to include additional combative language and references to starting/finishing a fight.
8. **That response becomes new criminal material.** It is later treated within the witness-intimidation / retaliation theory.
9. **The Son is arrested/remanded; devices are seized.** He loses ordinary control over his phone, computer, public communications, and practical ability to independently preserve a changing Facebook thread.
10. **The case proceeds while the public/media narrative continues outside his control.** Mercado retains access to media and institutional channels.
11. **12 August 2016 — conviction on three of four counts; five months’ unconditional imprisonment.** One count fails; exact reason remains a major missing item.

The second criminal-theory node therefore must be understood as:

**public political conflict → institutional referral → state intervention at private home → no-contact instruction → defiant reciprocal reply → witness-intimidation charge.**

That sequence is materially different from:

**first threat → police warn him → he independently threatens again.**

# 2. The tilhold question must now be decomposed into five separate propositions

The project must stop using `police order`, `warning`, `tilhold`, and `do not contact` interchangeably.

## Proposition A — Police attended the Son’s home

Status: contemporaneous reporting establishes police attendance before the remand hearing; the Son’s firsthand account provides the no-contact instruction detail.

## Proposition B — Officers told him not to contact Mercado

Status: high-weight firsthand claim; still requires police report / officer notes for independent corroboration.

## Proposition C — A formal tilhold existed

Status: **not yet established**.

Under the law in force in 2016, an ordinary tilhold could be issued administratively by the police director. A court order was not inherently required. But statutory conditions still applied.

## Proposition D — Any tilhold was lawfully issued

Status: cannot be assessed without the decision and grounds.

A valid order required statutory authority, relevant factual grounds, a prediction of continued conduct, proportionality, competent decision-making, and formal service/notification.

## Proposition E — The Son violated a binding legal order

Status: **not established merely from the fact that officers verbally told him not to make further contact**.

The project should therefore flag any later phrase such as `he disobeyed a police order` or `he violated a restraining order` unless a formal order is recovered.

# 3. Legal authority did not come from Mercado personally

Mercado, as an MP, had no personal statutory authority to issue a tilhold or command local police.

The formal authority, if a valid tilhold existed, came from Danish law and the competent police authority.

This does **not** end the power-abuse inquiry.

It changes the question from:

> `Did Mercado legally order the police?`

into:

> **What information, request, pressure, referral, or institutional signal traveled from Mercado to Folketing security and then to local police, and how independently did police assess it?**

This is the correct authority-chain question.

# 4. Strong institutional-pressure hypothesis

The project may now formulate the following hypothesis:

> **Mercado’s political position gave her access to a parliamentary-security channel unavailable to an ordinary citizen; that channel may have accelerated or intensified police attention toward the Son, after which the resulting police intervention generated the later communication used as additional criminal evidence.**

This hypothesis has three levels.

### Directly supported

- Mercado was a sitting MP.
- Courtroom reporting says Folketing security made a police report after contact from Mercado.
- Police then attended the Son’s home.
- The Son’s later message explicitly begins with the police having just attended/charged him.

### Strong circumstantial inference

- Mercado’s institutional status plausibly made a security referral more authoritative or urgent than an ordinary Facebook complaint.
- The police-door intervention became a causal antecedent to the later consequences message.

### Not yet established

- that Mercado ordered police to act;
- that Folketing security directed local police rather than simply referred information;
- that police were politically pressured to issue a tilhold;
- that any tilhold was unlawful;
- that police knowingly bypassed statutory criteria;
- that Mercado deliberately designed the sequence to generate new criminal evidence.

These stronger allegations require referral logs, emails, police notes, decision records, or testimony.

# 5. Why the police-door event matters even if lawful

Even a lawful police visit can be analytically important.

The state intervention changed the conflict from a public Facebook argument into an asymmetrical coercive situation:

- Mercado remained a public official with media and institutional access;
- the Son was confronted at his private home by police;
- officers reportedly told him to cease contact;
- the Son understood this as one-sided silencing;
- he reacted against that perceived asymmetry;
- the reaction then became new evidence against him.

Thus the legal system did not merely **respond to** the original communication. It became part of the causal environment producing the second communication.

This is the **state-intervention feedback loop**.

That loop is one of the strongest retrospective defense insights in the project.

# 6. If a formal tilhold existed, legality must still be tested

A formal tilhold would not automatically validate every later inference.

The project must ask:

1. Who made the decision?
2. On what date and time?
3. Under which statutory provision?
4. What conduct was relied upon?
5. Which statements were attributed to the Son at that moment?
6. Were any of those statements actually authored by other Facebook users?
7. Was the complete thread available to police?
8. Did police distinguish between the Son and other hostile commenters?
9. What evidence supported the prediction that he would continue the relevant conduct?
10. Was proportionality considered?
11. Was the decision formally served?
12. Was he informed of legal consequences?
13. Was a breach of tilhold separately charged?
14. Did the eventual court judgment rely on the existence of a formal order or merely the police warning?

If any formal contact restriction was based on contaminated attribution or incomplete thread evidence, that could become a foundational problem because the intervention itself then helped generate the later count.

# 7. New bootstrapping / self-generating-case hypothesis

The timeline supports a new retrospective model:

> **An initial contested speech case may have bootstrapped itself into a stronger criminal case through state intervention.**

Sequence:

**disputed Facebook speech → police intervention → perceived coercion → defiant reply → new witness-intimidation allegation → stronger remand/prosecution narrative.**

This does not prove entrapment in the formal legal sense, and the project should not use that legal term without meeting its requirements.

But it does show a **self-reinforcing evidentiary loop**: intervention prompted speech; that speech became evidence justifying further intervention.

This is especially important if the original attribution/context case was already unstable.

# 8. Connection to §123: the police-door sequence may expose a legal-nexus problem

The previously discovered 2015 Supreme Court precedent makes the timeline more consequential.

§123 was tied to retaliation connected with a person’s expected or already-given statement to police/court. It did not automatically cover every person who merely supplied evidence.

Therefore the project must determine, at the moment of the consequences message:

- Had Mercado already given a statement to police?
- Was she formally expected to testify?
- Had police told the Son anything about her witness role?
- Did the police-door instruction concern protection of a witness or simply cessation of contact?
- Was the later message directed at her because of expected/given testimony, or because police had just come to his home?

If the actual motive/nexus was **reaction to police intervention** rather than retaliation for witness testimony, that distinction may matter to the §123 analysis.

This does not itself defeat the conviction; it identifies a specific statutory element requiring primary-source verification.

Source:
- Højesteret 25 February 2015, U.2015.1802H / 158/2014: https://openlaw.dk/sag/hojesteret/158-2014-f74ed99f

# 9. The objectivity principle becomes even more important before any contact restriction

If police considered restricting the Son’s communication, they had an especially strong reason to determine **who had actually written what**.

The Facebook environment was multi-speaker. Some of the most violent comments are not currently proven to be the Son’s. Other defendants later emerged.

Under the prosecution objectivity principle, investigators were not supposed to investigate only material pointing toward guilt.

Thus a major new question is:

> **Before police told or ordered the Son not to contact Mercado, had they already preserved and distinguished the complete multi-speaker evidence?**

If yes, the state may have had a much stronger basis than the public record currently shows.

If no, the intervention may have been based on a flattened or contaminated threat picture.

This question is foundational because the intervention itself appears to have generated the next disputed message.

# 14. What would demonstrate unlawful or politically pressured police action

The project should actively search for any of the following:

- message from Mercado asking security/police to stop the Son contacting her;
- security instruction to local police going beyond a neutral referral;
- request to treat Mercado differently because of political office;
- tilhold issued without statutory factual findings;
- tilhold based on comments actually authored by others;
- absence of required service while later treating the Son as having breached an order;
- internal urgency/escalation note tied specifically to Mercado’s political status rather than objective risk;
- evidence that police knew the thread attribution was uncertain but acted as though it were settled;
- evidence that exculpatory context was available but deliberately omitted;
- instruction from political staff, ministry, party organization, or parliamentary administration that improperly dictated investigative outcome.

Any such artifact would materially upgrade the power-abuse hypothesis.

# 15. What would weaken the power-abuse hypothesis

The project should also preserve falsifiers:

- a contemporaneous police report independently documenting legally sufficient threatening conduct before any Mercado/security intervention;
- a properly reasoned formal tilhold based only on verified Son-authored material;
- evidence that police independently assessed the full Facebook thread;
- a valid witness statement from Mercado before the §123 communication;
- complete preservation of exculpatory material;
- evidence that the same intervention would routinely have occurred for a non-politician under comparable circumstances.

These records would weaken the hypothesis of political pressure even if they would not resolve media-framing issues.

# 16. Updated strongest conclusion

The new police-door timeline materially improves the project’s understanding of the case.

The later `consequences / not a threat, but a guarantee` statement should no longer be treated as an isolated second threat. It appears, on the Son’s firsthand timeline and the reported wording itself, to have been a **direct reaction to police intervention at his private home**.

The state therefore became part of the causal sequence producing the statement later used against him.

The project can now responsibly conclude that:

> **Mercado was not merely downstream of the police response. Courtroom reporting places her upstream of a parliamentary-security referral; police then intervened locally; the Son reacted to that intervention; and the reaction became additional criminal evidence. This creates a real institutional feedback loop and a serious retrospective question about whether the state response was independently grounded, proportionate, and based on correctly attributed evidence.**

The project cannot yet conclude as established fact that:

> **Mercado ordered or politically pressured police to act unlawfully, or that a formal tilhold was illegal.**

Those stronger findings now have very specific documentary tests.

The most important next records are no longer abstract: **the police attendance report, any tilhold decision and service record, Mercado’s police statement, Folketing-security referral records, and the local-police intake/decision chain.**

Those documents could determine whether the door-contact episode was ordinary lawful policing, unusually accelerated institutional protection, or an improperly pressured intervention that then helped generate the later criminal count.
