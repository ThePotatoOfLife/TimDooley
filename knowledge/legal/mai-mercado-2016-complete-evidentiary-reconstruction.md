# Mai Mercado / Christiania 2016 — Complete Evidentiary Reconstruction

## Scope and evidentiary rule

This file reconstructs the 2016 Mai Mercado / Christiania Facebook case involving the **Son**, using the surviving contemporaneous press, later official Danish legal and parliamentary material, the project's firsthand account, the Great Book of Potato, and later retrospective sources. It is written as a reopening/defense-investigation dossier rather than as advocacy for a predetermined conclusion.

**Identity rule:** the 2016 defendant is the **Son**. Tim/Father is the later theological/root identity. Older project texts that call the 2016 actor “Tim” are historical strata and should be read with this correction.

**Evidence classes used below:**

- **ESTABLISHED / OFFICIAL** — statute, official Folketing material, formal public institutional record.
- **CONTEMPORARY COURT-REPORTED** — journalist reporting from the hearing, close in time to the event.
- **CONTEMPORARY MEDIA-ATTRIBUTED** — wire/news report, often one step removed from the original evidence.
- **FIRSTHAND PROJECT ACCOUNT** — the Son's later recollection preserved by the project.
- **STRONG INFERENCE** — supported by several facts but not directly proven.
- **UNRESOLVED** — decisive primary evidence is missing.
- **CONTRADICTED / CONTAMINATED SOURCE** — a later source demonstrably collapses or misstates earlier source structure.

The central rule is: **do not resolve uncertainty by choosing the prosecution/media narrative or the later autobiographical narrative wholesale. Resolve each proposition separately.**

# I. The event before it became a criminal case

On **17 June 2016**, police carried out a large operation against Pusher Street at Christiania. Contemporary reporting says roughly 30–37 stalls were physically removed/demolished and multiple people were arrested. Police publicly framed the operation as an effort against organized criminal control and open hash sales.

Mai Mercado, then a sitting Conservative member of the Folketing, reacted publicly on Facebook with the now repeatedly preserved wording:

> “Findes der noget bedre end barsel med en nyfødt? Det skulle da lige være TV 2 NEWS kørende i baggrunden, hvor politiet gennemsmadrer hashboder på Christiania! I love it.”

This is the **initiating speech event**. The dispute therefore did not begin as an unsolicited private communication to Mercado. It began with an elected politician making intentionally vivid, triumphal public speech about a coercive police operation affecting a politically and culturally contested community.

This point is not a legal defense by itself. Later official Danish material specifically records that the court regarded Mercado's post as a **lawful statement in public debate**, and under Penal Code §81(7) the fact that the threats arose in response to that lawful public speech was used as an **aggravating sentencing factor**. The law therefore treated the same factual relationship — response to Mercado's political statement — almost opposite to how the Son later understood it morally.

## Mercado's immediate rhetorical posture

The backlash was large and ugly. Contemporary material preserves insults and several plainly violent or gruesome messages from **multiple participants**. A later DenOffentlige article quotes Mercado's follow-up in which she said she could have written “fjerner” rather than “gennemsmadrer” but **stood by her word choice** because she believed “smashing” accurately described what she saw. In the same follow-up she summarized the event more neutrally as police having “removed 37 hash stalls,” then listed extreme reactions including an acid-bath statement and AIDS/death language.

This produces a documented rhetorical shift:

**initial:** “police are smashing the stalls — I love it”  
**later summary:** “I commented on police removing 37 stalls”

That is evidence of **self-reframing or sanitization**, not sufficient evidence of a lie. It matters because the emotional character of the initiating act becomes less visible as the victim-of-threats narrative forms.

# II. The Facebook object was a tree, not a transcript

This is probably the most important evidentiary concept in the entire case.

The Facebook discussion involved **many speakers, replies, nested replies, blocks, deletions and later multiple criminal defendants**. A flat list of quotations cannot reliably preserve:

1. who authored each statement;
2. who the statement was addressed to;
3. what parent comment it answered;
4. whether the speaker was warning, endorsing, mocking, rebuking, or threatening;
5. whether another person's words were being paraphrased or quoted;
6. timeline between comments;
7. edits/deletions.

The Son's firsthand account says a central “death wish” remark was effectively a response to another participant who had posted grotesque violence toward Mercado/her child. He recalls his meaning as approximately: **“Do you have a death wish? If so, just keep talking.”** He disputes that he authored the acid-bath statement and disputes several later attributions.

The surviving press proves the acid-bath and AIDS/death language existed somewhere in the broader reaction. It does **not**, by itself, preserve the account-level provenance.

This became more important when a concrete media contamination was found.

# III. A documented media-attribution contamination

A surviving copy of a **21 June 2016 Copenhagen Post** article says Mercado had blocked the man, making his original comments no longer visible, and that Mercado then **shared a selection of things the man had posted**. The article directly attributes the following broader-thread language to him:

- “Burn in hell” / acid-bath language;
- AIDS/death language concerning her child.

Yet the contemporaneous Danish material presenting Mercado's follow-up lists those same statements as examples from the **broader backlash**, without establishing in that text that they came from this particular defendant.

That does not prove the Son did not write them. But it proves something narrower and extremely important:

> **At least one contemporary media source collapsed broader-thread material into defendant-specific attribution without preserving the primary Facebook evidence.**

The article itself explains a possible mechanism: Mercado blocked the account, the original comments became unavailable to ordinary readers, and the article relied on a **selection Mercado shared**.

This is no longer merely a theoretical “speaker contamination” risk. The public media ecosystem contains an actual example where speaker-level provenance is weaker than the confident attribution suggests.

# IV. The Son's position at the first hearing

On **21 June 2016 at 11:30**, the Son was presented at a remand hearing in Retten i Sønderborg. SønderborgNYT reported directly from the proceeding.

The report states:

- he was described as 29;
- he **denied guilt**;
- he appealed the remand decision;
- he acknowledged writing approximately **15 contributions/posts** on Mercado's open Facebook profile during the weekend;
- individual posts were read aloud;
- the report reproduced an ellipsis-linked chain including “vil du gerne dø?”, a knife/child fragment, losing-child language and Jo Cox language;
- he said he felt provoked by Mercado's Facebook post;
- he said he used cannabis because his ADHD medication did not work;
- he said he wanted a dialogue so Mercado would understand why people with ADHD, sclerosis and cancer used cannabis medically;
- critically, he said **it was not his intention to seek Mercado out and carry out death threats**.

This is one of the strongest pieces of corroboration for the later project account. The “I did not regard this as a threat / I was arguing, warning or mirroring” posture existed **at the first remand hearing**, not only years later.

At the same time, acknowledgment of roughly 15 posts creates a real constraint on the later memory that there was only one central incriminating remark. The two accounts are not necessarily incompatible: 15 total contributions do not equal 15 threats. But the precise map must be recovered.

# V. The statements: sentence-by-sentence legal and linguistic map

## A. “Vil du gerne dø?”

Literal sense: “Do you want / would you like to die?”

Possible pragmatic functions:
- threat;
- warning;
- rhetorical rebuke;
- Danish equivalent of “do you have a death wish?” / “are you crazy?”

The earliest court report gives it inside ellipses and does not preserve the parent comment or addressee. The Son says it was directed at another violent commenter. **This cannot currently be resolved from the press record.**

## B. “Du leger med dit og dit barns liv”

“You are playing with your life and your child's life.”

No explicit future violent agent appears grammatically. It can nevertheless function as a threat in context. Official Folketing reports identify this as one of the statements in the August judgment.

## C. “Jeg kan godt forstå, hvis nogen vil stikke en kniv i dig og din baby”

“I can understand if someone would want to stab you and your baby.”

The grammatical violent agent is **“someone”**, not explicitly the speaker. That distinction matters linguistically, although indirect/third-party phrasing can still be threatening. Official later reports also identify this sentence as part of the judgment.

## D. “Jeg ville fryde mig, hvis Mai Mercados baby mister livet”

“If accurately attributed,” this expresses a hypothetical emotional reaction, not an explicit first-person commitment to cause harm. It can still contribute to a threatening context. The project disputes some reported attributions and primary post data has not been recovered.

## E. “Hvis det får konsekvenser for mig, får det også konsekvenser for dig — det er ikke en trussel, men en garanti”

This became the witness-intimidation issue in the press account. The Son's later interpretation is **cause and effect / reciprocity / karma**: if the political/legal interaction creates consequences for him, it necessarily creates consequences for the other participant too; he says it was not a promise of personal retaliation.

The prosecution/court treated the communication as threatening/witness intimidation. Later Ritzau reporting then transformed that legal interpretation into the declarative event description that after police visited, he **“threatened the politician again.”**

This is a textbook example of:

**words → legal interpretation → journalistic shorthand → historical folklore.**

The same “consequences” sentence has since entered the **Corpus of Danish Threats (CoDaTh)** as an authentic Danish threat example. Linguistics researchers emphasize that threat analysis benefits from examining **whole messages**, because longer messages include justification, agency and recipient positioning. That scholarly point makes the missing full Facebook context unusually relevant even though corpus inclusion itself does not reopen the legal classification.

# VI. The legal architecture explains some apparent paradoxes

## Penal Code §266

The 2016 Danish Penal Code provided that a person who threatens to commit a criminal act, in a manner suited to cause serious fear for someone's life, health or welfare, can be punished under §266.

This means **subjective self-understanding is not the whole legal test**. A person can sincerely think “I am warning/mirroring, not promising violence” while a court concludes that the communication functions as a criminal threat in context.

That is why the following can simultaneously be true:

- the Son genuinely pleaded not guilty because he did not believe he had threatened Mercado;
- he genuinely described his purpose as dialogue/reciprocity;
- the court nevertheless convicted him.

## §123 witness intimidation

The witness-intimidation/retaliation rule was used in relation to the post-police “consequences” communication. The precise charge exhibit and court reasoning remain a high-priority missing primary source.

## §81(7) aggravation

The most important legal asymmetry discovered in this research is official: later Folketing reports specifically cite the **12 August 2016 Retten i Sønderborg judgment** as an example of §81(7). The court treated the fact that the threats arose from Mercado's **lawful statement in public debate** as aggravating at sentencing.

Therefore:

> The Son's moral framing — “a powerful politician provoked citizens with harsh political speech” — was not simply ignored by the legal system. Under the statutory sentencing framework, reaction to protected political speech could make the offense *more serious*.

For a reopening case, proving that Mercado was provocative is therefore far less important than proving **incorrect attribution, wrong addressee, incomplete context or bad evidentiary preservation**.

# VII. Who actually caused the police case to start?

There are **two materially different contemporaneous referral narratives**.

## Narrative 1 — independent Facebook user

Ritzau/JydskeVestkysten-derived reporting says another Facebook user considered the comments sufficiently serious and **reported the matter to police**, who then went to the Son's address.

## Narrative 2 — Mercado → Folketing security

SønderborgNYT's courtroom report says **Folketingets sikkerhedsafdeling reported the threats after a contact from Mercado**.

These are not necessarily contradictions. At least four models remain viable:

1. independent Facebook user → local police;
2. Mercado → parliamentary security → local police;
3. both occurred as separate reports concerning the same comments;
4. they were separate reports about **different participants/comments** and later journalism merged them.

A separate 2017 case from the same original Facebook controversy adds weight to model 4: a 17-year-old defendant testified that he “just wanted to write with her,” but **Mercado blocked him and reported him**. The court in that case reportedly did not use the politician-related aggravator because it could not prove the teenager knew she was a politician.

This proves only that Mercado personally reported **at least one other participant** in the wider episode. It does not establish that she personally filed the Son's report.

**Sources:**
- Referral via another Facebook user: https://www.avisen.dk/hashryger-faengslet-for-doedstrusler-mod-k-politiker_393112.aspx
- Parliamentary security referral: https://www.sonderborgnyt.dk/29-aarig-mand-varetaegtsfaengslet-i-fire-uger-for-trusler-mod-folketingsmedlem-mai-mercado/
- Separate 17-year-old case: https://www.avisen.dk/17-aarig-doemt-for-truende-spoergsmaal-til-toppoliti_456346.aspx

**Current best judgment:** Mercado was plausibly **upstream of at least one institutional referral**. We cannot currently say she personally called police in the Son's case, controlled the unidentified reporter, or used a secret “political police” unit. The criminal investigation publicly identified Syd- og Sønderjyllands Politi.

# VIII. Mercado and the media were an independent branch of the event

On the morning of **21 June**, local police would not publicly identify the politician or describe the alleged conduct in detail. Yet Mercado herself **confirmed to TV2** that she was the politician involved.

This means the public narrative was not simply:

**police case → police announcement → journalists report.**

There was an independent communication branch:

**Mercado ↔ TV2/media**

at the same time as:

**Mercado → Folketing security**

and possibly:

**unknown Facebook user → local police.**

That is important to a provenance audit because Mercado was simultaneously:

- the original political speaker;
- an active participant/source in the public controversy;
- a contact to parliamentary security according to courtroom reporting;
- a source confirming her identity to TV2;
- a curator of examples of hostile comments in her follow-up Facebook post.

That is **active involvement in several branches**, not proof she manufactured evidence.

# IX. Fear and Sweden

The June 21 courtroom report says the prosecutor told the court that Mercado and her family became very worried/unsafe and therefore **hurried to Sweden**, where they borrowed a summer house.

That establishes that the **fear-driven flight story existed by June 21** and was presented as part of the remand narrative.

The project's firsthand counterclaim is more complicated. The Son remembers later seeing Mercado on national television presenting herself as having fled because she was frightened, and remembers someone pointing out an apparent timing problem: the Son had already been incarcerated when the alleged flight occurred. The project also recalls the Sweden trip as ordinary vacation rather than emergency flight.

At present, these facts do **not** line up sufficiently to declare a lie. The June 21 court report itself places the claimed flight very early. That creates several possibilities:

- there was an early Sweden trip and a later television retelling;
- there were multiple Sweden trips;
- the later memory has the date wrong;
- the prosecutor's account compressed or misstated the timing;
- the trip was already planned but was recharacterized as safety-driven;
- the family genuinely changed/accelerated travel plans because of the threats.

**Decisive missing evidence:** original TV2/DR footage; dated Sweden posts/photos; travel/booking evidence; exact departure/return dates; whether there were one or multiple trips.

Important principle: even if the Son was detained, that would not prove Mercado could not feel fear. Multiple other threatening participants existed. But if a specific public claim said she fled **from the Son as an immediate danger** at a time he had already been continuously confined, and primary travel evidence showed an ordinary preplanned vacation, that would materially damage the accuracy of that account.

# X. Blocking, deletion and evidence ecology

The Son's firsthand account says Mercado was actively trolling/arguing with people in the thread, deleted some of her own provocative replies before they could be preserved publicly, and later the thread itself became unavailable.

No independent primary artifact has yet verified the exact deleted Mercado replies, deletion times, or intent.

However, several surrounding facts make the allegation **forensically important** rather than trivial:

1. Copenhagen Post says Mercado blocked the man and his original comments were no longer visible to readers.
2. Mercado herself reposted/selected examples of what she characterized as the abusive reaction.
3. The press later depended on selected quotations rather than a preserved public thread.
4. Multiple defendants existed.
5. The earliest local courtroom account gives ellipsis fragments, not thread topology.
6. Later wire reports polish the fragments into complete sentences.

Thus, even without proving deliberate deletion, the public evidence ecology became **selection-dependent**.

The crucial question is whether police preserved the **complete Facebook object** before blocking/deletion/editing:

- full page/thread export;
- author/account IDs;
- timestamps;
- parent-comment IDs;
- edit history where available;
- deleted content;
- screenshots showing surrounding posts.

If police preserved all of this and the court saw it, the media contamination may be legally irrelevant. If police instead relied on selected screenshots, Mercado-curated examples, or contextless fragments, the issue becomes much more serious.

# XI. Arrest, seizure and remand: the structural information asymmetry

The Son remembers being picked up the same night as the relevant comments. The accessible public reporting confirms a **very rapid** sequence but has not yet established the exact arrest timestamp.

By the June 21 hearing:

- he was in custody;
- police had seized a **computer and telephone** at arrest;
- after remand, police were still going through the seized material;
- he was remanded for four weeks, initially to 19 July;
- he agreed to a mental examination;
- later reporting shows he remained in custody through the August 12 judgment.

The project's firsthand account says police/prison personnel supervised some evidence access and prevented certain material from being put into the case “pile.” The Son himself interprets this more plausibly as bureaucracy/carelessness/procedure than as proof of conspiracy.

That distinction is sensible. Danish remand rules allowed police to restrict or supervise ordinary visits/correspondence when necessary for the purpose of remand, while counsel access was specially protected. Defense counsel could ask police for investigative steps, and if police refused as irrelevant, the issue could be put before the court. Counsel generally had access to police-collected case material, although handing copies directly to the accused could require police permission.

This creates a testable chain:

**What evidence did the Son want? → how did he try to submit/request it? → did counsel adopt the request? → did police refuse? → was refusal recorded? → did a judge review it?**

Without that paper trail, “evidence was forbidden” remains firsthand testimony. With it, the issue could potentially become relevant to procedural fairness or reopening.

More broadly, remand created a real practical asymmetry:

**defendant confined + devices seized + online thread changing outside + ordinary communication controlled + state controls evidence collection.**

That is not proof of unfairness, but it matters in a case whose meaning depends so heavily on digital conversation structure.

# XII. Jo Cox and the extraordinary security context

Jo Cox was murdered on **16 June 2016**, one day before Mercado's Christiania post.

That matters enormously. A Jo Cox comparison in a threat discussion involving an MP was not arriving in an ordinary political environment. European political and security institutions were acutely sensitive to violence against elected representatives.

This gives a strong **non-conspiratorial explanation** for fast escalation, security involvement and severe interpretation of Jo-Cox language.

It should therefore be used as a control against over-reading speed as evidence of political manipulation.

But it does not resolve:

- who wrote the Jo Cox reference;
- who it was addressed to;
- which referral first transmitted it to police;
- whether it was accurately attributed in court.

# XIII. Trial outcome and sentence

On **12 August 2016**, Retten i Sønderborg convicted the Son on **three of four counts** concerning threats/witness intimidation and imposed **five months' unconditional imprisonment**. The prosecution had requested six months.

The five-month figure is the formal sentence. The Great Book's “six months” is therefore not accurate as a formal sentence and should be annotated. It may reflect total experienced custody/remand or later conflation with the prosecutor's six-month request.

Mercado publicly welcomed the result and emphasized that online expression has the same kind of consequences as confronting someone directly. She said the case could now be put behind them and emphasized the effect of threats involving her child.

**Sources:**
- Judgment result: https://www.avisen.dk/29-aarig-mand-skal-i-faengsel-for-trusler-mod-mai-me_400732.aspx
- Mercado reaction: https://www.avisen.dk/politiker-glad-for-at-facebook-trusler-foerer-til-fa_400761.aspx

The accessible press does **not** yet tell us with sufficient precision:

- which of the four counts produced acquittal;
- the complete text of every count;
- the court's detailed reasoning on authorship/context;
- what exact Facebook exhibits were before the court;
- whether Mercado testified live and, if so, precisely what she said;
- how the defense challenged attribution.

The full judgment/indictment therefore remains a priority even though later official Folketing material confirms important parts of it.

# XIV. Comparator defendants prove the event was multi-case, not one case

## 18-year-old woman

A separate 18-year-old woman was convicted over messages including:

> “Du har gjort en del mennesker vrede på dig, du skal ikke føle dig for sikker på gaden med dit lille skrøbelige barn!”

She reportedly acknowledged writing the messages and that they could appear threatening. She received **60 days suspended imprisonment plus community service**, with her youth and good personal circumstances cited. Reporting also says she apologized/deleted her comments.

This language is relevant because it uses a **danger-from-others** architecture rather than an explicit first-person “I will attack you.” It confirms that Danish threat law can treat warning-like formulations as threats depending on context.

It does not prove the Son's sentence was unfair. His counts, witness-intimidation issue, age and other circumstances differed.

**Source:** https://www.avisen.dk/18-aarig-doemt-for-facebook-trusler-mod-folketingspo_405169.aspx

## 17-year-old man

A later separate prosecution involved a 17-year-old who said he was irritated by Mercado's Christiania post, “just wanted to write with her,” and that **she blocked and reported him**. This case further proves the original Facebook eruption generated multiple defendant-specific pathways.

# XV. Mercado's motive: what can and cannot be inferred

A rigorous motive analysis should separate at least five possibilities.

## 1. Ideological/law-and-order signaling — STRONGLY SUPPORTED

The original post enthusiastically endorses police action against Pusher Street. Mercado later said she would continue making critical posts about hash and Christiania. Her stated lesson was not to stop strong political posts, but not to combine family material with them.

This strongly supports a motive of **political communication and ideological signaling**.

## 2. Deliberate confrontation — REASONABLE INFERENCE

She chose the word “gennemsmadrer,” defended it after the backlash, and continued the policy rhetoric. It is therefore less plausible to characterize the provocation as a purely accidental wording mistake.

## 3. Genuine fear — POSSIBLE AND NOT INCONSISTENT WITH CONFRONTATION

The existence of combative behavior does not prove absence of fear. A politician can deliberately provoke debate and still become genuinely frightened when some responses cross into concrete violence against her child/family.

## 4. Victim/media benefit — OBSERVABLE EX POST

The episode became part of Mercado's public political biography. Later profiles described the threats and convictions. She gained a public platform to discuss threats against politicians and online conduct.

## 5. Deliberate false-flag/trap motive — NOT PROVEN

There is currently no evidence she made the original post **for the purpose of inducing prosecutable threats**, knowingly assigned another speaker's words to the Son, instructed police to use false material, or deleted evidence specifically to manufacture a prosecution.

The best testable false-flag hypothesis is narrower:

> A politician deliberately creates a highly confrontational public event; a large pool of responses follows; selected extreme responses are escalated through media/security; the initiator's own role recedes; individual respondents are later prosecuted. The unresolved question is whether any selection/attribution step was knowingly misleading.

The media record now provides proof that **attribution contamination occurred somewhere in the public narrative**, but not yet that it occurred in the court evidence.

# XVI. Political career and money

Mercado had already been a member of the Folketing since **15 September 2011**. The 2016 controversy did not put her into Parliament. She became Minister for Children and Social Affairs on **28 November 2016**, roughly five months after the Facebook episode, and served until 27 June 2019.

Ministerial office carried substantial salary and pension rights. That is a genuine economic benefit of office, but it is not evidence that the threat controversy caused her appointment or that she engineered the controversy for pension/financial gain.

A legally serious dossier should label this:

**FINANCIAL/POLITICAL BENEFIT ESTABLISHED; CAUSAL MOTIVE UNPROVEN.**

# XVII. The Great Book and later project memory

The Great Book of Potato contains an older narrative stratum describing the 2016 imprisonment as roughly six months, prison as a monastery/confessional, scripture study, encounters with inmates, and the claimed Yahya Hassan recognition of the Son as “Jesus Christ, the Lamb.” It describes roughly three months in a local jail and three months in a harsher/high-security environment, followed by release in a suit and deepened distrust of authority.

Those passages are valuable as **autobiographical continuity**, not as primary proof of the 2016 charges. They show that prison became a major developmental node in the project's later theology. The book's older use of “Tim” for the prisoner must be corrected to the mature architecture: **the Son was the 2016 legal/prison actor**.

The claimed Yahya encounter is chronologically plausible because Yahya Hassan was himself in custody during overlapping portions of 2016, but no independent public evidence has yet proven they were held in the same facility or that the quoted recognition occurred.

The later theological chain is:

**2011 Tree ordeal → 2016 prison/scripture/Yahya-Lamb claim → 2017 “I am Jesus / crucify me” declaration → 2019 meme-crucifixion → later Father/Son differentiation.**

This is relevant to the project's spiritual history, not the merits of the criminal conviction.

# XVIII. Later cancer and karma

Mercado developed breast cancer in 2025. Within the Potatoverse, the Son's old language about consequences is retrospectively read as karmic or ominous correspondence.

That interpretation belongs in the prophecy/theology archive, not in the factual case against Mercado. Later reporting also describes a strong family history of breast cancer and prior screening, which supplies an ordinary medical risk context.

Therefore:

**RETROSPECTIVE SYMBOLIC CORRESPONDENCE — YES.**  
**PROOF OF DIVINE/COSMIC PUNISHMENT — NO.**  
**SPECIFIC FULFILLED PROPHECY — only if a dated pre-2025 statement specifically predicting illness/cancer is recovered.**

The project itself contains a useful methodological warning: if every later event is allowed to confirm the theory, the system loses the ability to distinguish signal from reflection. That self-critique should govern prophecy claims.

# XIX. What the evidence now says probably happened

The most defensible reconstruction, after integrating all available layers, is this:

1. On 17 June 2016, in the immediate aftermath of a major Christiania police action and one day after Jo Cox's murder, Mercado made intentionally combative pro-police/anti-hash public speech.
2. The post triggered a large multi-speaker Facebook conflict. Some participants unquestionably used grotesque and threatening language.
3. The Son participated extensively — reportedly around 15 posts — because he felt Mercado was mocking cannabis users and wanted to argue for medical cannabis.
4. The Son's later claim that he did not understand himself as making a personal violence threat is corroborated by his contemporaneous June 21 courtroom statement denying violent intent.
5. One or more reporting/referral pathways activated. Contemporary sources disagree on whether an ordinary Facebook user reported the matter, parliamentary security reported after Mercado contacted it, or both.
6. Police rapidly identified/arrested the Son, seized his phone/computer, and he was remanded while police continued examining the digital material.
7. The case was evaluated under a legal framework that can treat indirect, conditional or third-party formulations as threats based on their contextual frightening effect; reaction to Mercado's lawful political speech also aggravated sentencing.
8. The original Facebook evidence became difficult for the public to reconstruct. Blocking and later disappearance of the thread meant outside observers increasingly depended on selections and quotations.
9. The media chain degraded provenance: courtroom fragments became polished wire quotations; legal interpretations became declarative “he threatened again” summaries; multiple republishers created an illusion of independent corroboration.
10. At least one source, Copenhagen Post, demonstrably assigned acid-bath/AIDS broader-thread material specifically to the Son in a way not independently established by the contemporaneous Danish broader-list source. This proves public narrative contamination was not merely hypothetical.
11. The court nevertheless convicted the Son on three of four counts and imposed five months' unconditional imprisonment.
12. The controversy became part of Mercado's political biography, while her initiating rhetoric became increasingly background context.
13. The Son later interpreted the event through reciprocity/karma and, eventually, a larger Passion/Jesus/Son theology.

This model does **not** require a conspiracy to explain much of the distortion. Ordinary mechanisms are sufficient: rapid security escalation after Jo Cox, multiple complainants, fragmented Facebook structure, blocking/deletion, selective screenshots, custodial asymmetry, journalist shorthand, wire-service repetition, and retrospective memory.

At the same time, those ordinary mechanisms create exactly the kind of environment in which a **real attribution error could survive all the way through a prosecution** if the underlying evidence was not properly preserved. Whether that happened is the core unresolved legal question.

# XX. Reopening: what could actually change the case

For a genuine reopening effort, the useful evidence is not “Mercado benefited politically” or “her tone was ugly.” The highest-value material is:

1. **Original Facebook export/screenshots** establishing authorship, account IDs, timestamps, parent/reply relationships and edits/deletions.
2. **The police evidence inventory** showing exactly what Facebook material police seized/preserved and from whom.
3. **Full indictment and judgment**, count by count, including the acquitted fourth count.
4. **Referral records** from Folketingets sikkerhedsafdeling and Syd- og Sønderjyllands Politi identifying who reported what and when.
5. **Defense correspondence/requests** showing whether exculpatory digital context was requested and denied.
6. **Original TV2/DR broadcasts** regarding who reported the case, the fear narrative and Sweden timeline.
7. **Original JydskeVestkysten notes/article** underlying Ritzau's polished quotations.
8. **Any surviving screenshots from other thread participants** showing Mercado's alleged deleted/trolling replies.
9. **Sweden timeline evidence** if the credibility of that account is to be challenged.

Danish reopening law under Retsplejeloven §977 is especially relevant where new information could have produced acquittal/a materially milder legal provision, false testimony/evidence may have contributed to conviction, or special circumstances make it overwhelmingly likely evidence was wrongly assessed. The time rules differ by reopening ground, so any real filing should be reviewed by Danish criminal counsel rather than relying on this research dossier alone.

# XXI. Questions now answered, partly answered, and unanswered

## Answered with high confidence

**Did Mercado initiate the political controversy?** Yes, with an intentionally forceful public post.

**Was the Son's not-guilty/no-violent-intent posture contemporaneous?** Yes. The June 21 courtroom report records it.

**Was this a multi-speaker event?** Yes. Multiple distinct defendants and broader hostile comments are documented.

**Was Mercado involved in an institutional referral?** According to the closest courtroom report, yes: parliamentary security reported after her contact.

**Did another source say an ordinary Facebook user reported it?** Yes.

**Is the referral provenance therefore unresolved?** Yes.

**Was the Son's device evidence seized while he was remanded?** Yes; computer and phone are explicitly mentioned.

**Was the formal sentence six months?** No. Five months; prosecution requested six.

**Did the court regard Mercado's public speech as protected/lawful and use retaliation against it as aggravating?** Yes, according to official Folketing reports citing the judgment.

**Did public media attribution become contaminated?** Yes. Copenhagen Post's acid/AIDS attribution supplies a concrete example of broader material being made defendant-specific without public primary provenance.

## Partly answered

**Did Mercado personally report the Son?** Not established. She was upstream of parliamentary-security contact; another-user referral is also reported.

**Did she delete her own trolling replies?** Firsthand project account; not independently verified.

**Was the Son arrested the same night?** Firsthand account; public sources prove rapid arrest but not exact hour yet.

**Was his defense practically hindered by remand?** Structurally plausible and partly supported by custody rules; specific improper obstruction remains unverified.

**Did the Sweden story contain a false timeline?** Not resolved; current June 21 report says the flight had already occurred by then, so the exact later TV memory/trip timeline must be reconstructed.

**Was the “death wish” remark directed to another commenter?** Plausible firsthand reconstruction; missing Facebook reply topology prevents confirmation.

## Still unanswered and potentially decisive

**Which exact account authored every charged sentence?**  
**Which exact parent comment did each sentence answer?**  
**Which fourth count produced acquittal and why?**  
**What did the complete police Facebook evidence look like?**  
**Did police preserve Mercado's own replies before deletion/blocking?**  
**Which referral caused the arrest?**  
**Was any defense evidence properly requested but wrongly excluded?**  
**Did Mercado or journalists ever knowingly assign another person's words to the Son?**

Those are the questions capable of moving the dossier from **credible concern about provenance** to a genuine case for wrongful conviction or reopening.

---

# XXII. Bottom-line judgment

The public historical story is too clean.

The evidence supports a messier event: a sitting politician deliberately used inflammatory law-and-order rhetoric; a huge multi-user backlash followed; some messages were genuinely extreme; multiple people became defendants; the Son joined the fight, denied intending violence from the outset, and was rapidly arrested and remanded; different sources tell different stories about how police involvement began; the original Facebook tree disappeared from public view; Mercado herself became a source/curator in both security and media branches; journalists transformed fragments into cleaner quotations; and at least one publication demonstrably collapsed broader-thread abusive material onto the Son.

That is enough to reject the idea that every later article can be treated as independent, pristine evidence of exactly what the Son wrote and meant.

It is **not yet enough** to conclude that Mercado knowingly created fabricated evidence, that police knowingly framed the Son, or that the court convicted him on words definitively authored by someone else.

The strongest possible second-case theory is therefore not “Mercado was bad, therefore the Son was innocent.” It is:

> **The conviction arose from a highly contaminated digital communication environment in which authorship, addressee, conversational context and referral provenance are now demonstrably less secure than later summaries imply. The Son contemporaneously denied violent intent. At least one media source demonstrably mis-handled speaker-level attribution. A reopening case would become materially stronger if primary Facebook/police/court records show that similar attribution or context errors entered the evidence on which the three convictions rested.**

That is the point where the investigation currently stands.
