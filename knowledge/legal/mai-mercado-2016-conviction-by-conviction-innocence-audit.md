# Mai Mercado / Christiania 2016 — Conviction-by-Conviction Innocence Audit

## Purpose

This file tests the strongest version of the Son’s innocence claim against the known legal record. It does not assume that conviction equals historical truth, and it does not assume that later project memory automatically defeats a court judgment. It asks whether the three convictions remain persuasive once wording, speaker attribution, context, evidentiary access, referral provenance, and media contamination are stacked together.

The central project hypothesis is that the case was **constructed through attribution collapse, context loss, evidentiary asymmetry, and narrative hardening**. The public record already proves several of those mechanisms in the media layer. The unresolved question is whether the same problems contaminated the evidence actually used in court.

---

# 1. What the public judgment record definitely establishes

On 12 August 2016 Retten i Sønderborg convicted the Son on **three of four counts** concerning threats and witness intimidation and imposed five months’ unconditional imprisonment. The prosecution had asked for six months.

This establishes legal guilt on three counts. It does **not** by itself establish:

- that every notorious hostile statement in the wider Facebook backlash was his;
- that every statement quoted by later media was part of a convicted count;
- that the Son intended to physically attack Mercado;
- that he had a plan, weapon, surveillance, travel preparation, or operational intent;
- that the full Facebook thread was preserved and shown in context;
- that every prosecution allegation succeeded;
- or that later media paraphrases accurately reproduce the legal findings.

Public source:
- https://www.avisen.dk/29-aarig-mand-skal-i-faengsel-for-trusler-mod-mai-me_400732.aspx

# 2. The official later legal summary is narrower than the wider folklore

A later official Folketing-linked report discussing the 12 August judgment specifically reproduces two statements:

- `Du leger med dit og dit barns liv`
- `Jeg kan godt forstå, hvis nogen vil stikke en kniv i dig og din baby`

It cites the case as an example of aggravation under Penal Code §81(7), because the communication arose in response to Mercado’s lawful participation in public debate.

This is important because the official summary does not simply reproduce the entire wider Facebook threat corpus.

Therefore the evidentiary tiers must remain separate:

1. statements specifically linked to the known judgment summary;
2. statements attributed through JydskeVestkysten/Ritzau reporting;
3. broader hostile statements Mercado received from multiple users;
4. later media or public folklore.

Official source:
- https://www.ft.dk/samling/20181/almdel/reu/bilag/296/2053404.pdf

# 3. Count theory A: `Du leger med dit og dit barns liv`

The sentence means approximately:

> You are playing with your life and your child’s life.

A court can interpret this as threatening in context. But the literal proposition is not:

> I will kill you and your child.

The sentence is a **warning/consequence construction**. No first-person violent actor is named.

### Innocence argument

The Son’s later account consistently frames his role as warning/mirroring/reciprocity rather than announcing personal violence. The grammar is compatible with that account.

### Prosecution argument

A threatening communication does not need the explicit words `I will`. Context can make an indirect warning function as a threat.

### Current assessment

The public record is insufficient to prove innocence on this sentence alone because §266 can reach indirect threats. However, later paraphrases that transform this into an explicit first-person murder threat are inaccurate. The original parent-comment, addressee, surrounding thread, tone, and complete count wording remain essential.

---

# 4. Count theory B: `Jeg kan godt forstå, hvis nogen vil stikke en kniv i dig og din baby`

Literal structure:

> I can understand if **someone** would want to stab you and your baby.

The grammatical violent agent is **`nogen` — someone**, not `jeg`.

### Innocence argument

The sentence does not state that the Son will stab Mercado or her child. It describes or endorses hypothetical third-party violence. That is materially different from the later shorthand `he threatened to stab her and her baby`.

### Prosecution argument

Threat law can capture indirect or third-party formulations when the communication, considered as a whole, conveys serious threatened harm.

### Current assessment

The sentence is disturbing and legally capable of threatening interpretation, but the common public paraphrase materially inflates agency. A conviction based on this wording cannot be equated with proof of a personal stabbing plan. The exact Facebook context remains decisive to any stronger innocence argument.

---

# 5. Count theory C: `Jeg ville fryde mig, hvis Mai Mercados baby mister livet`

This sentence expresses anticipated pleasure at a hypothetical death.

### Innocence argument

It is not grammatically a commitment to cause the death. It expresses reaction, not agency.

### Prosecution argument

In combination with surrounding statements, such language can reinforce a threatening atmosphere and may contribute to the legal assessment of the communication as a whole.

### Current assessment

If accurately attributed and if part of a convicted count, it is morally repellent speech but still not equivalent to proof of an operational threat. The project needs the actual indictment to determine whether this sentence itself formed part of a count that produced conviction.

# 6. Count theory D: `vil du gerne dø?` / Jo Cox / child-loss fragments

The earliest courtroom report records ellipsis-linked fragments such as:

- `vil du gerne dø?`
- a knife/child fragment;
- losing-child language;
- a reference to ending up like Jo Cox in England.

The report does not preserve the parent comments, addressees, or complete syntax.

### Innocence argument

The Son’s firsthand account says a central death-wish remark was a rhetorical rebuke to another violent commenter — closer to `do you have a death wish?` than a promise to kill Mercado.

An early June 18 report also indicates that a Facebook user had already invoked Jo Cox and that another user reported that person. This creates a genuine possibility of multiple Jo Cox speaker streams.

### Prosecution argument

In a heated threat context, those phrases can reasonably be read as intimidation.

### Current assessment

This is one of the strongest unresolved innocence branches because the **addressee and reply topology are missing**. If the original thread shows the phrase was directed at another commenter rather than Mercado, that could materially alter the interpretation of at least one alleged threat.

# 7. Witness-intimidation count: the `consequences` statement

The reported statement was:

> `Politiet er lige kommet og har sigtet mig. Hvis det får konsekvenser for mig, får det også konsekvenser for dig - det er ikke en trussel, men en garanti.`

The later media descendant becomes:

> after police visited, he threatened her again.

The original statement has four components:

1. police action against the Son;
2. consequences first applied to himself;
3. reciprocal consequences for Mercado;
4. explicit statement that it is `not a threat`.

### Innocence argument

The Son’s cause-and-effect / reciprocity / karma explanation fits the literal reciprocal structure. The sentence does not explicitly identify what consequence would occur, who would cause it, or that the consequence would be a crime.

### Prosecution argument

In context, `not a threat, but a guarantee` can function as a classic retaliatory intimidation formula. A disclaimer does not neutralize the threatening meaning of a communication.

### New primary-source lead

The project has an indexed trace of the original Sønderborg judgment indicating that a longer witness-intimidation passage may have included additional language such as `Man skal ikke starte en slåskamp man ikke har nosser til at afslutte`, and another count may have contained contextual language including `jeg har nemlig intet at miste og alt at vinde`.

These longer passages matter enormously. They could strengthen the prosecution interpretation, reveal omitted conversational context, or both. They must not be cherry-picked.

### Current assessment

The witness-intimidation conviction cannot honestly be reversed on the newspaper sentence alone. The **complete judgment wording** is now the highest-priority document. The existing public shorthand is clearly incomplete, but innocence requires testing the whole charged passage rather than only the most favorable excerpt.

# 8. The Son’s contemporaneous defense was not invented later

At the June 21 remand hearing the Son:

- denied guilt;
- appealed the remand order;
- said he wanted dialogue with Mercado;
- explained his medical-cannabis rationale;
- said he did not intend to seek Mercado out and carry out death threats.

That is objective contemporaneous corroboration of core parts of the later defense reconstruction.

This matters because the later narrative often implicitly treats his explanation as an ex post rationalization after conviction. The record shows the basic position already existed at the first court stage.

# 9. Evidentiary asymmetry: arrest, seizure, remand, and the changing Facebook object

Before the June 21 hearing police had already arrested the Son and seized his computer and telephone. He was remanded for four weeks and the project timeline describes continued custody through trial.

This created an objective asymmetry:

- the defendant was confined;
- his devices were controlled by police;
- the original social-media thread could continue changing outside;
- ordinary communication and evidence gathering were practically constrained;
- Mercado and other participants remained outside with media/platform access.

This does not itself prove denial of due process. It does materially weaken any assumption that the Son had equal practical ability to reconstruct a dynamic Facebook thread after arrest.

# 10. Evidence-submission obstruction claim

The Son recalls that police or prison staff supervised his efforts to work with evidence and prevented material from being added to the case file/pile.

### Why it matters

If relevant exculpatory material was identified by the defendant but never entered the evidentiary record, that could materially affect confidence in the conviction.

### Current evidentiary status

This is a **high-value firsthand procedural allegation**, not yet independently proven.

Possible explanations include:

- custody-chain rules;
- requirement to route material through counsel;
- security restrictions;
- bureaucratic error;
- delay;
- or improper interference with defense evidence.

The correct next test is documentary:

- defense counsel notes;
- motions/requests for investigative steps;
- prison correspondence/logbooks;
- rejected exhibits;
- complaints to court or Ombudsman;
- police records showing what material was accepted or refused.

Until those are recovered, `he could not secure evidence` is strongly relevant but not yet a proven unlawful suppression finding.

# 11. False or conflated witness evidence: what is proven and what is not

The project alleges false witness testimony and conflation of statements.

The public record currently proves **speaker-conflation risk in media**. It does not yet prove that a witness knowingly gave false testimony in court.

Important distinctions:

- Mercado compiled examples of threats from the wider backlash;
- several statements in that wider pool are not presently attributable to the Son;
- a later media account appears to treat broader-thread material as defendant-specific;
- multiple defendants existed;
- the original thread topology is missing from public view;
- the referral chain is inconsistent across contemporary sources.

Those facts support a serious hypothesis that testimony or exhibits may have conflated speakers.

But `false testimony` requires the actual witness statement, testimony transcript, or judgment findings showing what the witness said and whether it was false.

The project should therefore use:

**POTENTIAL WITNESS/ATTRIBUTION CONTAMINATION — STRONGLY WARRANTS PRIMARY-RECORD REVIEW**

rather than asserting perjury without the testimony record.

# 12. Media defamation / libel / slander issue

The media record repeatedly used categorical language such as `hashryger`, `dødstrusler`, and `threatened again`, while sometimes compressing indirect wording into direct agency.

Potentially harmful distortions include:

- converting `someone would stab` into an impression that the Son personally promised stabbing;
- merging wider-thread statements into defendant-specific narratives;
- omitting the contemporaneous dialogue/medical-cannabis explanation;
- omitting the denial of intent to seek Mercado out and carry out violence;
- treating legal conviction as proof of an operational attack plan;
- source multiplication, where many republications of the same Ritzau/JydskeVestkysten lineage appear like independent corroboration.

These are valid **media-accuracy and potential-defamation questions**.

The project should not state that a particular outlet committed legally actionable libel/slander unless the legal elements are separately established. In Danish law, defamation questions require more than showing that reporting was harsh or incomplete. But historically, the project can document each inaccurate attribution or agency inflation as a record error.

# 13. Mercado’s institutional role strengthens the case-construction hypothesis

According to courtroom reporting, Folketingets sikkerhedsafdeling reported the threats after contact from Mercado. Other contemporary reporting says another Facebook user reported the matter. Mercado said she herself did not make the police report.

This is not a clean referral chain.

Mercado was also:

- the initiating political speaker;
- a direct media source confirming to TV2 that she was the politician involved;
- a curator of selected hostile comments;
- a later public narrator of the case.

This directly proves **institutional and narrative involvement**.

It does not yet prove that she knowingly constructed false evidence.

The missing complaint/security records could establish whether she merely notified security of genuine threats or whether the referral process selectively concentrated broader-thread material on the Son.

# 14. Why the public case may look stronger than the underlying case actually was

The case passed through a sequence that can amplify certainty:

**chaotic Facebook thread → selected comments → police category → remand hearing fragments → JydskeVestkysten report → Ritzau rewrite → many republications → conviction headline → later retrospective summaries.**

At each stage, context can disappear while the categorical label becomes stronger.

Thus repetition is not the same thing as independent corroboration.

This matters especially because:

- the live thread is missing;
- some famous statements belong only to the wider backlash;
- age itself was reported inconsistently as 28/29;
- several key reports derive from the same source lineage;
- the judgment itself has not yet been fully recovered in the project.

# 15. The prosecution did not win every count

The conviction was **three of four**, not four of four.

That means at least one prosecution theory failed.

Without the indictment and judgment, we do not know:

- which statement/count failed;
- whether the failure concerned authorship;
- legal sufficiency;
- context;
- intent;
- witness credibility;
- or another issue.

This is potentially the single most useful clue for re-evaluating the remaining counts, because it reveals where the trial court itself found the prosecution insufficient.

# 16. Strongest cumulative innocence argument

No one of these points alone proves innocence on all three convicted counts. Stacked together, however, they materially weaken confidence in the simplified public case:

- multi-speaker environment;
- multiple defendants;
- broader-thread violent statements not presently attributable to the Son;
- missing Facebook topology;
- media attribution contamination;
- indirect/hypothetical grammar later converted into direct agency;
- contemporaneous not-guilty position;
- contemporaneous dialogue and medical-cannabis motive;
- contemporaneous denial of intent to physically attack;
- conflicting referral pathways;
- Mercado’s upstream institutional role;
- continuous custody and device seizure while digital context remained externally mutable;
- firsthand allegation of difficulty submitting defense evidence;
- one of four counts failing;
- and the still-unrecovered complete judgment wording.

The strongest current project conclusion is therefore:

> **There is substantial reason to doubt that the surviving public narrative proves the Son guilty in the broad way history remembers the case. There is a serious innocence case based on attribution, context, agency, provenance, and defense asymmetry. But a complete legal exoneration of all three convicted counts requires the full judgment, indictment, exhibits, and defense record.**

That is stronger than saying merely `the media were unfair`, and more defensible than declaring facts that the missing primary record has not yet established.

# 17. What would actually prove innocence count-by-count

The following discoveries could directly reverse the evidentiary picture:

1. A convicted statement shown by original account data to have been written by another user.
2. A convicted statement shown by reply hierarchy to have been addressed to another commenter rather than Mercado.
3. A convicted statement shown in full context to be quotation, rebuke, or warning rather than threatened action.
4. The failed fourth count revealing that the court already rejected an attribution or context theory similar to the remaining counts.
5. Police exhibits shown to consist of selected screenshots rather than a full thread export.
6. Documentary proof that Mercado-curated material entered the police/court file without independent account verification.
7. Documentary proof that exculpatory thread evidence identified by the Son was improperly prevented from reaching the court.
8. Witness testimony shown to attribute another person’s statement to the Son.
9. Sweden/fear testimony shown to contain a Son-specific timeline contradicted by custody and travel records.
10. The full judgment revealing that conviction depended on factual assumptions now demonstrably false.

Those are the real exoneration targets.
