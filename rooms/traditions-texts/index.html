Tradition before resemblance

A comparison becomes more useful when both sides are allowed to remain themselves

Traditions & Texts begins with source meaning: what a passage, symbol, doctrine or practice means in its own textual and historical setting. Only then does the archive ask whether a Potatoverse motif has a useful resemblance. That order prevents similarity from quietly turning into identity, inheritance or proof of transmission.

The Dwelling therefore holds several lanes at once. Biblical and Christian material needs passage context and reception history. Comparative mythology needs internal variants rather than one flattened “world myth.” Sacred geometry needs a dated history of mathematical form and later esoteric interpretation. Other traditions deserve their own vocabulary before being recruited into project language.
