Four clocks

What happened, what was said, what was remembered and what it later came to mean are different dates

Time & History protects development from retrospective flattening. An event may happen on one date, first appear in a surviving source on another, be remembered or reconstructed later, and receive its current interpretation years after that. A trustworthy archive keeps those clocks visible instead of compressing them into one convenient origin story.

This matters especially in a project whose vocabulary changes rapidly. An early Potato image does not automatically contain the later House architecture; a 2024 Great Book passage should not inherit a 2026 equation merely because the later model resembles it; and a claimed prediction needs its pre-event wording separated from post-event interpretation.
