A good Room should give you something before it tells you where you are. Enter one and meet a problem worth thinking about: God and relation, the fate of memory, the meaning of time, the body and brain, the power behind infrastructure, the way attention becomes culture, the difference between a symbol and a model, or the question of what can survive correction.

The map prevents confusion. The inhabitants are the reason to enter.

Ten doors, larger questions

The Dwellings are ten different ways reality can become difficult.

Canon asks what God-language, Father, Son, Spirit, Door and Garden are trying to say about source and relation. Archive asks what survives us, what deserves belief, and whether a memory is still alive when only a copy remains. Time asks whether meaning belongs to the moment itself or to the later life that finally knows how to read it.

Traditions asks why trees, ladders, mountains, eyes, gates, deaths and returns appear again and again in sacred imagination. Science asks when a beautiful analogy earns the right to become a model. Life & Body asks how a living organism turns matter, sensation, rhythm and space into a world it can inhabit.

World Systems asks what power is made of when speeches end and electricity, ports, law, logistics, debt, labor and institutions begin. Culture asks what repeated attention does to a person and what repeated people do to a story. Works asks what art can discover that explanation cannot. Forge asks what remains true after the favorite story has been heated, contradicted and made smaller.

Choose the question that bothers you. The filing structure can wait until you need it.

How the filing structure works

Dwelling scale

House → Dwelling/Mansion → Room is the active containment model. The ten entries above are the load-bearing Dwellings; the 38 nested contexts are Rooms beneath them. “Mansion” remains the older English symbolic bridge, while “Dwelling” is the preferred neutral term. Chamber remains reserved; no active Chambers are registered.
