Nested Room · Archive & Sources

Witness & Attestation

This Room answers a narrow but crucial question: what can the archive actually show was said, posted, recorded, published or witnessed, and when?

Boundary: attestation can establish wording and timing. It does not automatically establish the truth of the claim being attested.

Put the artifact on the table

“I remember saying it” and “here it is” are both useful. They are not the same gift.

Imagine Tim remembers a line from years ago with complete confidence. That matters. Now imagine somebody finds the original post with its date, spelling, surrounding replies and account context. The second thing does not make the memory worthless. It changes what the archive is allowed to say.

A remembered line can tell us what Tim now recognizes as part of his past. A surviving post can tell us what wording existed by a particular time. A screenshot can preserve wording while losing the thread around it. A clipped video can preserve a sentence while changing the apparent tone. A repost can preserve content while proving almost nothing about the original publication time.

Witness is restraint before it is certainty.

The artifact says what it says. Do not make it testify to the part that is outside the frame.

Spelling can be evidence.

Tim's actual public voice is often messy, fast, funny and self-correcting. “Cleaning up” a quote may make it easier to read while quietly removing evidence about cadence and context.

Context can change force without changing words.

“I am God” in a title, an argument, a joke, a repeated declaration and a response to somebody else can be the same words doing different social work.

A witness does not need to explain the whole person. It needs to stop us inventing the missing pixels.

That is why this Room feeds both Claims and the Tim portrait: exact wording gives the portrait something solid enough to remain personal without becoming fictionalized.

The witness problem

Memory, quotation and evidence are different things

A remembered phrase may be important. A recovered screenshot may be stronger. An original timestamped post may be stronger still. The archive needs to know which one it has before it builds history on top of it.

Direct primaryOriginal post, recording, document or first-party artifact with recoverable date/context.
Recovered primaryOriginal wording recovered from conversation history, export or archived capture, with recovery status stated.
Secondary witnessAnother person or source reports what was said or done. Useful, but not identical to the original artifact.
ReconstructionLater synthesis from partial evidence. May be valuable, but must not be presented as exact attestation.

“Earliest recovered wording” is not the same claim as “first time this was ever thought or said.”

Four attestations, four different claims

2024-10-06 · Potato of Life.

“Hello I am the Potato of Life. My name is Tim Dooley.” This supports a clear public identity claim by this date.

2025-04-21 · Axis.

“the potato axis is real.” This supports public Axis wording in the Turning corridor; it does not establish a physical cosmic axis.

2025-05-03 · God.

The current incomplete public compilation contains exact “I am God” instances on this date. The archive deliberately calls this a lower bound: earliest recovered exact public wording, not proof no earlier instance existed.

A captured public title-stack includes “I am The God in the machine, and The returning Messiah” alongside Potato/World-Axis material. Witness stores the wording and date; Theology owns what those titles mean.

A digital witness should have a provenance graph, not only a citation

The W3C PROV model gives this Room a useful literal grammar: an entity is the artifact itself; an activity is what created or changed it; an agent is the person, organization or software associated with that activity. A post can therefore be one entity, a screenshot another entity generated by a capture activity, and a cropped quote-card a third entity generated by an edit activity. That is much clearer than storing all three as equivalent “sources.”

Reference: W3C PROV Model Primer ↗

Authenticity, attestation and truth are three separate questions

AuthenticityIs the asset what its provenance claims it is, and has its bound content been altered?
AttestationWhat wording, image or action does the artifact actually show, by whom and when?
TruthIs the proposition inside the artifact factually correct?
InterpretationWhat later meaning should the project assign to the attested artifact?

C2PA makes the distinction particularly explicit. Content Credentials can carry signed assertions about an asset's creation and editing history, but the specification does not attempt to decide whether those assertions are morally “good” or whether every proposition shown in the media is true. Provenance is a trust signal and integrity mechanism, not an oracle.

Five artifact classes the archive should stop mixing

Native originalOriginal first-party post, upload, VOD, document or file with stable identifier/metadata where available.
Faithful captureScreenshot/export/capture preserving enough source context to identify the original, while retaining crop/timezone/context limitations.
Derived copyMirror, repost, quote-card, clip, transcription or conversion. Another distribution event; not automatically another independent witness.
Edited derivativeCrop, splice, annotation, translation, enhancement or montage. The edit activity belongs in the provenance chain.
ReconstructionLater recreation from fragments, memory or multiple derivatives. Never exact attestation.

What a good attestation record contains

At minimum: artifact identifier or best locator; speaker/publisher; platform; publication timestamp and timezone/precision; capture time separately; exact or bounded wording; context envelope; artifact class; derivation parent; edit/capture activities; hash or integrity signal where available; recovery method; and missing/uncertain fields. If a URL, post ID or source artifact is missing, the absence should remain visible.

Why exact wording matters

Voice is part of provenance. A polished paraphrase may preserve proposition while losing humor, hesitation, repetition, slang, spelling, self-interruption or emphasis—the very things that make a person recognizable.

Small differences can change later theology. “I am God,” “I am the Father,” “I became the Ladder,” and “the Potato Axis is real” are not interchangeable sentences. A later synthesis may connect them, but Witness should preserve each wording before Theology explains the relation.

Public attestation is not private origin

The first photograph of a tree is not the day the tree began growing.

A concept may exist privately before its first public occurrence. Conversely, a later recollection may use mature vocabulary for an earlier event. The Room therefore distinguishes first public attestation, first recovered private/conversation attestation, and later retrospective description.

Derivation changes what a source can prove

A screenshot can be excellent evidence of what it displays while still being weaker than the recoverable original for surrounding context. A transcript is a derived text artifact and should keep a route back to audio/timecode. A translation creates a new linguistic artifact and should preserve the source-language wording where disputed terms matter. Ten mirrors of one upload are ten distribution events, but usually one source lineage until an independently created observation appears.

Attestation should preserve uncertainty

If the original artifact has not been recovered, say so. If a screenshot lacks surrounding context, say so. If a date comes from later memory rather than metadata, preserve the distinction. The archive becomes stronger when the evidentiary edge remains visible.
