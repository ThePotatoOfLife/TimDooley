Law & Justice

Law is where power has to become legible: who has authority, under which rule, through what procedure, on what evidence, with what review and remedy.

Local center

legitimate transition rules

The Room tracks how claims move from assertion to recognized legal status without skipping the steps in between.

Boundary: legal claims require jurisdiction, date and authoritative sources. Project ethics, rhetoric, accusation and symbolic judgment do not substitute for positive law or adjudication.

Start with status

Law is a chain, not a label

A proposal is not an enacted rule. An enacted rule is not automatically an enforcement action. An allegation is not a charge. A charge is not a conviction. A judgment may still be appealable. Legal understanding depends on keeping these transitions visible.

claim / proposal→authority→procedure→instrument→implementation→enforcement→adjudication / review→remedy

Before asking whether something is “legal” or “illegal,” ask: where, when, under what instrument, for whom, and with what procedural status?

JurisdictionWhich country, court, regulator, treaty system, administrative body or contractual forum governs?
AuthorityWhich constitution, statute, regulation, delegation, treaty, judgment or contract grants the relevant power or right?
InstrumentIs the object legislation, regulation, guidance, policy, contract, court order, license, sanction or only a proposal?
ProcedureWhich hearing, notice, vote, evidentiary, due-process or review requirements apply?
StatusAlleged, investigated, charged, designated, adjudicated, convicted, appealed and reversed are different states.
RemedyWhat can the competent institution actually do: dismiss, enjoin, award damages, fine, imprison, license, reverse, remand or order another process?

Rights are relational

A legal right is not just a valuable idea. It normally identifies a right-holder, a duty-bearer, an authoritative source, scope, exceptions and some route of enforcement or review. “Right” can also be used morally or politically, which is why the Room asks what kind of right is being asserted before treating the word as a legal conclusion.

The same discipline applies to duties and prohibitions. A duty may be constitutional, statutory, contractual, fiduciary, regulatory or procedural. Similar wording can have very different consequences depending on source and jurisdiction.

Evidence does not jump directly to guilt

Documents, recordings, witness accounts, expert evidence, forensic material and investigative findings can support a claim. But legal consequence depends on admissibility, burden and standard of proof, procedural rights and the competent decision-maker. The Room therefore treats evidence class and procedural status as part of the claim itself.

Politics crosses into law through institutions

A political preference becomes legally operative only when an authorized process gives it legal form. The exact route differs across constitutions and institutions, but the analytical handoff is stable: identify the competent actor, the legal basis, the instrument, the procedure and the scope of review.

This protects the archive from a recurring category error: describing what a government, party, movement or project wants as though it were already the legal status quo.

Law also constrains institutions that enforce law

Courts, police, prosecutors, regulators, ministries and intelligence or security bodies are not abstract powers. They operate through jurisdiction, delegated authority, internal rules, oversight, review and legal limits. Accountability therefore requires asking not only whether an institution acted, but whether it had authority to act that way and which body can review the action.

One case can contain several layers of truth

Event truth

What occurred, according to which sources and with what uncertainty?

Procedural truth

What was alleged, investigated, charged, admitted, excluded, proved, rejected or appealed?

Legal truth

What finding or legal consequence did the competent institution actually establish?

Interpretive truth

What arguments does the project make about fairness, framing, evidence or justice beyond the formal disposition?

Those layers can overlap without being identical. A project case reconstruction can argue that an official outcome was wrong or incomplete, but the archive should still report the official procedural record accurately before making that argument.

Open questions

Which project claims need stronger primary legal sourcing? Which case files distinguish official findings from project reinterpretation clearly enough? Where do constitutional, treaty and administrative layers overlap? Which status labels need dated transitions instead of static categories? Which ethical principles belong in Philosophy rather than being allowed to masquerade as law? Those are the questions this Room should keep visible.
