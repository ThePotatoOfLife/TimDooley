Research Programmes

Where an interesting question stops being a note and becomes coordinated work: owners, sources, stages, tests, outputs and promotion gates.

A programme coordinates work; it does not steal ownership from the Rooms supplying facts. A research plan is not a result, and a result is not canon until it passes the relevant review.

The project has grown large enough that curiosity itself needs infrastructure. Without programmes, every new insight becomes another file, another branch or another half-finished idea. This Room exists to turn breadth into sequence.

A programme starts with a real deficit.

What is missing? A source? A quantitative model? A country layer? A historical genealogy? A reader surface? A validation rule? “Research more” is not yet a programme until the missing capability can be named.

Ownership comes before synthesis.

Politics facts stay with Politics. Biology stays with Biology. Legal status stays with Law. The programme may coordinate all three, but it should return each mature result to the correct owner instead of creating a giant research super-room.

Materialization matters.

The project now explicitly asks whether a clever model has become usable capability: does it have an owner, real objects or observations, interfaces, evidence boundaries, a reader surface, and validation? If not, it may still be only architecture.

Negative results belong here too.

A search that finds no evidence, a model that fails, or a comparison that collapses under better sources should remain visible. A research system becomes trustworthy when failure can reduce the system instead of merely causing another layer to be added.

The Forge is successful when fewer things remain vague—not when more things receive names.

A programme is a promise with receipts

Large questions deserve sequences that survive enthusiasm

“We should research this” is often the sentence a good idea says just before disappearing into thirty tabs. A programme begins when curiosity acquires a next action, an owner, a stopping condition and some memory of what failed.

The moral value of a research programme is not that it makes work look serious. It protects future attention from having to rediscover the same confusion. Good programmes turn scattered enthusiasm into cumulative capability.

PromiseWhat deficit are we actually trying to remove?

Name the missing source, model, route, dataset, reader surface or capability.

TrailWhat did the work leave behind?

Sources, commits, negative results, diagrams, tests and revised assumptions should outlive the mood that began the project.

GateWhat would count as enough?

Without a promotion or stopping rule, research can become permanent motion mistaken for progress.

ReturnWhere does the result go when it matures?

Science gets the model, Archive gets the source, World gets the system fact, Canon gets the internal meaning. The Lab should not hoard adulthood.

A programme is finished when the House can do something tomorrow that it could not reliably do yesterday.

A programme should leave a trail of things, not only intentions

For each programme the page should be able to point to input deficit → source corpus → commits/artifacts → failed assumptions → next gate. “Research religion” is not a programme. “Build source-sensitive lineage readers for five underrepresented traditions and validate their routes” is.

Current work is already multi-scale.

Recent programmes include project-wide materialization audits, commit-depth/crystallization work, North/world-system synthesis, scientific formalization and the continuing effort to turn the House's deeper research into reader-facing Rooms. This richness programme itself now belongs to that lineage.
