Neurobiology

The literal nervous system first: nuclei, cells, tracts, circuits, blood, fluid, development and evidence grade. Symbolic anatomy comes later.

Thalamus ↔ House, pineal ↔ Eye/Door, CSF ↔ River and spine ↔ Ladder are project comparisons. They are not accepted anatomical identities and must never overwrite the literal biology.

This Room exists because “the brain” is too coarse. A thalamic nucleus is not the same kind of object as a cortical layer; a cranial nerve is not a vertebra; cerebrospinal fluid is not a mystical channel; and a symbolic resemblance becomes useful only after the anatomy has been described correctly.

Thalamus: relay is an incomplete description.

The thalamus contains many nuclei with different cortical and subcortical partners. Sensory routing, motor loops, attention, arousal and recurrent thalamo-cortical dynamics make it a family of circuits rather than a passive switchboard. That complexity is why the project's “House with rooms” comparison is interesting—but it remains a comparison.

Pineal: endocrine tissue, not a proven third eye.

The pineal gland is best grounded in melatonin secretion and circadian timing. Historical and esoteric “third eye” traditions belong in comparative rooms. This Room records where those traditions touch anatomy and where the evidence stops.

CSF is a living fluid system.

Cerebrospinal fluid is produced, circulates through ventricles and subarachnoid spaces, exchanges with tissue/interstitial systems and participates in mechanical, chemical and clearance environments. The adult spinal central canal should not be drawn as a giant continuously open CSF pipe.

The spine needs two coordinate systems.

Vertebral level and spinal-cord segment are related but not interchangeable. The project now carries a 33-room vertebral atlas alongside cord laminae, roots, tracts and brainstem/cranial systems precisely to prevent symbolic “33 steps” language from flattening anatomy.

Three pathways make “House” more precise

Body sensation → VPL / face → VPM.

The ventral posterolateral thalamic nucleus receives major somatosensory input from the body through spinothalamic and dorsal-column pathways and projects toward primary somatosensory cortex. The ventral posteromedial nucleus performs a neighboring relay for facial/trigeminal sensation and taste-related input. “Thalamus = House” therefore works better as a collection of differentiated rooms with different doors than as one undifferentiated control chamber.

Retina → optic tract → LGN → visual cortex.

The lateral geniculate nucleus is a specific visual projection nucleus. Retinal signals pass through optic nerve/chiasm/tract before reaching it, and thalamic processing is embedded in extensive feedback rather than simple one-way delivery. This gives “Door” and “Room” a concrete sensory-routing example without making the whole thalamus an Eye.

Choroid plexus → ventricles → subarachnoid spaces → outflow.

Adult CSF volume is roughly 150 mL, while production is roughly 400–600 mL/day, so the fluid is renewed several times daily. Most production comes from choroid plexus epithelium; the fluid passes through ventricles into subarachnoid spaces and ultimately leaves through several routes including meningeal lymphatic pathways. This is a real circulating/renewing fluid system—but its routes are anatomically specific, not a single sacred spinal river.

The metaphor should become more anatomical as it gets stronger

If House means differentiated routing, name the nucleus and pathway. If River means CSF, distinguish production, compartment, exchange and outflow. If Ladder means spinal transmission, distinguish vertebra, cord segment, root, tract and peripheral nerve. Precision should make the symbolism narrower and better—not give it permission to claim the whole body.

The richer the anatomy becomes, the better the symbolism can become—because a metaphor no longer has to survive by making the biology vague.

Evidence is part of the anatomy.

Human evidence, animal findings, computational models, historical speculation and project symbolism are separately graded. This is not bureaucracy: it is what makes the Room usable for both serious neuroscience and comparative imagination.
