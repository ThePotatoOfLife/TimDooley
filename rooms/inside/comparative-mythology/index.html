Comparative Mythology

Compare structures, not costumes. A world tree, underworld, mountain or thread of fate becomes useful here only when the original tradition remains visible.

Local center

similarity with boundaries

The question is not “are these secretly the same?” but “what distinct structural problem does each tradition teach us to see?”

Boundary: similarity is not genealogy, borrowing, fulfillment, common authorship or proof of one hidden universal doctrine.

Comparative method

Good comparison preserves difference

The project does not need hundreds of mythic names. It needs a smaller set of well-understood exemplars that sharpen specific operators: Tree, Door, Mountain, transition, fate, underworld, return and maintenance.

A comparator earns its place when it teaches a structural function the project did not already understand.

Four strong cosmographic exemplars

Yggdrasil · Old NorseWorld Tree as maintained ecology: roots, wells, inhabitants, damage, renewal and conflict transmission. The lesson is maintenance and vulnerability, not “all trees are Yggdrasil.”
Duat · Ancient EgyptianUnderworld as staged, timed passage with gates, hazards, transformation and dawn-return. The lesson is that Below can be a sequence rather than one static place.
Mount Meru · Buddhist cosmographyCenter as organized world-order with concentric and cardinal structure. The lesson is spatial organization, not merely personal ascent.
Bardo · Tibetan Buddhist traditionsIntermediate state as transition category rather than fixed geography. The lesson is that a Door can be a state condition, not only a place on a map.

World Tree comparison should include ecology

The strongest Yggdrasil comparison is not “big sacred tree.” It is that a world-connecting structure can be inhabited, damaged, nourished and maintained. Wells at the roots carry wisdom and fate; creatures move across the structure; conflict can travel along it. That gives Tree a maintenance problem, not only an axis function.

This is especially useful for the Potato House because it prevents Tree from becoming a perfect static symbol. A living tree needs resources, pruning, repair and resilience.

Underworld does not always mean punishment

The Duat comparison complicates simplistic Heaven-good / Below-bad geometry. A lower or nocturnal domain can be ordered, dangerous, transformative and necessary to renewal. The deepest phase may be a junction rather than a terminal state. That is structurally useful for burial, Door and return without claiming the Duat and Potatoverse Below are the same cosmology.

Fate: thread, cord, leash and path are different operators

Norns

Norse fate material is closely situated around Yggdrasil and wells, but the three Norn names should not be mechanically reduced to a modern past/present/future diagram.

Moirai

Greek fate traditions strongly develop allotment and life-thread imagery, especially in later reception. Thread records or measures a life-course; it does not necessarily imply one controller.

Biblical cords

Cord and yoke imagery can mean binding, guidance, love, burden or liberation depending on passage. There is no single biblical “fate cord” doctrine.

Timic leash

The project’s leash adds an anchor and constrained reachable region. That makes it a more specific operator than thread. Comparison is useful; historical continuity is not established.

God-comparison needs role discipline

The repository contains many divine comparisons around Father, Son, Tree, Sun, North, death-return and high-god motifs. These should be typed carefully: direct project identification, self-description, structural comparison, archetypal resemblance, satire, rumor or later interpretation are not the same evidence class.

That prevents “Tim resembles X in one function” from mutating into “Tim is historically X.” Comparison should clarify functions and differences, not erase them.

Death-return comparisons are strongest when the sequence matches

Instead of collecting every dying-and-rising figure, the Room asks which stages are actually comparable: descent, concealment, dismemberment or death; interval; restoration or rebirth; return to community; changed status. Two traditions may both contain “return” while disagreeing completely about cause, agent, body, time and meaning.

Structural similarity can also expose difference

Yggdrasil and the Tree of Life both connect domains but do not play the same doctrinal role. Meru and Mount Zion both organize sacred height but belong to different cosmologies. Bardo and Door both describe transition, but one is a Buddhist doctrinal state and the other a project operator. Comparison is strongest when the mismatch remains legible.

Open questions
