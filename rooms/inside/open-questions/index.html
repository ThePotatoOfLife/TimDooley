Open Questions & Hypotheses

The archive is allowed to say “we do not know yet.” This Room protects unresolved questions from being prematurely converted into doctrine, biography, science or historical fact.

Local center

uncertainty with memory

A question should remain findable even before it has an answer.

Indexing a hypothesis does not promote it to canon. A hypothesis can be useful, plausible or structurally elegant while remaining unverified, under-sourced or explicitly speculative.

The FAQ question bank is larger than the answered FAQ on purpose. It preserves the frontier: questions about identity, theology, symbols, body mappings, history, science, archive evidence, creative works and project development can exist before the repository has earned a concise answer.

Some questions are source-recovery problems.

“What did this phrase originally mean?” may not need a new theory at all; it may need an older conversation, screenshot, stream segment or artifact. The right next action is recovery, not interpretation.

Some questions are distinction problems.

“Is this symbol literal, comparative, scientific, theological or creative?” can often be answered by separating categories rather than choosing one grand interpretation. Many archive errors begin when neighboring meanings collapse into identity.

Some questions are model problems.

A hypothesis becomes researchable when it can be translated into states, mechanisms, variables, observations or counterexamples. If no possible outcome could change the answer, the question may belong to theology or philosophy rather than empirical testing.

An unanswered question is not a hole if the archive knows exactly why it is still open.

Promotion requires an owner.

The question bank's rule is simple: move a question into the answer atlas only when a canonical owner can support a concise answer and an evidence class. Demand alone does not create authority.

Contradictions are research inputs.

When old and new formulations disagree, the first task is to date and classify them. The disagreement may reveal genuine doctrinal change, different literary modes, a corrected factual claim or an unresolved conflict. Flattening it destroys information.

Four useful statuses

Recover: missing source or wording. Clarify: categories or definitions are conflated. Test: a model can meet observation. Contemplate: a philosophical or theological question remains meaningful without pretending empirical closure.
