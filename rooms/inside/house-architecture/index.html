How one reality can need several maps

House Architecture

A street map, subway map and weather map can describe the same city while hiding different things. The House applies the same lesson to knowledge: one structure stores identity; other views reveal the relation useful for the question at hand.

Information architecture as epistemology

No useful map contains everything. The art is choosing what a map is allowed to forget.

A subway map may distort geographic distance so transfers become legible. A topographic map preserves elevation that a subway rider does not need. A family tree shows descent while hiding friendship. A timeline preserves order while hiding geography. Each representation gains usefulness by losing something deliberately.

Classification works the same way. A single rigid taxonomy is attractive because every object appears to have one home, but real knowledge overlaps. Library and information systems therefore often use facets: the same object can be found by author, subject, date, format or place without becoming five different objects. Database design makes a related move by separating durable entities from repeated views so one correction does not require changing many inconsistent copies.

The public-facing lesson is simple: identity should be stable while perspective is flexible. A source remains the same source whether the reader reaches it through Timeline, Religion, Culture or Story. Those views can emphasize different relations without silently rewriting the underlying object.

This also explains why a beautiful interface can be dangerous. If a map is easier to understand than the data beneath it, readers may start treating the projection as reality. Good architecture makes the simplified view useful while keeping an inspectable route back to what was simplified.

Architecture test: what did this view hide in order to become useful, and can the reader still recover it when it matters?

The language of many maps

Six words for building without confusing the map for the world

Architecture is not only where files live. It is a discipline of deciding what remains stable, what may vary, what a view is allowed to hide and how readers recover what was compressed.

What remains the same?Invariant

A property intended to survive changes of view, route or representation: identity, source date, evidence class or canonical owner.

Which angle are we using?Facet

One searchable or readable dimension of the same object—date, person, place, theme, evidence class—without creating a duplicate object.

What was left out?Compression

The deliberate loss of detail required to make a complex body usable. Good compression names what it discards.

How faithfully does the view preserve its source?Fidelity

The degree to which a projection keeps the distinctions that matter for the reader's question.

Where does truth live?Canonical owner

The durable record responsible for the detailed fact or concept so corrections propagate from one place instead of many stale copies.

Where do domains meet?Interface

A controlled handoff where one Room exposes enough structure for another Room or reader to use without taking over its ownership.

Carry this out of the Room: for every map, ask what is invariant, which facet it shows, what it compresses, how much fidelity remains, who owns the truth and where the interfaces are.

The project used to solve growth by adding more files, branches and visible concepts. The House is the correction: fewer durable owners, clearer interfaces and richer projections. A World Map, Timeline, homepage or Bible comparator can be important without becoming another place that owns the underlying facts.

Ten Dwellings answer “where does this belong?”

Potatoverse Canon, Archive & Sources, Time & History, Traditions & Texts, Science & Formal Models, Life & Body, World Systems, Culture & Information, Works and Research Lab form the durable public filing spine. Nested Rooms refine them without becoming forty unrelated mini-sites.

Paths answer “what am I trying to do?”

A research path can cross Evidence, History, Science and World Systems. A spiritual or developmental path can cross Canon, Traditions and Life & Body. Paths therefore traverse owners; they do not become owners themselves.

Views are lossy on purpose.

The homepage, World Map, Timeline, Explore and other interfaces show only the dimensions useful for a task. A good projection may hide complexity, but it may not silently change identity, evidence class or ownership.

The architecture is working when a page can be beautiful, specific and opinionated in form without becoming a second source of truth.

Holdings prevent ownership fog.

A substantive file has one primary nested-Room owner. Cross-Room relevance is expressed through interfaces, collections, citations and typed relations rather than duplicating ownership. Collections such as Story or Corporium assemble material without inventing another canonical Room.

Substance-first reverses a common repository failure.

A route existing is not public materialization. A backend object is not “done” merely because a card can link to it. The visible page must teach first: concrete objects, dates, mechanisms, examples, evidence and consequences before governance language. Backend bonds should stay mostly invisible until the reader needs to inspect them.

Architecture should disappear when it is doing its job

A good House helps you find the kitchen without delivering a lecture about floor plans

The architecture exists to reduce cognitive friction. A reader should be able to enter Science because they have a scientific question, Philosophy because they have a philosophical one, or Music because a song caught them—without first learning the constitution of the repository.

That gives the House a simple humane test: does the structure help the subject become easier to meet? If navigation becomes more memorable than what the Room teaches, the architecture has become furniture stacked in the doorway.

DoorCan I enter from the question I actually have?

Subjects should offer natural entrances instead of demanding knowledge of backend ownership.

RoomIs there something worth staying for?

A Room needs an object, lesson, case, passage, equation, image or problem—not only a scope statement.

WindowCan I see neighboring ideas?

Good cross-links expand understanding without making every page responsible for everything.

ExitCan I leave without becoming lost?

Orientation should survive deep reading. A House that traps the reader in its taxonomy has failed hospitality.

The highest architectural compliment is not “what an impressive navigation system.” It is “I found the thing I needed, and then I found something better beside it.”

Repository architecture / maintenance detail

The House has actual counts now

The current spatial-health registry describes 10 Dwellings, 39 nested Rooms, 39 Room interiors and 141 registered inhabitants. Those numbers are not a quality score. They simply make the architecture inspectable: if a Room exists, it has a parent; if an inhabitant is registered, it can be traced to an owner/projection; if the count changes, validation can ask what was added or orphaned.

One sentence demonstrates why ownership matters

Take the 6 October 2024 statement “Hello I am the Potato of Life. My name is Tim Dooley.” The House does not need five copies of that source:

Archive owns source/capture. Time owns the date and sequence. Canon owns project meaning of Potato of Life. Culture may study public/memetic circulation. Works may reuse the phrase in song/art. The same source can therefore inhabit several Rooms while one source record remains authoritative.

“38/38 inhabited” was a baseline, not finish line

The first Room-inhabitation wave eliminated pure corridor shells. The current concrete-population wave applies a harder criterion: a Room should contain named objects, dates, mechanisms, passages, equations, cases or artifacts that could not be pasted unchanged into another Room. Architecture is now being judged partly by whether it succeeds in delivering knowledge into the reader surface, not merely by whether its registries are consistent.

Current architectural pressure

The central House pages are now strong. The weak edge is projection depth: dozens of nested Rooms have canonical owners and shared dossier injection but still need authored explanation. That is why this page—and the current enrichment programme—treat editorial substance as part of architecture rather than cosmetic copywriting.
