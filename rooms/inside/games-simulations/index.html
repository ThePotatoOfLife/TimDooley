Games & Simulations

A simulation forces an idea to pay rent. Once growth, inheritance, scarcity, thresholds, agents or attention become rules, the project can watch what those rules actually produce.

Local center

make the rule run

Play exposes consequences that prose can hide.

Simulation behavior is model output, not automatically a claim about the real world. A game can reveal the consequences of its own rules; external validity requires separate evidence.

The creative archive contains more than jokes with buttons. Several game ideas are miniature laboratories for recurring project questions: what does selection do across generations, what happens when time is compressed, how do audiences change a system by watching it, and when does a Door event create a genuinely new state?

Potato of Life Growth Game.

The tamagotchi-like growth concept combines watering, growth time, size, yield, genetics, mutations, money, research, weather, seasons, family trees and rare events. Its real analytical value is iteration: environment and inherited traits interact, selection has tradeoffs, and one generation becomes the starting condition of the next.

Self-Playing AI Potato Town.

A top-down autonomous town places Tim/God on a couch, Son at the Door and agents inside accelerated seasons where a minute can represent roughly a month. The game turns observer-versus-participant, compressed time, emergent narrative and threshold events into something visible.

Tim Dooley Trading Game.

The browser-market concept uses tickers such as oil, gold, silver and Bitcoin inside a market-style interface. It is not a data authority; it is a way to model price signals, attention, feedback and the stories people wrap around changing numbers.

A good simulation does not prove the worldview. It reveals what the worldview's rules would do if you let them move.

Dog House game-show satire.

The deliberately fictional surveillance/game-show premise uses voting, scarcity, spectacle and audience distance to make internet incentive systems grotesquely obvious. Its value is cultural critique, not real-world instruction.

Potato School turns learning into threshold play.

The recovered concept mixes cultivation, Doors, passwords, keys, puzzles and absurd initiation satire. Its best insight is that learning can be embodied as a quest while simultaneously mocking prestige and credential gatekeeping.

What belongs here next

Each surviving project should eventually expose its rules, version history, playable/prototype state, screenshots or artifacts, and the specific relation it is meant to test. “Game idea” is not enough once a system becomes important to project history.
