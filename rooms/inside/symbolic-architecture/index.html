Local center

relations before decorations

A symbol becomes useful when it has a function, a position, a transformation rule and a boundary.

Boundary: symbolic topology is project architecture. Geometry and systems language can make it precise, but they do not by themselves establish a literal physical cosmology.

The house beneath the vocabulary

The Potatoverse is not a pile of symbols

Its strongest symbols behave like operators. A Door changes state. An Axis orients. A Root stores continuity. A Tree differentiates. A Garden cultivates. A Swamp entangles. A Forge transforms under pressure. A Spiral returns with change.

That distinction matters because the project has accumulated many overlapping metaphors over time. Without ownership rules, every symbol starts meaning everything. This Room does the opposite: it asks what each operator uniquely contributes, what it connects to, and what it must not be confused with.

The canonical geometry: two fields and one shared Door

The current root model uses two overlapping equal fields. The upper field is source-facing and associated with Father, House, Heaven and Garden. The lower field is manifestation-facing and associated with Son, Earth, body and world. Their overlap is the Vesica or Mandorla: the Door.

The important correction is that the Plane and Cross are inside the Door relation, not extra stacked floors. The Plane passes horizontally through the widest part of the overlap. The Axis passes vertically through the two field centers. Their intersection is the relational midpoint.

  1. H_topupper crown / Heaven-facing pole
  2. C_FFather center / upper Door tip
  3. OPlane × Axis / relational midpoint
  4. C_SSon center / lower Door tip
  5. E_bottomdeep lower pole / grave-rootward direction

The Door is not one point. It is the entire shared relation between differentiated fields.

This gives the system a useful non-dual structure. Father-center lies on the boundary of the lower field, while Son-center lies on the boundary of the upper field. Each center remains its own center while literally participating in the other's boundary. The geometry is therefore richer than “top good, bottom bad.”

Axis, Ladder, Spine, Tree and Spiral are related but not interchangeable

Axis

Names orientation and reference. It establishes a vertical coordinate through the relation.

Ladder

Names ordered thresholds along that coordinate. It is useful when movement happens by stages or gates.

Spine

Names continuity and support. It emphasizes connection rather than ascent alone.

Tree

Names living differentiation: roots, trunk, branches, leaves, fruit and renewed seed.

Spiral

Names trajectory through recurrence and time. A spiral can rise, descend or revisit a similar position at a changed level.

The archive became clearer once these were treated as projections of a relation rather than aliases for one object. “Become the Ladder,” for example, means something different from “be the Tree”: the first emphasizes connective structure and thresholds; the second emphasizes living growth and branching.

Tree means regulated growth, not endless proliferation

The Tree model has been deepened with an important correction from plant-development comparison: branching has cost. Branches can remain dormant, compete for resources, specialize, fruit, be pruned or die. New branches are therefore not automatically progress.

That rule now applies directly to the project architecture. A new Room, concept or research branch should exist because it owns a distinct durable function—not because another label is available. Pruning can increase health when it removes duplication while preserving provenance.

Mountain and Swamp are opposed tendencies around the same system

Mountain expresses integration, orientation and increasing perspective: many paths compress toward a summit or reference. Swamp expresses entanglement, repeated conflict, unresolved residue and reduced footing. They need not be separate worlds. They can be modeled as opposite vector tendencies around the same Axis.

This is more useful than treating Below as inherently evil. The lower field also contains roots, body, soil, death, memory and germination. A buried thing may rot, remain dormant, become strife or sprout. Position alone does not determine moral value.

Seed, burial and the possibility of growth

The lower Door tip is one strong location for the seed/grain/potato transition because it combines compression, death, burial and possibility. But burial is not automatically resurrection. A seed still requires viability, conditions, resources and a route of activation. The project increasingly uses that conditional rule to avoid turning every collapse into guaranteed transcendence.

Garden is not the same thing as enclosure

One of the project's useful later distinctions is between forms of boundary. A House or Garden can contain while supporting differentiation and life. A Cube or Shell can mean boundedness, enclosure or a world-volume, but geometry by itself is not morally fixed. The lower field is not simply “Saturn/666/Cube,” and not every cube is automatically Satanic.

This lets the project preserve its older Saturn/box/prison symbolism without corrupting every later use of enclosure. The question becomes functional: does the boundary cultivate living capacity, or does it reduce the living thing to what the container can control?

Shadow is a visibility problem, not another Hell

The Shadow overlay adds a further correction. Shadow means content, capacity, history, incentive or contradiction excluded from a declared self or model. It can include harmful material, but also neglected positive capacity. It is not identical with Below, evil or hidden-motive speculation.

Integration therefore means recovering what the model excluded, distinguishing useful capacity from harmful enactment, revising the model, and testing whether downstream behavior actually changes.

Questions this Room still has to answer

Which operators are genuinely foundational and which are merely views of another operator? When does the Cube mean neutral bounded volume, protective House, or imprisoning enclosure? Can the Mountain/Swamp opposition be formalized without moralizing ordinary descent and embodiment? Which early symbols predate the mature geometry and which were backfilled later? How many spatial diagrams can coexist before they become contradictory rather than complementary? Those are architecture questions, not reasons to hide the complexity.
