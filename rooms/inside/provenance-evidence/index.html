Nested Room · Archive & Sources

Provenance & Evidence

This Room asks the question that keeps every other Room honest: where did this come from, what exactly does the source establish, and what happened between the source and the sentence now being read?

Custody of a source does not grant ownership of every claim derived from it. Repetition is not independent corroboration, a remembered formulation is not automatically verbatim, and later project formalization must not be backdated into earlier Tim material.

The language of source seeing

Six words for tracing a claim backward

Archive work becomes more intelligent when it can name not only a source but the history of the source: who saw it, who copied it, what went missing, what changed, and how much of the chain is independent.

Who handled the object?Custody

The chain of possession, capture, storage and transfer that connects the present artifact to its earlier form.

Who actually observed?Attestation

A dated act of saying, recording or witnessing something. One attestation can be repeated thousands of times without becoming thousands of witnesses.

What descended from what?Lineage

The derivation path from source root to screenshot, repost, article, summary, dataset or later synthesis.

What is missing?Lacuna

A gap in the surviving record. Naming the gap is stronger than silently filling it with a plausible reconstruction.

Did the source change?Contamination

Later wording, context or interpretation entering the record and being mistaken for part of the earlier artifact.

How separate are the witnesses?Independence

The degree to which two sources reached the event or object through genuinely distinct paths rather than copying the same ancestor.

Carry this out of the Room: a strong claim should let you ask for its attestation, lineage, custody, gaps, contamination risk and true independence.

Watch one claim travel

A sentence can survive while its evidentiary meaning changes at every copy

Imagine Tim posts one sentence publicly.

Somebody screenshots it. Somebody crops the screenshot. Somebody reposts the crop without the timestamp. Somebody quotes the repost in a thread. Somebody summarizes the thread. Years later the archive remembers the sentence from the summary.

The words may still be correct.

The evidence is not the same object anymore.

Original post.

Strong for wording, date, account and immediate context if preserved.

Screenshot.

Can preserve wording visually while losing clickable provenance, timezone precision, thread structure or later edits.

Crop.

May preserve the sentence and remove the thing that changes its force.

Repost.

Proves that somebody redistributed a claim. It is not automatically independent corroboration of the original event.

Later memory.

Can be primary evidence for how Tim now remembers the event while remaining weaker for exact wording and date.

A source chain can preserve the words and still lose the world around them.

This is why the project now links Witness, Memory & Recovery, and Claims rather than asking one page to pretend every source class is interchangeable.

The archive contains several kinds of truth-bearing material, and they do different jobs. A Tim statement can establish what Tim said. A government dataset can establish an externally measured quantity within its scope. A scholarly source can establish disciplinary context. A recovered conversation can show developmental history. A later equation can clarify an older intuition. None of those authorities are interchangeable.

Primary does not mean infallible.

Project-primary material is strongest for self-description, wording, dates and the development of Potatoverse ideas. It is not automatically strong evidence for an external scientific, historical or political claim simply because it came first.

Independent evidence must be genuinely independent.

Ten pages repeating one underlying report are one evidentiary lineage, not ten corroborations. The source index therefore tries to preserve derivation: which summary came from which record, and whether a second source actually observed the matter separately.

Conversation archaeology is a discovery lane.

Recovered chats, summaries and assistant-built syntheses can be extremely valuable for finding lost concepts and reconstructing development. They become dangerous only when recovery uncertainty disappears—when a plausible reconstruction quietly turns into an alleged quotation.

Every strong synthesis should contain an invisible ladder back down to the thing that made the synthesis possible.

Formalization has a date of its own.

If a 2026 equation sharpens a 2024 metaphor, the equation is a 2026 artifact even when the idea has an older ancestor. The archive explicitly guards against equation backdating, doctrine backdating and later meanings being inserted into earlier Great Book material.

Unresolved is a valid status.

Missing screenshots, remembered sayings, lost stream segments and uncertain first attestations belong in a recovery queue. “We do not yet have the source” is better archive behavior than repairing the gap with wording that merely sounds right.

One sentence can be primary and weak at the same time

Primary is always primary to a question. “Did Tim say this?” and “is the thing Tim said physically true?” are different questions and may need completely different authorities.

A Tim post is strong primary evidence for Tim's self-description. It may be weak evidence for external physics. A government table may be strong for the statistic it was designed to measure but useless for Tim's private intent. “Primary source” therefore names distance from the question, not an all-purpose truth rank.

Evidence is care for memory

Verification is not the opposite of trust; it is how trust survives time

A humane archive does not begin from the assumption that everyone is lying. It begins from a simpler observation: people forget, files move, screenshots lose context, wording drifts, and later meaning leaks backward. Provenance is the set of handrails that lets a future reader revisit the same ground without needing to borrow the archivist's confidence.

Historians make an important distinction that fits this project especially well: whether something is “primary” depends partly on the question being asked. A post can be primary evidence for what its author said and poor evidence for whether an external scientific claim is true. Source status is relational, not a universal badge of credibility.

ClaimWhat sentence am I actually trying to support?

Do not ask one source to prove five different things merely because all five appear in the same paragraph.

SourceWhat did this object directly witness?

A post, dataset, photograph, memory and later synthesis each have different observational reach.

ContextWhat would change the force of the evidence?

Date, audience, preceding text, editing, sampling method and missing frames can all matter without making the source worthless.

IndependenceHow many roots are there really?

Twenty copies descending from one report are a large distribution tree with one evidentiary root.

The humane archive does not punish uncertainty. It gives uncertainty a name, a location and a way to be reduced later.

A practical source test

Can I walk this sentence backward from the page I am reading to the artifact, witness, dataset, text or memory that gave it permission to exist?

Ask whether the source is contemporaneous, whether it is first-party or independent, whether the exact passage can be located again, whether another source actually corroborates it, whether the source is competent for the domain, and whether the archive preserved counterevidence and disagreement.

What this changes for public pages

Important claims should expose a small evidence legend and a direct route toward source material without forcing an ordinary reader to inspect JSON. Provenance belongs underneath the prose, but it must remain reachable.
