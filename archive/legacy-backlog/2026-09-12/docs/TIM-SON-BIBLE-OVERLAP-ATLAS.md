# Tim Dooley / Son ↔ Bible Overlap Atlas

## What overlaps, where, when, how strongly, and what it actually means

This page is the reader-facing entrance to the project’s biblical-overlap system.

The goal is not to impress the reader with a huge pile of verses. It is to make every claimed overlap **inspectable**.

At the current 2026-09-11 snapshot, the canonical census contains **78 overlap nodes**.

That means 78 analytically distinct relations between documented Tim/Son project material and biblical texts. It does **not** mean 78 fulfilled prophecies, 78 statistically independent coincidences, or 78 proofs that Tim or the Son is supernaturally identical with a biblical figure.

The census is intentionally modular and auditable:

- Base census: 74 nodes.
- Wave-14 addendum: 4 newly promoted nodes.
- Current total: **78**.

Strength distribution:

| Strength | Nodes | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| 5 | 49 | direct scriptural use or unusually dense structural correspondence with good provenance and explicit differences |
| 4 | 15 | strong comparison with important chronology, role or source qualification |
| 3 | 13 | meaningful but substantially interpretive comparison |
| 2 | 1 | weak/contested symbolic or numerical comparison retained mainly for provenance |
| 1 | 0 | speculative material normally excluded from the canonical census |

These numbers describe the **registry**, not probability of a theological claim.

# 1. How to read an overlap

Every serious overlap should answer at least nine questions.

## Who?

Is the project-side actor:

- Tim / Father-side,
- the Son,
- a Tim–Son shared relation,
- the Great Book/project system,
- or a later archive comparator?

This matters because the Bible also differentiates roles. A Father-role comparison cannot silently become a Son-role comparison simply because both occur in one project diagram.

## What?

What exact Tim/Son wording, event, image or structure is being compared?

A good record does not merely say “Tim resembles Revelation.” It says something like:

**2026-04-09:** one guardian at the right, one at the left, a narrow central passage toward Heaven, Jesus at/between the passage, Father above.

That can then be compared with specific biblical structures instead of a vague religious atmosphere.

## When?

There are often **two dates**, not one:

1. **Project date** — when Tim/Son actually used the motif.
2. **Discovery date** — when the archive later noticed a biblical correspondence.

This is essential for preventing hindsight bias.

For example, the April 9 paired-guardian architecture existed on the project side before the archive later compared it with Genesis 3, sanctuary cherubim and Hebrews.

## Where in the Bible?

The record should name the passage, not merely the motif.

“Ladder” is weak information.

**Genesis 28:10–22 → John 1:51** is much stronger because it lets the reader inspect the heaven-earth passage, House/Gate language and later Son-of-Man reuse.

## How?

What type of relation exists?

The archive now distinguishes at least:

- explicit biblical quotation/use,
- near-direct biblical phrase,
- Tim-led discovery,
- project motif first / biblical comparison later,
- later structural parallel,
- role/function correspondence,
- sequence correspondence,
- typological comparison,
- countertext,
- contextual synchronism,
- reception-history comparison,
- weak lexical/numerical resemblance.

## In which theological respect?

The seven operator categories are:

**PRESENCE — ACCESS — MEDIATION — TRANSFORMATION — PARTICIPATION — FRUIT — RESTORATION.**

These tell the reader what the overlap *does*.

## How strong?

Strength 5 does not mean “95% likely to be prophecy.” It means the relation is unusually useful for comparison because the source, role or multi-step structure is strong.

## What differs?

Every identity-sensitive strong comparison should show at least one mismatch.

Differences are not embarrassing footnotes. They are what stop the project from becoming unfalsifiable pattern matching.

## Where can I verify it?

Each census node routes to a canonical owner containing the underlying chronology, source, biblical analysis or countertext.

So the correct conclusion is **dense structural correspondence**, not fulfillment.

# 7. Shepherd theology fills an important gap

The archive had Dog, Farmer, Cow, Gardener, King and flock-like social imagery, but had not sufficiently developed biblical **Shepherd** theology.

Ezekiel 34 changes that.

Bad shepherds:

- feed themselves,
- fail to strengthen the weak,
- fail to heal the sick/injured,
- fail to seek the lost,
- let the flock scatter,
- allow shared pasture/water to be trampled or muddied.

The restoration answer is rescue, gathering, feeding, protection and a Davidic shepherd.

This gives the project one of its best leadership tests:

> **A ruler/shepherd is evaluated by the condition of the vulnerable flock, not the grandeur of the shepherd’s title.**

John 21 sharpens the same point:

**love → feed/tend the sheep.**

This should now sit beside Father-as-Gardener and servant-greatness as a principal test of divine/kingship claims.

# 9. Noah’s Ark and the Ark of the Covenant must not be flattened

This is a small linguistic correction with large consequences.

English uses “ark” for both Noah’s vessel and the Ark of the Covenant, but the Hebrew terms are different.

Noah’s vessel / Moses’ basket uses **teivah/tebah**.

The Covenant Ark uses **aron**.

Their theological functions are also different:

### Noah-type vessel

preservation → catastrophe → carried life/seed → survival → renewed world.

### Covenant Ark

covenant/testimony → sanctuary → cherubim/atonement-cover → sacred presence/meeting.

Later Jewish reception can compare these structures, but the archive should not manufacture lexical identity from an English translation coincidence.

This becomes a general rule:

> **Always check whether an apparent biblical word-coupling survives the original-language distinction.**

# 12. What was still missing from the project?

The project had already gathered a remarkable amount of material. What it lacked was mostly **epistemic architecture**.

## A. No stable denominator

Saying “there are hundreds of parallels” is rhetorically impressive but analytically weak.

Now we have a precise current registry:

**78 canonical overlap nodes.**

Future discoveries should add explicit census segments so a reader can see exactly how the number grew.

## B. Verse count and overlap count were being mixed

One overlap can cite six verses. Six verses can also be one repeated idea.

The project now separates:

- overlap-node count,
- unique reference count,
- unique book count,
- project-attestation count,
- high-density-passage count,
- countertext count.

## C. Project date and discovery date were being mixed

This is perhaps the biggest problem in retrospective theology.

A later discovery can be genuinely interesting without becoming evidence that Tim consciously knew the verse years earlier.

The archive now prefers two-date provenance.

## D. Density was not distinguished from independence

Psalm 80 can have enormous motif density. But if Tim adopted all of those motifs from Psalm 80 first, the later correspondence would not be independent.

The measurement protocol therefore tracks **temporal independence and genealogical dependence**.

## E. Countertexts were under-counted

A serious comparative theology must track where the Bible does *not* fit.

Examples already retained include:

- Jesus’ enemy-love versus Son-side reciprocal rhetoric,
- Father/Gardener vs Jesus/Vine role distribution in John 15,
- builder/House/Son distinctions in Hebrews 3,
- Deuteronomy’s king-under-law constraint,
- Philippians’ non-grasping service before exaltation,
- Ezekiel 34’s condemnation of self-feeding shepherds.

A countertext does not destroy the project. It tells us what kind of project theology can survive the biblical comparison.

## F. Original-language false friends needed more attention

The Noah-Ark / Covenant-Ark distinction shows why this matters.

Original-language checks should be routine for especially consequential word correspondences.

## G. Reception history needs its own layer

The Bible, later Jewish interpretation, patristic Christianity, medieval mysticism, Kabbalah, modern esotericism and Potatoverse synthesis must not be presented as though they are one historical source.

They can be compared. Their genealogy must remain visible.

## H. We needed reader navigation by question

The reader should be able to ask:

- What are the strongest Son overlaps?
- Which overlaps were explicit before 2026?
- Which are only later research discoveries?
- What did Tim actually say on April 9?
- Where does the Bible itself call the Father a Gardener?
- Which passages contradict or constrain Tim’s theology?
- Which single Bible passages have the highest motif density?
- How many current overlap nodes involve Access?
- Which project motifs predate their biblical comparison?
- What is the difference between a typology and a claimed fulfillment?

The census and measurement protocol now make those queries possible.

# 13. The best way to understand the whole archive

The reader should move through four layers.

### Layer 1 — Dated evidence

What Tim/Son actually said, wrote, experienced or later claimed, and when.

### Layer 2 — Biblical text

What the passage says in its own literary/historical context.

### Layer 3 — Relation

What is shared: function, sequence, role, symbol, ethical test, access structure, transformation, or narrative form.

### Layer 4 — Interpretation and limitation

What the correspondence might illuminate—and what it does **not** establish.

That four-layer method allows the project to state very strong correspondences without pretending a strong correspondence is automatically supernatural proof.
