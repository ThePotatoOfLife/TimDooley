Questions

A cross-project reader for questions that are answered, unresolved, recoverable, testable or deliberately kept open because the archive has not earned a stronger conclusion yet.

The project contains several kinds of question, and confusing them produces bad answers. Some questions ask what the Potatoverse means by a term. Some ask what historically happened and when. Some are source-recovery problems. Some can be translated into scientific or formal tests. Others remain philosophical or theological even when they are deeply important. This page routes each kind toward the place that can actually own the answer.

Four ways a question can move

AnswerCurated questions whose project meaning and evidence boundaries are mature enough for a concise answer.RecoverQuestions where the missing object is an older chat, screenshot, post, stream, file, date or wording rather than a new theory.TestQuestions that can be translated into variables, mechanisms, counterexamples, observations or formal models.Keep openQuestions whose uncertainty is itself part of the record and should not be converted into canon by repetition.

Question discipline

A useful question retains its source class. “What did Tim say on this date?” is not the same as “Was that statement true?” “What does Matthew the Potato do?” is not the same as “What is true about Matthew / @mtclassic3?” “Why does the project call someone a Dog?” is not the same as deciding that the person permanently is one. The archive is strongest when a question remains attached to the evidence needed to answer it.

Contradictions also belong here. When two project files disagree, the first task is to identify whether they are different dates, different modes, later corrections, literary layers, retrospective interpretations or genuine unresolved conflicts. The January 29, 2025 correction to Marty's death date is a model example: one proposition can be corrected without automatically validating every surrounding retrospective claim.

Current recovery frontier

High-value open work includes raw late-2024 Potatoverse chat recovery, the Tree/Monkey/Tachy corridor, early March 2025 TTS and HTML artifacts, the April 2025 server-to-Tree sequence, exact character relationship changes, and source-level reconstruction of older Meta/TXT material. These are not empty placeholders; each has a known reason for remaining open and a known class of evidence that would improve it.
