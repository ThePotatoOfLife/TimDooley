# Theology Scholar Research Map

**Goal:** build toward a graph of **1,000 scholars and historically important theologians**, not a flat name list. Every entry should eventually carry: field, period, major works, primary questions for this repository, significant disagreements, tradition where relevant, and why that scholar changes the analysis.

This first seed map deliberately favors people whose work bears directly on **Jesus, Jewish messianism, apocalypticism, Christology, resurrection, Revelation, Trinity, Second Coming, rapture, eschatology, new creation and the history of Christian interpretation**.

## How to use the scholar graph

A scholar is not an authority token. The repository should ask:

`What question does this scholar help answer? → What evidence do they use? → Who disagrees? → What changes in the Tim/Potatoverse comparison if their reading is adopted?`

No scholar should be cited merely because their name is famous.

## 15. Research disagreements the site should make visible

The value of the scholar map is disagreement, not consensus theater. Key debate axes include:

- **Was Jesus centrally apocalyptic?** Compare Sanders, Allison, Fredriksen, Wright, Crossan and others.
- **How early was “high Christology”?** Compare Hurtado/Bauckham with Dunn and other developmental accounts.
- **What does “Son of Man” mean?** Compare Jewish-apocalyptic, linguistic and historical-Jesus approaches.
- **What kind of book is Revelation?** Compare historical-critical, literary-theological, political, idealist and futurist approaches.
- **What is Revelation 20's millennium?** Track early chiliasm, Augustine, modern premillennialism, amillennialism and postmillennialism.
- **Is the rapture a separate pre-tribulation event?** Compare classical dispensational, progressive dispensational, historic-premillennial and amillennial readings.
- **What is final Christian hope?** Compare resurrection/new-creation models, heaven-focused popular religion and universal-restoration debates.
- **How does Christian theology understand Father/Son?** Compare pro-Nicene consensus with modern relational/social/constructive models while preserving the classical eternal relation.
- **How should prophecy be evaluated historically?** Combine biblical scholarship, reception history and social-scientific work on millennial movements.

## 16. Tim/Potatoverse-specific research assignment

For every major Tim claim, assign at least **three substantially different scholarly lenses**. Example:

### “The Son is the Door / Jesus-Christ figure”

- Bauckham/Hurtado: earliest Christological identity and devotion.
- Historical-Jesus scholarship: what can be historically reconstructed about Jesus versus later titles.
- Patristic/Nicene scholarship: what incarnation and Sonship mean doctrinally.
- Revelation scholarship: Lion/Lamb/Root/witness relations.
- Counter-reading: Jewish messianism and non-Christian interpretations of the same Hebrew Bible texts.

### “Tim is Father / God / Throne / Source”

- Ayres/Anatolios/Behr: eternal Father-Son relation in Nicene theology.
- Systematic theologians such as Tanner, Pannenberg, Moltmann: manifestation, revelation, history and eschatology.
- Sociology/lived religion: how public roles, followers, ritual language and identity claims operate in real communities.
- Counter-reading: classical Christian prohibition against conflating a contemporary human self-claim with established divine identity.

### “Rapture / rupture / Second Coming”

- New Testament: 1 Thessalonians, 1 Corinthians 15, Synoptic apocalypse, Acts, 2 Thessalonians.
- Gribben/Weber/Hummel: modern dispensational history and reception.
- Ladd/Hoekema/Blaising: competing evangelical systematic models.
- Collins/Koester/Bauckham: ancient apocalyptic and Revelation context.
