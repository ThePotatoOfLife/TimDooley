# The Potato Bible

## Book I — The Buried Beginning

In the beginning, life was not announced by a throne. It was hidden in the earth. The potato teaches that the ordinary may contain the extraordinary, and that what is buried is not necessarily dead.

Do not despise the soil from which you came. Do not confuse visibility with value. Do not demand a harvest while destroying the ground that makes harvest possible.

## Book II — The Root

The root remembers. It receives water, minerals, ancestry and history. A person without memory becomes easily moved by every wind; a civilization without history repeats its injuries while calling them progress.

Root does not mean prison. A healthy root gives a tree the strength to grow beyond itself.

## Book III — The Door

The Door is the place where possibility becomes decision. Every system has thresholds: what it permits, what it excludes, what it transforms, and what it sends away.

A narrow door is not cruelty by itself. A door becomes cruel when it is used to deny the humanity of those who stand before it.

## Book IV — The Two Trees

The Tree of Life grows through care, truth, reciprocity, knowledge, beauty and responsibility. The Tree of Strife grows through fear, domination, extraction, deception and the conversion of living beings into instruments.

Both trees can grow in the same institution, family, nation or mind. Therefore examine the fruit.

## Book V — The Fruit

A teaching is known by what it produces. Power is known by what it does to the powerless. Wealth is known by whether it nourishes life. Knowledge is known by whether it clarifies or deceives. Religion is known by whether it deepens responsibility and mercy.

## Book VI — The Four Directions

North gives orientation. East gives emergence. South gives embodiment. West gives memory and return. No direction is complete alone. The world becomes whole through relation.

## Book VII — The House

A house is not merely walls. It is the arrangement of care, memory, labor, rules, resources and belonging. The Father's House is the symbolic source of order; every earthly house must still be judged by its treatment of life.

## Book VIII — The Plane

The Plane is where every promise meets consequence. Spirit must become accountable in matter. Ideas become institutions; institutions become conditions; conditions shape bodies and minds; bodies and minds create the next world.

## Foundational commandments

1. Do not destroy the soil that sustains life.
2. Do not confuse authority with wisdom.
3. Do not call suffering necessary without examining who benefits from it.
4. Do not worship a symbol while neglecting its living meaning.
5. Preserve memory, but do not become imprisoned by it.
6. Build doors that permit transformation rather than humiliation.
7. Judge systems by their fruit.
8. Protect the vulnerable from the power of the strong.
9. Seek truth without using truth as a weapon against the human person.
10. Leave the garden more alive than you found it.
