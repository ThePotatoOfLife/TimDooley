# Farm, Sektur and Swamp: Systems Theory

## Purpose

This document turns the Farm vocabulary into a research model. The Farm is not a list of people to ridicule. It is a recurring attention ecology in which people, audiences, platforms, archives, incentives and institutions interact. The project's terms **Cow, Farmer, Dog, Milk, Mud, Farm, Sektur and Swamp** describe roles and processes inside that ecology.

The central question is not:

> Who is a lolcow?

It is:

> **What process turns a human being into a persistent object of collective attention, and what does every participant gain, lose, produce or accumulate as the process continues?**

The distinction matters. A person can become highly documented without being morally defective. An audience can participate without being malicious. A critic can become a content producer. A subject can become an active producer of their own archive. A platform can amplify a cycle without creating it. A wiki can preserve a history while also becoming part of the attention economy it documents.

---

## 1. The basic transformation

The Farm can be modeled as a sequence:

**person → public subject → recurring story → character → archive node → ecosystem node → reusable attention source**

The transformation is not automatic and is not inevitable.

A private person may remain a private person. A public figure may produce one news cycle and disappear. Another figure can accumulate years of posts, videos, threads, reaction channels, wiki pages, screenshots, court records and counter-narratives.

The difference is the creation of a **feedback loop**.

### The loop

1. A person becomes visible.
2. Something about the person becomes narratively salient.
3. Observers document it.
4. Documentation attracts more observers.
5. The subject reacts to the observation.
6. The reaction becomes new material.
7. Secondary creators reproduce the material.
8. Platforms distribute the reproduction.
9. Audiences reward distribution with attention, status or money.
10. The archive grows.
11. The archive makes future content easier to produce.
12. The larger archive increases the probability of another reaction.
13. The loop repeats.

This is why an archive can become a **machine for generating its own future evidence**: old material is continually recontextualized into new material.

## 2. Cow, Farmer and Dog

### Cow

**Cow** is the visible subject around which attention organizes.

A Cow is not inherently stupid, bad, guilty or deserving of attention. Cow is a structural position: the person has become unusually harvestable by an attention network.

Useful empirical indicators include:

- repeated discussion across independent channels;
- dedicated threads or wikis;
- recurring reaction videos;
- screenshots and archival mirrors;
- long-lived search interest;
- repeated public incidents;
- secondary creators whose output depends on the subject;
- audience communities organized around the subject;
- monetization linked to coverage;
- persistent reactivation of old material.

### Farmer

**Farmer** is the cultivation function.

A Farmer takes a subject and produces conditions under which attention can continue:

- follows developments;
- archives material;
- investigates claims;
- edits clips;
- writes summaries;
- streams reactions;
- asks questions;
- creates compilations;
- provokes responses;
- predicts future events;
- sells or monetizes coverage;
- recruits audiences;
- creates a historical narrative.

The same person can be both Cow and Farmer. This is a crucial part of the model.

### Dog

**Dog** is the pursuit/reaction function.

The Dog follows movement through the system. It may defend, attack, challenge, monitor, respond to, expose or amplify the Cow. A Dog can become a Farmer whenever its reaction becomes a durable content stream.

The project should never use “Dog” as an empirical insult or as evidence that a real person secretly serves another person. It is an analytical role.

Within the Potatoist spiritual layer, Dog can additionally represent an instrument or expression through which a divine/systemic function operates. That is theology, not an empirical claim about real-world control.

## 4. Mud

Mud is accumulated residue.

In the symbolic system, Mud is what sticks after conflict. Empirically, the repository should translate Mud into observable traces:

- documented allegations;
- court filings;
- judgments;
- settlements;
- corrections;
- retractions;
- platform sanctions;
- advertiser withdrawals;
- broken relationships;
- public apologies;
- contradictory statements;
- archived disputes;
- regulatory findings;
- reputational claims;
- documented financial liabilities.

A Mud record should always distinguish:

**allegation → evidence → response → adjudication → remaining uncertainty**.

This prevents the archive from turning accusation into fact merely through repetition.

- meme communities;

Sektur is not a single organization. It is a field of overlapping communities.

That distinction is essential. A shared vocabulary or shared interest does not establish centralized control or conspiracy.

## 8. Why archives matter

An archive changes the economics of attention.

Before an archive, a creator must observe something new. After an archive exists, a creator can produce content by retrieving something old.

Therefore:

**archive → lower production cost → more commentary → more audience → more incentives to archive**.

The archive becomes infrastructure.

A wiki may therefore be simultaneously:

1. a preservation system;
2. a research resource;
3. a community identity system;
4. a narrative authority;
5. a source for new content;
6. a participant in the attention economy.

The repository should study all six functions.

## 9. The Farmer/Cow inversion

One of the most important recurring patterns is role inversion.

A commentator begins by studying a Cow.

The commentator accumulates an audience.

The commentator becomes interesting in their own right.

The Cow begins documenting the commentator.

The commentator becomes a Cow.

This creates:

**Cow → Farmer → Cow → Farmer → Cow**.

The Farm therefore does not have a stable center.

It behaves more like a network than a hierarchy.

This is why the project should record relationships rather than merely assigning labels.

---

## 10. The Dog/Farmer transition

The Dog initially reacts.

Reaction generates attention.

Attention generates an audience.

Audience creates incentive to produce more reaction.

Reaction becomes commentary.

Commentary becomes a channel.

The channel becomes an institution or brand.

Thus:

**Dog → reaction → audience → Farmer**.

The reverse can also happen:

**Farmer → failed cultivation → personal controversy → Cow**.

These transitions are more informative than fixed labels.

## 11. Alpha and Omega

The project's “Alpha/Omega” language can be used structurally.

### Alpha

The beginning of the attention chain:

- first viral event;
- first unusual public act;
- first observer;
- first archive;
- first dedicated thread.

### Omega

The point at which the archive becomes larger than the originating event.

At Omega:

- old material generates new material;
- new audiences inherit old narratives;
- the person becomes partly an archive character;
- secondary creators may never have witnessed the original event;
- the community can reproduce itself through memory alone.

This is why death, disappearance or retirement does not necessarily end the Farm.

---

## 12. Death and the archive

Death creates a major state transition.

The living subject can no longer directly create new primary reactions. But their historical material remains.

The system can then shift from:

**live reaction ecology**

to

**historical archive ecology**.

Possible outcomes include:

- memorialization;
- posthumous criticism;
- documentary production;
- mythologization;
- archival preservation;
- disputes over interpretation;
- disappearance;
- renewed attention after a later event.

The repository should never treat death as entertainment. It is a reason to study how memory systems preserve, distort and reproduce human narratives.

## 13. The 10,000 Neighbours

The “10,000 Trees of Strife” should not mean that exactly 10,000 real people were harmed, sold or transformed.

It is a corpus-design target and symbolic capacity.

A strong 10,000-neighbour corpus would contain relational dossiers, each asking:

- Who is involved?
- What happened?
- When?
- What is documented?
- Who says what?
- What evidence exists?
- What relationships connect the actors?
- What incentives are observable?
- What attention flows?
- What money flows?
- What legal obligations exist?
- What archive exists?
- What changed afterward?
- What repair occurred?
- What remains unresolved?

The goal is not to manufacture 10,000 villains.

The goal is to understand 10,000 examples of relational transformation.

## 14. The Potatoist theological layer

Within the Potatoist canon, the Farm is a lower-plane expression of the Tree of Strife.

The upper theological structure is:

**God → Most High → Tim → Father/Source → Throne → North of North → Axis → Ladder → Door → Son/Thomas/Twin → World**.

The lower transformation is:

**World → society → Swamp → Farm → Farmer/Dog → Cow → Milk → Mud**.

The return path is:

**Mud → Root → Potato → Sprout → Tree → Fruit → Seed → New Life → return toward Father**.

This is a theological interpretation created by the project. It is not an empirical cosmology.

Within the Potatoist canon, **Tim is God** in the project's spiritual ontology.

This does not mean the repository should claim that an empirically identifiable human being has been scientifically proven to be God. The statement belongs to the project's theological layer.

## 16. How God works for Tim / how Tim works for God

The apparent paradox is deliberate.

God works for Tim because God supplies the transcendent function that makes Tim more than an ordinary narrative character inside the canon.

Tim works for God because Tim gives that transcendent principle:

- a name;
- a story;
- a genealogy;
- a throne;
- a mythology;
- a relationship with the Son;
- an orientation toward North;
- a Garden;
- a Potato;
- a Door;
- a vocabulary through which the principle can be narrated.

Thus the two names describe different sides of one function.

**God = the source viewed from transcendence.**

**Tim = the source viewed through the project's named identity.**

The “work” is therefore reciprocal naming, manifestation and interpretation rather than employment between two independent beings.

## 19. Red Potato and Blue Potato

The dual-potato model provides another way to read the Farm.

### Red Potato — Life

- creativity;
- love;
- light-seeking;
- outward/upward movement;
- sprouting;
- expansion;
- renewal;
- connection.

### Blue Potato — Strife

- memory;
- meticulous recording;
- fear;
- downward/root movement;
- contraction;
- preservation of residue;
- defensive knowledge;
- fixation.

The Farm can contain both.

An archive can preserve harm, but it can also preserve evidence. Documentation can feed outrage, but it can also enable accountability. Memory can become fixation or wisdom.

The question is therefore not simply whether the archive exists.

It is **what the archive does with what it remembers**.

## 20. From Tree of Strife to Tree of Life

The central ethical transformation is:

**attention → observation → understanding → documentation → verification → synthesis → repair → knowledge**.

The Farm becomes destructive when:

**attention → humiliation → escalation → monetization → repetition**.

The same raw material can therefore feed two trees.

### Tree of Strife

Attention is converted into conflict and repeatedly harvested.

### Tree of Life

Attention is converted into understanding, evidence, memory, accountability and repair.

This distinction should govern the repository itself.

The project should not merely reproduce the Farm's incentives. It should metabolize them.

## 21. Research protocol

Every real-world case should record:

1. **Identity resolution** — who or what is being discussed?
2. **Primary sources** — what did the person actually say or do?
3. **Independent corroboration** — what can be verified elsewhere?
4. **Timeline** — what happened first?
5. **Relationship graph** — who connects to whom?
6. **Audience graph** — who distributes or reacts?
7. **Economic graph** — who receives money or resources?
8. **Institutional graph** — platforms, companies, courts, regulators, organizations.
9. **Archive graph** — wikis, forums, videos, mirrors, articles.
10. **Evidence status** — allegation, disputed, documented, adjudicated, unknown.
11. **Counterevidence** — what challenges the dominant narrative?
12. **Repair events** — what reduced harm or closed the loop?
13. **Uncertainty** — what remains unknown?
14. **Update date** — when was the record last verified?

The repository must never allow a large number of repeated claims to masquerade as independent evidence.
