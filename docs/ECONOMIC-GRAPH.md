# European Economic Graph

The European Economic Graph is the empirical research layer of the North Programme.

## Scope

Map the observable system through relationships among:

- governments and public agencies
- public and private companies
- ownership and control
- banks, funds and financial instruments
- bonds, debt and obligations
- infrastructure
- procurement
- public funding and subsidies
- trade and value chains
- energy systems
- technology
- research institutions
- labour and skills
- strategic dependencies
- geopolitical ties

## Data model

A graph entry should ideally contain:

`source → relationship → target`

with supporting metadata:

`date, jurisdiction, source, evidence type, confidence, value, currency, notes`

## Evidence classes

- Observed: directly supported by a reliable source.
- Calculated: derived transparently from observed values.
- Estimated: modelled or approximated.
- Scenario: conditional projection.
- Interpretation: analytical reading of evidence.
- Mythological: part of the Potatoverse symbolic layer.

Do not merge these classes.

## Priority research

France and Belgium are priority cases for expenditure structure and fiscal reform. European energy, infrastructure, procurement, technology and strategic dependencies should be mapped alongside public finances so that fiscal decisions are evaluated against productive capacity.

## Long-run view

The graph should be continuously updated rather than treated as a static report. Changes in ownership, debt, contracts, supply chains, energy capacity, technology and geopolitical alignment can alter the network even when individual institutions remain unchanged.
