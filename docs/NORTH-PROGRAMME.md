# North Programme — Live Baseline

## Mission

Build a continuously updated, evidence-backed map of Europe's observable economic system while developing a serious programme for fiscal sustainability, productive capacity, strategic resilience and long-term prosperity.

The programme is a framework for research and policy design. It is not a description of an existing government programme.

The North Programme is also the principal empirical arm of the project's broader **repair-of-the-world** ambition. The symbolic North-of-North viewpoint asks the atlas to see the whole system; the North Programme asks what can actually be done inside that system once its mechanisms, constraints and dependencies are known. This is why the economic graph belongs inside the larger World Atlas rather than existing as an isolated policy document.

## Tikkun Olam and the North Programme

The project uses **tikkun olam — repair of the world** as a comparative Jewish concept and as a project-defined ethical horizon. Historically the phrase has different meanings across Jewish sources and periods, including rabbinic measures introduced for practical social order and later mystical and modern ethical interpretations. The North Programme does not claim to reproduce any one of those traditions. It takes the underlying repair question as a methodological challenge: **if a system is producing avoidable harm, fragility or waste, what would it take to understand the mechanism well enough to improve it?**

That question changes the programme's relationship to data. Fiscal numbers are not collected merely to make a dashboard. Ownership records are not collected merely to make a company list. Energy infrastructure is not collected merely to draw cables on a map. Each dataset is valuable because it can reveal a mechanism: a dependency, bottleneck, duplicated function, concentration, vulnerability, incentive or opportunity for intervention.

Repair therefore requires a chain:

**observe → document → connect → explain mechanism → identify intervention → estimate distributional effects → implement where legitimate → measure → revise.**

The programme must remain capable of discovering that an assumed problem is smaller than expected, that an apparent solution creates a larger secondary problem, or that the evidence does not justify intervention. Repair is not a licence for arbitrary control.

## The central rule

**Map relationships, not isolated entities.**

A country is not just a GDP number. A budget is not just a deficit. An energy source is not just a production figure. Each becomes meaningful when connected to the institutions, infrastructure, capital, labour, technology and dependencies around it.

The same rule applies to religion, civil society and security. A country's religious composition is not an explanation of its politics. An NGO is not merely a name and a donor is not automatically a controller. An intelligence service is not a free-floating hidden actor. Each relationship must be specified: legal authority, funding, personnel, procurement, information exchange, documented cooperation, public policy, historical affiliation or another identifiable mechanism.

## North Axis

The working geographic architecture is anchored in Denmark and Greenland and extends through a dynamic northern/eastern/western European network:

`Canada / Greenland → Scandinavia / Finland → Britain / Ireland / Scotland → Iceland → continental Europe → Ukraine → Turkey`

This is a conceptual research geography, not an existing political alliance. Membership is therefore not assumed. Russia, Mesopotamia and other regions remain possible future research questions rather than predetermined members.

From the North-of-North viewpoint, this geography is not merely a political boundary. It is a research corridor through which to study energy, trade, finance, migration, security, technology, infrastructure, religion, demography and cultural exchange. Every proposed connection must nevertheless be established empirically.

## The world-repair data requirement

If the project is going to reason about repair at all, it needs a sufficiently rich world model. The North Programme therefore treats the following as first-class research layers rather than peripheral metadata:

### People and communities

Publicly documented people, communities, populations, migration patterns, labour markets, demographic structure and relevant biographies. The aim is to understand who is affected by a system and how effects are distributed, not to reduce people to abstract statistics.

### Nations and government

Constitutional structure, ministries, agencies, public expenditure, taxation, debt, regulation, elections, public assets and intergovernmental relationships. Administrative layers matter because a reform that looks simple at national level may cross federal, regional, municipal or supranational authority.

### Religion and worldview

Religious composition, institutions, historical development, movements, places of worship, migration-linked changes and relationships with law and public life. Religious identity should be treated as context and social structure, not as a shortcut for attributing political motives to individuals or institutions.

### Civil society and NGOs

Organizations, missions, funding sources, programmes, beneficiaries, partnerships, procurement and documented institutional relationships. Funding should be mapped as a relationship with a source and amount where available; it should not automatically be interpreted as control.

### Security and intelligence

Publicly documented structures of police, military, intelligence and security institutions, their legal mandates, budgets, oversight, procurement and confirmed institutional relationships. The atlas should distinguish documented operations from allegations and speculation. The presence of an institution in the graph is not itself evidence of wrongdoing.

### Companies, ownership and finance

Beneficial ownership where publicly available, parent/subsidiary structures, board relationships, investment, debt, procurement, supply chains, lenders, customers and infrastructure dependencies. The aim is to see how economic power actually travels through institutions.

### Infrastructure and physical systems

Ports, rail, roads, pipelines, electricity grids, cables, data centres, satellites, factories, hospitals, universities, water systems and logistics nodes. Physical bottlenecks often reveal dependencies that financial or political data alone cannot show.

### Technology and research

Semiconductors, AI compute, cloud, industrial software, telecommunications, cybersecurity, robotics, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, advanced materials, research institutions and patent/knowledge flows. Strategic resilience depends on both ownership and capability.

## Programme pillars

### 1. Fiscal sustainability

The long-run analytical target is to move participating high-debt countries toward debt/GDP below 85% while avoiding indiscriminate cuts to productive capacity.

The first task is decomposition:

`total expenditure = pensions + healthcare + transfers + administration + interest + defence + infrastructure + education + other`

Each category must then be decomposed again by institution, programme and legal commitment.

### 2. Productive investment

Fiscal consolidation should distinguish consumption from investment. Infrastructure, energy, research, education, digital capacity and productive industrial investment should be tested for long-run returns rather than treated as interchangeable with administrative overhead.

### 3. Administrative architecture

Investigate duplicated agencies, overlapping competences, procurement structures, reporting requirements and public back-office functions. The goal is not “cut bureaucracy” as a slogan; it is to identify specific relationships that can be simplified without reducing essential service delivery.

### 4. Healthcare

Map spending, providers, procurement, staffing, pharmaceuticals, prevention and outcomes. Efficiency proposals should be evaluated against health outcomes rather than judged by expenditure reduction alone.

### 5. Pensions and ageing

Separate demographic pressure from policy design. Map retirement age, contribution structures, replacement rates, public transfers, occupational pensions and long-run dependency ratios.

### 6. Procurement

Treat public procurement as a graph:

`public buyer → framework → supplier → subcontractor → infrastructure/service`

Look for aggregation, interoperability, open standards, lifecycle cost and cross-border purchasing opportunities while preserving competition and resilience.

### 7. Energy

The EU Energy Union explicitly combines security, solidarity, an integrated internal energy market, efficiency and decarbonisation. The North Programme therefore treats energy as infrastructure, market design, industrial policy and strategic dependency simultaneously.

Source: https://energy.ec.europa.eu/strategy/energy-union_en

### 8. Technology and research

Map European capacity in semiconductors, AI compute, cloud, industrial software, telecommunications, cybersecurity, robotics, pharmaceuticals, biotechnology and advanced materials. Track both ownership and dependency.

### 9. Labour and skills

Map demographics, labour-force participation, education, vocational training, migration, wages, productivity and sectoral shortages. Fiscal policy should be evaluated against its effects on the future productive base.

## From map to repair

The programme should maintain an explicit intervention model for any proposed reform:

**problem → evidence → mechanism → affected groups → intervention → cost → benefit → distributional effect → second-order effects → implementation authority → legal constraint → time horizon → measurable outcome.**

This prevents the North Programme from becoming a collection of intuitions. It also makes it possible to compare very different interventions. A procurement reform, a pension reform, an energy interconnector or an education investment can all be represented as interventions with inputs, mechanisms, affected populations and measurable outcomes.

The project should also record **non-intervention conclusions**. Sometimes the evidence will show that a system is working adequately, that an apparent inefficiency is the cost of resilience, or that a proposed intervention would create more damage than it prevents. In those cases, the correct repair action may be to leave the system alone and document why.

## Fiscal baseline

Eurostat reports that at the end of 2025 government debt was 115.6% of GDP in France, 107.9% in Belgium and 27.9% in Denmark. EU government debt was 81.7% of GDP. In 2025 the EU government deficit was 3.1% of GDP; France's was 5.1% and Belgium's 5.2%.

Source: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/fr/web/products-euro-indicators/w/2-22042026-bp

These figures establish the starting point for research. They do not determine the correct policy response.

## Country workstreams

### France

First map expenditure by function and level of government. Then identify legal commitments, overlaps, tax expenditures, subsidy structures and productivity-enhancing investments. Do not propose a cut until the underlying function and outcome are understood.

### Belgium

Map the federal, regional and community division of responsibilities before assessing administrative duplication. Belgium is a particularly useful case for relationship mapping because institutional structure itself is a major part of the fiscal question.

### Denmark

Use Denmark as a comparative case for fiscal structure, labour-market institutions, public administration, energy and industrial organization. Comparative analysis should distinguish transferable mechanisms from country-specific conditions.

## Energy / resources

Greenland, the North Sea and the Netherlands are research areas for resources and energy infrastructure. Any claim about extraction potential must be tested against geology, economics, environmental law, infrastructure requirements, permitting, local interests and geopolitical constraints.

## European integration

The EU single market already provides a powerful existing relationship layer: the European Commission describes free movement of goods, capital, services and people across the EU and a market of roughly €18 trillion GDP.

Source: https://commission.europa.eu/topics/single-market_en

The North Programme should therefore build on actual European institutions and relationships rather than inventing parallel structures where existing ones can do the job.

## Diplomatic principle

Participation is voluntary. The programme can propose cooperation, infrastructure links, research partnerships and institutional alignment without presuming political accession.

## Research discipline

Every empirical claim should have:

`claim → source → date → jurisdiction → evidence class → confidence → method → notes`

Every policy proposal should additionally have:

`problem → mechanism → intervention → cost → benefit → distributional effect → implementation risk → measurable outcome`

That is the standard required to move from a vision to an actual programme. In the broader repair architecture, the same standard applies whether the subject is a national budget, an NGO, a religious institution, a security service, a supply chain or an individual trajectory.
