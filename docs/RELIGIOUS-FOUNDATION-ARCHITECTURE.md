# Religious Foundation Architecture

The religious layer is represented as a growing historical tree embedded in a field.

## The vertical tree

**FIELD → SUBSTRATE → FOUNDATION → SEED → SPROUT → FORMATION → TRUNK → BRANCHES → PRESENT**

A foundation is not necessarily a single founding date. It is the layer where a recognizable formation enters the historical field. Ancient traditions may have several legitimate beginnings and long periods of development.

### Field

The world already present: geography, ecology, languages, political structures, economies, social institutions, neighboring traditions, inherited stories and material culture.

### Substrate

The traditions and structures from which a formation inherits vocabulary, practices, institutions, stories, rituals, places or problems. Inheritance does not imply simple derivation.

### Foundation / Seed

The earliest identifiable formation or formative event-set. This may be a founder, movement, community, revelation tradition, textual corpus, ritual complex or gradual regional development.

### Sprout

The early community, teachers, texts, practices, sacred places and institutions through which the formation becomes recognizable.

### Formation

The period in which authority, identity, doctrine, ritual, textual transmission and institutions crystallize.

### Trunk

Long-duration continuity across generations: institutions, canon, interpretation, ritual, law, memory, culture and transmission.

### Branches

Schools, denominations, sects, reforms, mystical currents, geographic forms, diaspora forms and modern transformations.

## Multiple clocks

Every foundation can carry separate temporal markers:

- traditional or sacred origin
- historical emergence
- earliest material evidence
- earliest textual evidence
- community formation
- institutional crystallization
- state recognition or adoption
- modern transformation

This prevents a traditional sacred timeline from being silently converted into a historical fact, while also preventing historical reconstruction from erasing how a community understands its own origins.

## Cross-cutting dimensions

The tree is intersected by additional structures: ultimate reality, cosmology, anthropology, salvation/liberation, ethics, law, text, oral transmission, interpretation, ritual, calendar, leadership, institution, community, family, education, art, music, architecture, material culture, economy, property, charity, state, politics, migration, diaspora, technology, media, demography, sacred space, ecology, death and burial, gender/family, intergenerational transmission, relations with other traditions, modernization, secularization, revival, memory and evidence.

## Religious ecology

The field continues after a tradition emerges. A tradition can migrate, transform its environment, encounter other traditions, form institutions, lose institutions, revive, split, merge or become newly interpreted. The field therefore surrounds the tree through its entire life rather than existing only before the foundation.

## Potatoism

Potatoism is represented using the same architecture. Its current project-defined birth marker is **2020-12-25**. That date is a seed point in the project's own timeline, not a claim that its complete later architecture existed at that moment.

The developing Potatoism timeline records the growth of the potato metaphor, Potato of Life, Axis and North, Father and Father's House, Source, Heaven, Ladder, Tree of Life, Tree of Strife, Door, Son, Vessel, Plane, Red Potato, Blue Potato, Spudlight, North of North and the wider comparative architecture.

The research rule is simple: **birth is the seed; the seed is not the tree.** Later additions should be dated where possible so the development of the system can itself become a historical record.

## Comparative rule

Similarity is a research object, not proof of common origin. The archive preserves textual parallels, structural analogies, shared symbols, historical influence, contradiction and deliberate departure as different relationship types.
