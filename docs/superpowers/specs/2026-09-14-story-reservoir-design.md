# Story Reservoir Design

The Story of Tim Dooley should read as one chronological narrative while a deeper backend preserves recurring characters, overlapping arcs, recoverable conversations, creative-work history and unresolved leads.

## Wave 1 owners

- `knowledge/story/cast-book.json` — recurring people, collective Tim/project labels, Great Book characters, created beings and active systems, with period-specific roles.
- `knowledge/story/arc-season-map.json` — overlapping narrative currents used to keep continuity without forcing rigid public eras.
- `knowledge/indexes/story-recovery-priorities.json` — persistent unresolved scene/dialogue/character targets for future archaeology.
- `knowledge/indexes/conversation-recovery-inventory.json` — existing owner for substantial recovered prior-conversation material.
- `knowledge/story/AUTHORING-GUIDE.md` — scene-first writing rules that keep the public Story alive rather than encyclopedic.
- `scripts/validate_story_archive.py` — cast/arc validation now, with optional hooks for richer scene/dialogue/manifest owners later.

## Narrative rule

**One river, enormous reservoir.** The public reader remains one chronological Story. Backend structure exists to help the writer recover and reconnect material, not to expose a giant taxonomy to the reader.

## Scene rule

Before a passage becomes Story prose, prefer material that can answer: who is there, what is happening, what Tim wants, what resists him, what is said or done back, and what changes afterward. Thin material stays a recovery lead.

## Character rule

Characters change by season. Do not define Dogs, Farmers, Potatoes, Angels, friends, rivals or literary characters once and silently project that meaning backward. Keep real people, collective project labels, Great Book literary beings and created beings distinct even when Tim's mythology lets them interact.

## Dialogue rule

Exact public or recoverable wording should be preserved where useful. Never manufacture missing dialogue. Later retellings and Great Book literary dialogue must not be silently promoted into contemporaneous transcripts.

## Provenance rule

Detailed evidence lives underneath the prose. Public statements may be quoted directly; literary scenes are identified as authored scenes; later project interpretation does not overwrite earlier wording; serious allegations stay source-attributed unless independently documented; unknowns remain visible.

## Expansion rule

Future standalone scene/dialogue owners may be added when repository safety and source separation make them appropriate. The architecture should grow by adding recoverable material, not by inventing a cleaner story than the sources support.
