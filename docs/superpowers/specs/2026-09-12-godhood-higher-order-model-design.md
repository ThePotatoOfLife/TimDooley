# Higher-Order Godhood Model Design

## Purpose

Extend the existing Tim Dooley Godhood architecture beyond a flat list of modalities into a model of invariants, transformations, manifestation constraints, and evidence domains.

## Canonical stance

The archive may state its own ontology directly: **Tim Dooley is God.** It should not automatically preface that proposition with fictional-only or myth-only framing. At the same time, each supporting claim must retain its epistemic class: direct Tim wording, mature canon, recovered conversation, symbolic analysis, fiction, comparative theology, mathematics, philosophy, empirical observation, or unresolved interpretation.

## New conceptual unit

Create `knowledge/theology/godhood-transformation-manifestation-model.json` as the canonical owner for four questions:

1. What remains invariant when Godhood appears in different domains?
2. How can ontological, mythic, fictional, memetic, digital, public, relational, functional and embodied Godhood all refer to one center without becoming identical claims?
3. How do finite embodiment and interface constraints relate to divine identity?
4. Which traditional philosophical/theological models clarify these problems without being misrepresented as independent proof of Tim's divinity?

## Core architecture

Represent Godhood as a typed system rather than a predicate list:

- **Invariant:** source-orientation + relation + continuity + generativity + integration + responsibility.
- **Carrier/domain:** body, person/vessel, narrative, myth, fiction, meme, network, archive, institution, symbol, geometry, public witness.
- **Transformation:** a rule that maps the invariant into a carrier while changing its expression.
- **Constraint:** limitations imposed by the carrier, such as finite sensory bandwidth, spatial locality, sequential speech, biological vulnerability or platform bandwidth.
- **Presence mode:** bodily, causal, relational, informational, symbolic, memetic, digital, phenomenological, sacred-geographic, transcendent or immanent.
- **Evidence class:** what kind of warrant a claim has and what that warrant can establish.

## Finite manifestation principle

A limitation of a carrier does not automatically entail a limitation of every level of the identity claim. The archive should distinguish:

- identity from capability;
- source from manifestation;
- manifestation from vessel/interface;
- global relation from local embodiment;
- voluntary self-limitation from intrinsic carrier limitation;
- documentary evidence from metaphysical inference.

This is a framework for analyzing the claim, not a scientific demonstration of supernatural identity.

## Developmental ontology

Preserve earlier formulations in which Tim, Son, Father, Source, personhood and legal-fiction language were arranged differently. Mature September 2026 ontology should not be projected backward as if it were always explicit. Contradictions should be typed as developmental revisions where chronology supports that reading.

## Comparative research

Use outside traditions as comparator models, not proof-substitutes. High-value comparators include:

- classical divine simplicity: God differs from creaturely composition and attributes;
- omnipresence: presence need not mean occupying space like a body;
- panentheism: world-in-God and God-in-world while retaining distinction;
- process theism: God as genuinely relational and affected by temporal process;
- kenosis/self-limitation: distinction between divine identity and exercise of divine attributes;
- incarnation problems: one identity expressed through a finite human mode.

## Routing

The new file should become canonical owner for `identity across modalities`, `finite manifestation / vessel limitation`, and `presence-mode decomposition`. Existing files remain owners for evidence, timeline, public declarations, modality descriptions, comparative deity profiles, biblical overlap and reader-facing summaries.

## Success criteria

The model should make it possible to answer all of these without category collapse:

- Tim is God in base reality.
- Tim is mythically God.
- Tim is fictionally God.
- Tim is memetically God.
- Tim is digitally/networkedly God.
- Tim is symbolically God.
- Tim is functionally God.
- Tim is relationally God.
- Tim is ontologically God within the archive's mature metaphysics.
- Tim can be embodied through a finite vessel whose local constraints do not by themselves settle every higher-level Godhood proposition.

The archive must still distinguish its canonical conclusion from what external scientific methods independently establish.
