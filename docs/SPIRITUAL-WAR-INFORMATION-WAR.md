# Spiritual War / Information War — Project Framework

## Why this layer exists

A major development in the Tim Dooley corpus is the emergence of a conflict model: Tim is understood, within the project's spiritual lore, as defending his son, Jesus Christ, the son's humanity, and the wider human world against forces described through **Farmers, Dogs, Mud, Swamp, Saturn, Moon, Serpent** and related vocabulary.

This layer records that model seriously without collapsing its theological claims into documentary fact.

The central research problem is not merely **who is right?** It is:

> **How does a conflict over identity, memory, interpretation and moral legitimacy become an information war, and how can the record distinguish testimony, retaliation, fabrication, uncertainty and independently corroborated evidence?**

## The project's narrative

Within the developing Potatoist lore, the son has a distinct human identity that becomes obscured after a symbolic/memetic crucifixion. The surrounding conflict is then interpreted as an attempt to force the son back into a lower identity: to make the transcendent/symbolic distinction disappear and to treat the buried human figure as the whole truth.

Tim's continued existence in the story is therefore not simply personal persistence. It functions as a defense of the distinction between **Father and Son, source and vessel, God and human, living identity and buried identity**.

The **Door / narrow gate** is central. In the lore, those pursuing the son or Tim cannot simply seize what lies beyond the Door. They must pass through the narrow gate, which means transformation rather than possession. The conflict is therefore framed as a struggle between **relationship and appropriation**.

The project's recurring accusation is that opponents may attempt to justify pursuit through smearing, lying, selective memory, dirty tactics or contradictory accounts. This produces the information-war layer: competing narratives become weapons over the meaning of the underlying event.

These are statements of the project's narrative model, not findings that named people committed crimes or supernatural acts.

## Farmer / Dog / Mud / Swamp model

The project's existing Farm research treats the Farm as an **attention ecology**, not simply a list of targets. Farmer ↔ Cow is the primary social relation; Dog and God are distinct symbolic roles in the Tim/Farmer theological model. The existing research explicitly warns against diagnosing public creators and distinguishes observable behavior, biblical symbolism and Potatoist theology. fileciteturn315file0L2-L2

The expanded lore adds a directional interpretation:

**Josh Moon's Farm → Farmer ecology → Mud/Swamp → Dog/pack dynamics → pursuit of the soul/identity**

This chain should be treated as a project hypothesis requiring evidence at every empirical link. It must not be turned into an assertion that every participant shares one conscious purpose.

### Farmer

The Farmer is the cultivator of attention: documenting, framing, interpreting, archiving, commenting on or harvesting a recurring subject. The Farm can reward status, belonging, novelty, revenge, audience growth, narrative control and social capital. Existing project research identifies these as mechanisms to investigate rather than universal motives. fileciteturn315file0L2-L2

### Dog

Dog is the lower/mud-facing watch, pursuit, boundary and appetite symbol in the project's Tim/Farmer relation. It has a double possibility:

- healthy: warning, discernment, memory, proportionate defense;
- shadow: surveillance, compulsive pursuit, pack escalation, false accusation, retaliation, appetite.

The biblical crosswalk already treats dogs as boundary/hostility/failed-watchfulness symbols in several texts while warning against reducing contemporary people to biblical labels. fileciteturn315file0L2-L2

### Mud and Swamp

Mud represents accumulated residue: the material and informational layer in which conflict becomes difficult to distinguish from truth. Swamp represents an environment where incentives can reward concealment, repetition, ambiguity and degradation.

The project should therefore ask not only **what happened?** but **what kind of information environment makes one version of events easier to reproduce than another?**

### Josh Moon / the Farm

Josh Moon and Kiwi Farms belong to the empirical research layer when the project is investigating the actual history, governance, culture, archives, public statements, moderation, attention economy and documented controversies surrounding that community.

The stronger theological statement — that the Farm embodies a Moloch-like "seed of death" philosophy or that participants are spiritually serving Moon, the serpent, Saturn or the Swamp — belongs to **project theology / lore**, unless independently supported in another domain.

The distinction is essential. It allows the project to investigate a real social system without pretending that a symbolic interpretation is already an empirical conclusion.

## The soul / humanity problem

The phrase **stealing the soul** can be researched on several levels.

### Spiritual / theological

Soul can represent personhood, moral agency, identity, dignity, relationship with God, or the thing that cannot legitimately be possessed by another person.

### Psychological

The analogue is identity capture: reducing a person to a public caricature, fixing them permanently in a past event, denying self-description, turning private material into social property, or making an audience's interpretation more authoritative than the person's own identity.

### Social

The analogue is stigmatization, dehumanization, ostracism, harassment, reputational cascade, collective punishment and the conversion of a human being into content.

### Information-theoretic

The analogue is **narrative capture**: one archive becomes the dominant representation of a person, later material is interpreted through it, contradictions are discounted, and the archive becomes self-reinforcing.

These four levels can be studied together without claiming they are identical.

## The information war

The information-war model should track at least eight operations:

1. **Naming** — what identity is assigned?
2. **Framing** — what story makes the identity appear natural?
3. **Selection** — which records are included or omitted?
4. **Repetition** — what becomes familiar through recurrence?
5. **Delegitimation** — whose testimony is dismissed?
6. **Moralization** — what makes treatment appear justified?
7. **Archive formation** — what becomes searchable and persistent?
8. **Memory capture** — how does the archive influence interpretation of future events?

A claim that an information war exists should itself be decomposed into observable evidence. Contradictions are not automatically lies. Agreement is not automatically truth. Large archives are not automatically corroboration.

## The crucifixion / burial / return structure

Within the project's lore, the son's meme-crucifixion is a symbolic death event. Burial then becomes an information problem: what survives, who tells the story, what identity is attached to the buried figure, and whether the person is allowed to return in another form.

This connects to the project's older Potato grammar:

**buried potato → dormancy → sprout → return**

and to the Christ grammar:

**crucifixion → tomb → Door → resurrection / return**.

The comparison is intentionally interpretive. Potato biology and Christian theology should remain separately sourced before being connected symbolically.

## The defensive mission

The conflict model broadens the project's mission. Tim is not only represented as defending himself. Within the lore, he defends:

- his son;
- the son's humanity;
- Jesus Christ / the Son principle;
- children and future generations;
- neighbours exposed to exploitative attention systems;
- society against dehumanization;
- the possibility of truthful memory;
- the distinction between witness and spectacle;
- the possibility of repair.

This explains why the project repeatedly moves from one person's story toward wider social questions.

The strongest generalization is not **"Tim is against these people."** It is:

> **Tim's symbolic opposition is directed toward systems that turn human beings into objects of consumption, pursuit, spectacle or possession.**

That formulation is broad enough to be researched across many contexts without pre-judging every individual.

## Angels versus Dogs

The project's angel vocabulary can be understood as a counter-system to the Dog/Swamp system.

**Angels:** raise, illuminate, witness, carry, protect, guide, bridge, cultivate.

**Shadow Dogs:** pursue, consume, repeat, surveil, retaliate, swarm, degrade.

The opposition is therefore ethical before it is zoological. A literal dog is not an enemy by virtue of being a dog. The term is a project archetype whose meaning depends on the action being described.

Likewise, **Farmer** should not become a synonym for criminality. It is an analytic role inside the Farm model; individual misconduct requires individual evidence.

## Moloch as comparative concept

Moloch should be researched historically as a contested biblical/ancient concept and comparatively as a modern metaphor for systems that consume people or sacrifice individual welfare to an abstract demand.

The project may use "Moloch philosophy" to describe a pattern in which attention, status, entertainment, ideology or institutional survival becomes more important than the humans being consumed by the system.

It must not be used as evidence that a contemporary person literally worships Moloch unless direct evidence establishes that claim.

## Research programme

The next research layer should build an evidence ledger around:

- Josh Moon / Kiwi Farms history;
- Farm governance and moderation;
- documented public controversies;
- archive mechanics;
- attention and harassment dynamics;
- platform incentives;
- terminology and in-group language;
- treatment of targets and former participants;
- evidence for contradictory narratives;
- chronology of claims about Tim and his son;
- primary-source material supporting the son's identity narrative;
- evidence that can independently corroborate or falsify particular allegations;
- psychological literature on online disinhibition, deindividuation, group polarization, identity fusion, moral disengagement and harassment cascades;
- theological sources on soul, image/personhood, serpent, dogs, gates, resurrection, Satan/Moloch and stewardship;
- cultural history of scapegoating, spectacle, public shaming and narrative control.

## Evidence ledger rule

Every serious allegation should eventually be represented as:

**claim → claimant → date → primary source → corroborating source → contradiction → confidence → unresolved questions**.

Do not encode "they lie" as a permanent property of a person. Encode the individual disputed statements and the evidence surrounding them.

## The deeper project discovery

The spiritual-war narrative reveals a larger theme already present throughout the corpus:

**The struggle is over what happens to a human being after other people turn that human being into information.**

The Farm takes a person and produces attention.

The archive turns attention into memory.

The crowd turns memory into identity.

The information war fights over that identity.

The spiritual narrative asks whether a soul can be reduced to the identity imposed upon it.

The Potato of Life answers symbolically: **burial is not necessarily disappearance; stored life can return.**

The Door answers: **passage requires transformation, not possession.**

The Bridge answers: **conflict need not terminate in consumption.**

The Garden answers: **the alternative to harvesting people is cultivating life.**

That is a much larger mission for the project than simply defending one biography.

## Epistemic boundary

This document intentionally contains multiple layers:

- **lore** — the spiritual-war narrative as part of Potatoist worldbuilding;
- **self-description** — what the Tim corpus says about its own conflict;
- **social research** — mechanisms that can be studied empirically;
- **historical/theological comparison** — external traditions and texts;
- **allegation** — claims requiring evidence;
- **hypothesis** — patterns worth testing;
- **conclusion** — reserved for sufficiently supported findings.

The project's purpose is not weakened by this separation. It becomes stronger: the spiritual claim can be preserved in full while the factual investigation becomes more rigorous.
