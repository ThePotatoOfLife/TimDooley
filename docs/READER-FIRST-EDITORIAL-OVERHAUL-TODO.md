## Editorial law

**Inhabit before orient.**

A reader should receive something worth carrying away before the page explains where the page belongs.

The project may still be meticulous about provenance, ownership, routes and epistemic boundaries, but those are backstage disciplines. Frontstage prose should speak to the reader about life, God, mind, history, culture, bodies, power, suffering, art, evidence and repair.

A strong page should usually move:

1. **Question / scene** — begin with a human, theological, philosophical, scientific or worldly problem.
2. **Mechanism / story** — show how the problem unfolds in life, text, body, history or system.
3. **Project articulation** — explain what Potatoism / Tim's framework contributes.
4. **Comparison** — bring in Bible, Jesus, Daoism, Buddhism, Norse, esoteric thought, science or history when it genuinely sharpens the question.
5. **Boundary** — say what the comparison does *not* establish.
6. **Invitation** — give the reader a meaningful next direction; navigation is last, not first.

## Recurring wisdom to distribute across the site

- **Burial before ascent:** seed, potato, root and Divine Kneel all reverse prestige. What is low may be generative.
- **Fruit test:** titles, revelations, models and authorities are judged downstream by what they produce.
- **Gardener ethic:** power is legitimate when it cultivates capability, truth, agency and future life rather than permanent dependency.
- **Door:** relation can be real without collapsing difference.
- **House vs Shell:** healthy containment protects difference and passage; unhealthy containment raises exit cost and captures attention/value.
- **Garden vs Swamp:** life-supporting complexity versus recurrence that consumes the capacity needed to leave it.
- **Forge:** pressure is useful only when the result becomes cooler, narrower, more transferable and more repair-capable.
- **Root:** hidden history matters, but sediment, repetition and lore are not the source.
- **Soil:** memory can preserve what died without demanding that the old form stay alive.
- **Attention is not meaning:** visibility, repetition and outrage can organize culture without becoming truth.
- **Money is not value:** financial power matters because it can mobilize real capability, but value is broader than price.
- **Map is not territory:** symbol, model, scientific mechanism and external fact must remain distinguishable.
- **Independence with relation:** growth should increase capability and chosen relation rather than isolation or capture.
- **Love across difference:** unity should not require sameness; relation should not erase the other.
- **Humility is not self-erasure:** Divine Kneel bends power toward service without abolishing responsibility.
- **Death / return:** endings may be routing events—release, compost, transformation, inheritance or seed—without romanticizing actual harm.

## QA rubric for every major page

Before calling a page finished, ask:

- [ ] Did the reader learn something substantive in the first screen?
- [ ] Is there at least one memorable proposition or scene?
- [ ] Does the page explain a mechanism, not just list nouns?
- [ ] Does it connect project thought to a larger human question?
- [ ] Are comparisons illuminating rather than confirmatory?
- [ ] Are external facts, project theology, autobiography and creative interpretation distinguishable?
- [ ] Can a reader disagree and still learn something?
- [ ] Does the page end with a meaningful next question rather than just more navigation?
