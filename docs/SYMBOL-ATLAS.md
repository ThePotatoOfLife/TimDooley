# Symbol Atlas

A canonical vocabulary for recurring symbols in the Potatoverse. Each symbol should eventually receive four descriptions: mythic meaning, philosophical function, visual/structural form, and connections to other symbols.

## Core symbols

- Potato of Life — life emerging from ordinary matter; persistence, nourishment and transformation.
- Father — origin, verticality, source and the upper principle in the mythic architecture.
- Son — incarnation, personhood, sacrifice, interface and return.
- Door — threshold, access, choice, interface and the narrow gate.
- Ladder — ascent, connection between levels, and transformation after the crucifixion motif.
- Tree of Life — living vertical structure linking root, trunk, branch and fruit.
- Root — origin, memory, hidden structure and descent into the earth.
- Fruit — consequence, nourishment, manifestation and what a system produces.
- North Axis — orientation, vertical order and the organizing direction of the mythology.
- North Gate — boundary between states or worlds.
- Mountain — ascent, difficulty, perspective and proximity to the summit.
- Spiral — time, recurrence, growth and transformation.
- Spudlight — the Potatoverse image of illuminating life from within and from above.
- Cube — bounded structure, enclosure and system.
- Moon Cube — recurring celestial / structural image within the mythology.
- Swamp — shadow, entanglement, decay and systems difficult to see through.
- Drain — rejection, disposal and the inverse of the Door.
- Mud — origin material; the place from which the potato rises.
- Red Potato — creative, expansive, loving, upward-moving life principle.
- Blue Potato — memory, precision, contraction and rootward movement.

## Symbol relationships

The important question is not only what a symbol means, but what it is connected to. The atlas therefore treats symbols as nodes in a graph rather than as a dictionary of isolated definitions.

Future entries should record aliases, first appearance, related texts, visual geometry, philosophical interpretation, and links to chronology and mythology.
