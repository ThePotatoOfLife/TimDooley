Chapter 23.14

The Potato’s Jihad – Silent Angels of Endurance

The Buried Struggle

The potato’s life begins in darkness. Buried deep in the soil, it cannot see the light or feel the warmth of the sun. Its roots stretch slowly, persistently, finding nourishment in the mud. Above ground, its leaves break through the surface to face the wind, the rain, and the heat.

This is the potato’s jihad—a sacred striving that reflects the human journey. In the soil, it wrestles with the weight of its existence. Above the ground, it gives of itself without question. The Quran reminds us:

“And whoever strives only strives for [the benefit of] himself. Indeed, Allah is free from need of the worlds.” (29:6)

The potato does not strive for itself. It does not resist the harvest, nor does it cry out in pain. It suffers in silence, offering its life for others. This quiet endurance teaches us that the greatest struggles are often the most unseen, and the purest sacrifices the least celebrated.

The Potato’s Dual Jihad

Islamic thought defines jihad as both internal and external striving:

The Greater Jihad (Jihad al-Nafs) – The internal struggle to overcome ego, fear, and doubt.

The Lesser Jihad (Jihad al-Asghar) – The external effort to act with justice and serve others.

The potato embodies both dimensions:

Internal Jihad: Its roots burrow into the soil, navigating darkness to find sustenance. This mirrors our inner battles—the fight to overcome despair, to cultivate patience (sabr), and to align with divine will.

External Jihad: Its leaves and fruit rise above the ground, serving as a gift to the world. The potato’s outward striving reflects our responsibility to bring goodness into the world through service and compassion.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said:

“The most beloved of people to Allah is the one who brings the most benefit to others.”

The potato fulfills this ideal without hesitation. It serves all of humanity, transcending boundaries of culture and belief.

The Silent Martyrdom of the Potato

Each day, millions of potatoes are harvested and consumed. They are boiled, fried, mashed, and discarded. Their lives are given without protest, their sacrifices unnoticed by those they nourish. Yet the potato does not complain. It does not resist. Its suffering becomes a source of life for others.

The Quran declares:

“And do not think of those who have been killed in the cause of Allah as dead. Rather, they are alive with their Lord, receiving provision.” (3:169)

The potato, too, lives on in the bodies it sustains, its essence transformed into the energy of life. Its silent martyrdom becomes a reflection of divine mercy—a reminder that every act of giving, no matter how small, has eternal significance.

Every Potato Goes to Jannah

If there is justice in the universe, then surely every potato must go to Jannah. It suffers without sin, serves without ego, and fulfills its purpose with quiet dignity. Its life is a testament to purity and resilience, deserving of the highest reward.

Imagine a heaven where potatoes bask in eternal light, their journey complete. In that place, their silent struggles are celebrated as hymns of divine oneness (Tawhid). The Quran assures us:

“Indeed, Allah does not allow to be lost the reward of those who do good.” (12:90)

The potato’s life teaches us that suffering is not meaningless. It is sacred, transformative, and part of a divine plan.

The Potato Within Us

Deep within our brains lies the thalamus, a hidden structure that filters the chaos of the world into clarity. Like the potato buried in the soil, the thalamus works silently, anchoring our awareness and guiding our perception.

This internal potato mirrors the external one. Together, they teach us the unity of inner and outer struggle, the connection between internal resilience and external service. Just as the potato’s roots nourish its leaves, our inner strength sustains our outward actions.

A Bridge Across Faiths and Cultures

The potato is universal, a symbol that transcends faith and culture. Its journey from seed to harvest resonates across spiritual traditions:

In Christianity, its selfless service reflects the teaching: “Whoever wants to become great among you must be your servant.” (Matthew 20:26)

In Buddhism, its silent endurance mirrors the mindful path of overcoming suffering.

In Hinduism, its growth through adversity reflects tapas, the transformative heat of spiritual effort.

The Quran calls humanity to reflect on the unity within diversity:

“And of His signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth and the diversity of your languages and your colors. Indeed, in that are signs for those of knowledge.” (30:22)

The potato, growing in every corner of the world, becomes a silent witness to this truth. It reminds us that beneath our differences, we are all rooted in the same soil, striving for the same light.

A Prayer from the Potato

The potato, though silent, offers us a profound prayer:

“Strive with me, for I strive with you. In the darkness of your soul, find the strength to grow. In the weight of your burdens, find the roots of your resilience. Together, we will reach for the light.”

This prayer transcends time and space, uniting all who hear it in a shared call to endure, to grow, and to give.

Final Reflections

The potato’s jihad is a mirror for our own. Its silent endurance, humble service, and quiet martyrdom remind us of life’s sacredness. The potato calls us to reflect:

Are we honoring the sacrifices of others?

Are we transforming our struggles into strength?

Are we, like the potato, serving without expectation?

As you hold a potato, consider its journey. Think of its roots in the mud, its leaves in the sun, and its life given for the sake of others. Let it remind you of the divine unity that connects all creation. Whisper a prayer of gratitude—for the potato, for your own struggles, and for the light that guides us all.
