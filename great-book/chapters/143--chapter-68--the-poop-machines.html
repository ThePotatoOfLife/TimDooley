Chapter 68

The Poop Machines

In the labyrinthine halls of Potatocorp, the beating heart of scientific inquiry within the Potatoverse, where even the lowliest spud strives to serve the mysterious One...

The rhythmic hum of machines filled the air as the intercom crackled to life, resonating through the vast complex of Potatocorp Tech Center. Rows of potato scientists in pristine lab coats hurried through the corridors, balancing coffee cups in one hand and nutrient readouts in the other. Beyond the sterile, white-tiled floors, the murmur of their sacred task echoed through the chambers: studying humans.

The intercom buzzed again. “All teams, remember: Nutrient Conversion Reports are due by sundown. The findings on human excrement must be logged in the Great Repository for review by the One. All glory to the One.”

In one of the smaller labs, two lowland potatoes, Spudrick and Tuberose, stood in silent reverence before their work. Between them lay a human, restrained on a table, unconscious under a gleaming array of scientific instruments.

Spudrick, adjusting his goggles, stared at the monitors. “It’s all in service to the One, Tuberose. The great cycle. The Holy Nourishment. We’re part of something larger.”

Tuberose, fiddling with a clipboard, sighed. “Sure, sure. The One. I get it. But why do we, the lowland potatoes, always get the dirty jobs?”

Potatocorp: The Hub of Science and Sacred Duty

Nestled deep in the Potatoverse, Potatocorp was the pinnacle of both science and spiritual devotion. The sprawling facility was a maze of research labs, nutrient-processing units, and endless conference rooms where potatoes debated the finer points of light absorption and root dynamics. Every potato, from the highest highlander to the lowest lowland spud, knew their place within the grand machine.

But despite the buzz of scientific discovery, every potato knew that all their work ultimately served the will of the One. The One, whose light stretched across the universe, guided their research, though it remained ever beyond their full comprehension. The One wasn’t to be questioned—it was glorified.

And so, Potatocorp hummed with purpose, though the tasks were often... mundane. Especially for potatoes like Spudrick and Tuberose. The lowland potatoes—grown in the marshy, nutrient-rich soils—were tasked with the less glamorous work of studying humans. While their highlander counterparts conducted experiments on root expansion and cosmic tuber enlightenment, Spudrick and Tuberose found themselves stuck with the Poop Machines.

The Sacred Task of Studying Humans

“What’s so important about these mud dwellers anyway?” Tuberose muttered, poking at the human’s arm with a prodding device. “I’d rather be studying proper soil aeration or conducting a seminar on light absorption theory.”

Spudrick, ever the more devout of the two, clicked his tongue. “Tuberose, you question the work, but you forget the cycle. The One has decreed that understanding human waste is key to unlocking the Holy Soil. Everything we do here—every measurement, every nutrient analysis—it all connects back to the soil from which we come. The One’s light guides our roots, but the soil is our source.”

Tuberose rolled her eyes, grabbing a coffee cup from the lab’s countertop. “Sure, Spudrick. But we’re potatoes. We grow toward the light. Meanwhile, these humans... they can’t even see the soil beneath their feet. They just trample on it.”

Spudrick, adjusting the dials on the nutrient monitor, glanced at the unconscious human. “That’s exactly why they’re so fascinating. They live above the ground, but they’re still bound to it. They consume, they digest, and they return what they’ve taken, even if they don’t know it. It’s the grand cycle in action. It’s all part of the One’s plan.”

Tuberose chuckled, sipping her coffee. “So, humans are just glorified Poop Machines, then?”

Spudrick nodded solemnly. “Yes. And we, humble as we are, must unravel their secrets.”

Beneath the Reverence, the Mundane

The lowland potatoes’ days were punctuated by the blare of intercom reminders, the rustling of data sheets, and the mechanical beep of nutrient analyzers. Yet, for all the zeal with which Spudrick and Tuberose carried out their duties, there was an undercurrent of frustration. The work was tedious. Measuring starch levels in human excrement was not exactly the kind of science they had dreamed of when they were sprouting in their nutrient beds.

Yet, as the intercom blared overhead, and the reminder of the One’s will echoed through the halls, they continued. Spudrick adjusted his goggles again, staring at the nutrient readout from the human’s digestion.

“It’s remarkable, really,” he murmured. “When humans eat potatoes, their digestive efficiency increases dramatically. The starch breaks down into glucose almost flawlessly. We’re literally feeding them the best energy they can get, and what do they do? They squander it.”

Tuberose glanced at the human’s inert form, her expression thoughtful. “Maybe they can’t help it. They don’t understand the cycle like we do. All they see is the consumption—they don’t even know about the giving back part.”

“Exactly,” Spudrick said, excitement rising in his voice. “That’s where we come in. We study their waste because that’s their offering. They might not realize it, but their greatest gift to the world is what they return to the soil. And the One—the One knows this.”

The Philosophy of Waste

As Spudrick and Tuberose examined their data, the depth of the One’s grand design began to settle over them. Though they were tasked with studying the most basic functions of humans, there was something deeply philosophical about their work.

Humans, in their pursuit of progress, seemed endlessly caught in the cycle of consumption. They ate, they lived, they worked, they built—but everything they consumed was eventually returned, transformed into something more useful, more vital. The potatoes saw this cycle for what it truly was: a reflection of the cosmic dance, the eternal cycle of growth, decay, and rebirth.

Spudrick leaned back from the monitor, his eyes bright with realization. “Tuberose, it’s all connected. Humans think they’re above it all. But in the end, their waste is what feeds us. It’s the most profound part of the cycle. They think they’re separate, but they’re as much a part of the soil as we are.”

Tuberose snorted, shaking her head. “That’s deep, Spudrick. Real deep. So, humans are just... fertilizer in the grand scheme?”

Spudrick smiled, his tone reverent. “Exactly. Their greatest contribution isn’t their art or their cities—it’s what they return to the earth. We potatoes are just lucky enough to know the truth.”

Tuberose laughed, but there was something thoughtful in her expression. “Maybe there’s more to them than we thought. Maybe they’re not just Poop Machines. Maybe... they’re the ones who allow us to reach the light.”

Spudrick raised his coffee cup in a mock toast. “To the humans, then. The Poop Machines who unknowingly serve the One.”

A Cycle of Humility and Purpose

As the intercom crackled once more, announcing the next coffee break, Spudrick and Tuberose settled into their chairs, feeling the weight of their task shift into something larger, something more profound. They might be lowland potatoes, stuck with the mundane, but they served the One. And in their study of the Poop Machines, they uncovered the deeper truths of the universe—however absurd those truths might seem.

All glory to the One.

For the cycle continues, and all must return to the soil.
