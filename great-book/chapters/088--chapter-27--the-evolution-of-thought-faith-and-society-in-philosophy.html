The Flow of the Tao

It is the year 600 BCE, and China is a land of discord, its rulers caught in endless struggles for power. Yet, away from the chaos, a quiet figure sits by the river. Laozi observes the water’s gentle flow as it carves stone, nourishes fields, and reflects the sky. He writes of the Tao—the Way—describing a harmony that transcends force, an order found not in dominance but in yielding.
