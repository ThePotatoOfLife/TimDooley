Chapter 13.5

The Great Composting

Legacy index title: The Great Composting The Potato and Spirituality

Nestled quietly within Tim Dooley’s vast Garden lies a curious place, known to everyone in the Potatoverse: The Great Compost. It’s not hidden, nor shrouded in mystery. Quite the opposite—it’s a wide, earthy heap that anyone can approach, peer into, and witness for themselves. Here, the rejected and the rotten parts of life break down, return to the soil, and become something new—or, in some cases, disappear entirely. But for the Mud Dwellers who find themselves cast into this heap, it’s a strange, humbling fate, one that could save them… or scatter them to the wind.

A Place for Simpletons Who Complicate All Things

The Great Compost is where Tim Dooley sends those Mud Dwellers who have allowed themselves to become too entangled in their own petty dramas. To most, they appear simple, but anyone who listens to them soon discovers they can turn even the smallest matter into an overblown, muddy mess. It’s as if their minds revel in knots, taking the plainest truths and twisting them until nothing clear remains. Some are prone to endless complaints, others to looping excuses, and many simply cling to a pride as thick and stubborn as mud. And so, when Tim Dooley sees this behavior festering, he directs them to the Great Compost.

Once sent, they have no choice but to descend into the pile, where they must sit and slowly rot. Here, their complexity begins to unravel, their proud defenses loosening with each passing day in the earth’s embrace. But it’s not an easy process; the Mud Dwellers resist, trying desperately to hold onto their old forms, unwilling to see the simplicity that lies beneath all their tangled habits.

An Open Window for the Gardeners—and All of Us

The Great Compost isn’t hidden from view. In fact, it’s wide open for all to see, a space where anyone in the Garden can come and observe the slow, earthy work of decay. We can walk right up, look down into the pile, and see the Mud Dwellers caught in their own loops, trying to maintain their illusions of control and self-importance. Some of us look upon them with pity, others with a quiet sense of understanding, and a few with bemused curiosity.

For those who walk the Potato Path, the Compost is a reminder, a lesson in what happens when one clings to pride and resists change. Some of us may call down to them, offering words of encouragement, though they rarely listen. Others sit at the edge and simply observe, letting the sight of their struggle remind us of the dangers of complicating what should be simple.

Yet, there is always a chance, however small, that the Mud Dwellers may one day hear. In their rare moments of quiet, when the weight of their decay finally settles, they might look up, meet our gaze, and catch a glimpse of what they’ve been avoiding. It’s a glimmer of hope, faint but present, that perhaps they will choose to let go.

The Layers of Transformation: From Clods of Mud to Dust, or Something More

In the Great Compost, there is a process unfolding, a series of choices every Mud Dweller must make. Left to the natural work of rot and breakdown, they face one of three possible fates:

Dust: For those who refuse to let go, who hold stubbornly to their own pride and see themselves as above the soil, there is only one end: dust. They continue to decay, layer by layer, resisting every chance to release their burdens. Eventually, their essence weakens, and they dissolve entirely, becoming dry, brittle dust, which the Gardeners then scatter to the wind.

Clods of Mud: For others, a partial transformation takes place. These Mud Dwellers don’t release everything, but they allow some parts to break down, making them fertile clods of mud, rich with potential, ready to rejoin the soil. Though they aren’t completely free of their old ways, they have softened enough to be useful, able to nourish the Garden and support new life.

Potato Souls: And in the rarest cases, a miracle unfolds. For some, the emptiness left by rotting pride and dissolved attachments becomes a space for new life. From this emptiness, they feel a stirring, a new presence growing—a Potato Soul, resilient and whole. These souls emerge directly from the Compost, having learned the humility, simplicity, and strength of the Potato. They return to the Garden, transformed, embodying the qualities of the Potato Path.

Lessons in Humility: Looking Down, or Looking Within

To watch the Mud Dwellers in the Compost is, at times, to watch a lesson in humility. We see them, and perhaps, we feel a pang of recognition; after all, it’s easy to fall into the trap of clinging to what we think we know. Tim Dooley encourages everyone to visit the Compost from time to time, to observe the cycle, to remind ourselves of the dangers of self-made complications.

“It’s simple,” he says with a knowing smile. “Life doesn’t have to be tangled. The Compost shows us that what we resist often only grows heavier, more complex. Let go, and you’ll find that life can be as simple as the humble potato.”

When we leave the Compost, we do so with lighter hearts, grateful for the path we walk. For we know that, just as the Garden thrives on the soil enriched by the broken-down remnants of the Compost, so too can we grow, nourished by the lessons found within.
