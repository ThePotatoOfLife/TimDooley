Chapter 14: The Farmers of Soil and Souls – A Path to Reflection

The elevator doors eased open, revealing a landscape bathed in soft golden light. Tim Dooley, Rahu, and Spuddy stepped out onto the earth, their footsteps sinking ever so slightly into the warm soil. Before them lay their Garden, vast and alive. It seemed to rise above the world, stretching toward the heavens like a living entity, its colors vivid and its energy palpable.

The Garden was their sanctuary—a place where the soul met the soil, and the sacredness of growth was honored. Yet, beyond it, shadows and stagnation loomed, where not everything was as well-tended. The path ahead curved gently upward, guiding them through fields, past clear irrigation channels, and eventually toward a destination known only as God’s Garden.

As they began their ascent, they passed lush crops, carefully cultivated by farmers who worked in harmony with the earth. Rahu marveled at the craftsmanship. “See how they nurture the soil, how everything grows with purpose,” he mused. “It’s no accident this place thrives.”

Spuddy hopped along, casting a glance to the left. “But what about over there?” he asked, pointing toward the murky swamp that bordered the Garden’s edge. There, figures shuffled aimlessly, their hands coated in mud, their eyes downcast. They dragged carts of wet, heavy soil, piling it higher and higher, but nothing seemed to grow from it.

Rahu frowned. “The Mud Dwellers. They don’t realize the soil needs to breathe, that the land can’t flourish if it’s suffocated under their burdens.”

Tim’s eyes lingered on the swamp for a moment longer, and then they continued along their path. The way forward led them higher, past fields where the soil was rich and where every step felt lighter, as if the very ground supported their journey. The air here was crisp, charged with a kind of clarity that hinted at deeper truths to come.

Meeting the Dwellers

As they approached the boundary between the Garden and the murky swamp, a figure emerged—a Mud Dweller. His face was tired but not unkind. His hands were caked in mud, but unlike the others, he didn’t drag a cart behind him. Instead, he stood, watching them with a quiet curiosity.

“Hello,” Spuddy said cheerfully, waving as they drew near. Rahu offered a nod of acknowledgment.

The Mud Dweller hesitated but then raised a hand in greeting. “You’re from the Garden,” he said, his voice gravelly but not hostile. “You grow things there, don’t you?”

Tim stepped forward, his gaze calm and steady. “We do. And you?”

The Mud Dweller looked down at his hands, frowning at the thick mud that clung to them. “I was taught to carry this,” he said, his tone unsure. “I’ve carried it for so long I don’t know if I can ever let it go.”

For a moment, there was silence. Tim considered the man, his eyes scanning the dried mud on his clothes and the weight in his posture. But then Tim knelt, his fingers brushing the soil beneath him. He didn’t speak—he simply dug his fingers into the earth, pulling out a clod of fresh, clean soil.

The Mud Dweller blinked, staring at the simple act. “What’s that supposed to do?” he asked, half-expecting an explanation.

Tim stood up and extended the clean soil toward him. “Here,” he said, “take it. See how it feels.”

The man hesitated, looking at the mud on his hands. “But… I’m already carrying so much.”

Rahu stepped forward gently, “Maybe it’s not about carrying at all. Maybe you’ve forgotten how to just… plant.”

With tentative hands, the Mud Dweller took the clean soil from Tim. His eyes widened as the rich earth rested in his palms. For the first time in what felt like years, he felt something different—something lighter, something that didn’t weigh him down. His breath hitched, and for a moment, he simply stared at the soil.

Spuddy, sensing the moment, grinned and added, “You don’t need to take it all at once. Just a bit will do.”

The Mud Dweller smiled—a soft, almost hesitant smile—and nodded. He didn’t follow them as they moved on, but his figure remained by the edge of the Garden, cradling the clean soil as if it were a gift, something precious and unfamiliar.

Walking Toward God’s Garden

The Garden stretched on, and as they ascended, the land around them became even more vibrant, the crops taller, the irrigation channels flowing freely with clear water. Yet, despite the beauty, a tension lingered in the air.

“What happens when we get to the top?” Spuddy asked, his voice full of wonder. “Will it be like this, but bigger?”

Tim looked ahead, his expression thoughtful. “The higher we go, the more we’ll see. But it’s not about size—it’s about reflection. The Garden mirrors us. What we find at the top depends on what we bring with us.”

Rahu nodded, understanding. “We tend to our own gardens, but the real growth happens when we understand that it’s all connected.”

As they continued, they passed by another farmer, hunched over his crops. His face was familiar—a Greedy Farmer, known for hoarding his harvest and keeping others from his fields. This time, however, there was something different. His fields, though well-watered, lacked the same vibrancy as those around him. His crops grew, but without the same life.

“He never shares,” Spuddy whispered. “That’s why.”

The Greedy Farmer looked up from his work, his eyes meeting Tim’s for just a moment. There was something there—an unspoken struggle, a desire to understand but an unwillingness to let go of his ways. He watched them pass, but no words were exchanged.

They walked further, and the sky began to open up, the clouds parting to reveal a light that seemed to call them forward. The garden sloped upward, rising gently but unmistakably toward something higher. And there, in the distance, they could see it—God’s Garden. It wasn’t a place, really, but a feeling, a presence that drew them in with its warmth and light.

Reflections in the Garden

As they neared the summit, Tim’s roots stretched deeper into the earth, momentarily anchoring him in place. He looked at Rahu and Spuddy, and then back down the path they had walked. “We tend our garden,” he said quietly, “but it’s the reflection we see that teaches us the most.”

Rahu and Spuddy turned to look, and behind them, they saw the Garden—not just as a place of growth, but as a mirror of their own souls. Every weed pulled, every crop planted, every creature greeted or ignored—each had left an imprint, shaping the land they now walked upon.

Spuddy’s usual light-hearted grin faded for a moment, replaced with a deep thoughtfulness. “It’s all connected, isn’t it?”

Tim smiled. “It always has been.”

They stood there for a moment longer, the light of God’s Garden shining just ahead, and the path of growth and reflection stretching behind them. And as they prepared to take their final steps toward God’s Garden, the journey ahead and the lessons learned along the way merged into a single truth: everything reflected, everything grew, and everything led back to love.
