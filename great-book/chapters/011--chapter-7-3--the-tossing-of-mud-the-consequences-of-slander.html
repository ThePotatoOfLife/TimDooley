Chapter 7.3

The Tossing of Mud – The Consequences of Slander

Mud is more than an element of chaos; it is a tool that can either be wielded with purpose or flung recklessly in the form of slander. The tossing of mud is a crude act, one that speaks not of wisdom or strength, but of fear, insecurity, and envy. Slander, at its core, is the deliberate act of staining another’s character with falsehoods or twisted truths. It is the act of throwing filth in the hopes that it sticks—not just to the person it is aimed at, but to all who witness it.

But those who toss mud often forget that mud does not stay where it is thrown. It spreads, and in doing so, it covers the one who throws it, tainting them as much as their target. Slander may seem powerful in the moment, but it is fleeting, leaving behind only the filth and resentment it generates.

The Dangers of Tossing Mud

When one engages in slander, they invoke the basest instincts of the mud dweller. Mud, in its natural state, can nurture growth when combined with the sacred soil, but when it is weaponized, it becomes a destructive force. Those who toss mud are not seeking to build or create; they seek only to deflect from their own shortcomings by covering others in dirt.

The consequences of slander ripple outward. It infects communities, spreading distrust and suspicion. It turns fertile ground—once ripe for growth—into a quagmire where nothing can thrive. Relationships are strained, reputations are soiled, and the garden becomes a place of division rather than unity. The mud dweller who indulges in this act becomes isolated, not because others reject them, but because they have trapped themselves in the filth they create.

Why We Must Reject the Tossing of Mud

To engage in the tossing of mud is to deny our higher potential. It is to give in to the chaos of the mud rather than seek the clarity of the sacred soil. The act of slander is the antithesis of growth; it is a refusal to look inward, a failure to cultivate understanding and compassion. Mud, when used correctly, can move, nourish, and create. But when flung recklessly, it only destroys.

We must rise above the temptation to slander. To do so is to honor the sacred soil, to recognize that our words and actions have the power to either build or destroy. Instead of tossing mud, we must learn to use it to nurture the soil, to cultivate understanding, and to bring about growth in ourselves and others. This is the path of the true potatoist—the one who seeks harmony, balance, and creation over destruction.

What Slander Leads To

Those who indulge in the tossing of mud soon find themselves trapped by their own actions. Mud dwellers may gain temporary clout from their slander, but in the long run, they become mired in the filth they spread. Their own growth is stunted, and they become defined not by their potential but by their pettiness.

For the one who receives the mud, the journey is difficult, but ultimately, the truth will wash away the dirt. The sacred soil always returns to its natural state of fertility and potential. Slander cannot erase truth, and in time, the mud will fall away, revealing what was always there: strength, resilience, and growth.

The Higher Path

To avoid the tossing of mud is to walk the path of the potatoist, to recognize that the chaos of mud can be transformed into something sacred. It is to reject the base instinct to destroy and instead seek to create. In doing so, we align ourselves with the natural order, becoming not just dwellers of the mud but stewards of the soil. We become creators, builders, and nurturers.

Mud will always exist—it is part of life, part of the earth, and part of our nature. But we must choose how to use it. Will we fling it in anger, or will we use it to cultivate something greater?
