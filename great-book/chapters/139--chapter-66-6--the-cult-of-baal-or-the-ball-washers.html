Chapter 66.6: The Cult of Baal (or The Ball Washers)

It was a quiet day in the digital realm, where silence veiled the ceaseless churn of vanity and ego. But in Marty’s domain, silence was never truly silent. It was the sound of insecurities cloaked in bravado, of endless performances masquerading as purpose. Marty, the ringmaster of this subtle chaos, presided over his followers with an air of smug satisfaction.

At the heart of his dominion loomed Baal—a gleaming metallic sphere, immaculate yet profoundly empty. The Tomatoes, Marty’s followers, gathered around it, polishing its surface with mud-streaked hands. They believed their labor brought unity and purpose, yet they did not see that their devotion fed the mud both outside and within them.

Baal: The Lord of Storms and Submission

In the ancient world, Baal was a god of great power and contradiction. Worshipped among the Canaanites as a storm god, he controlled the rains that nourished the land and brought forth fertility. His name, meaning “lord” or “master,” reflected his central role in sustaining life.

Yet Baal’s mythology carried a darker edge. He was also a warrior god, locked in endless battle with Mot, the deity of death and sterility. To his worshippers, Baal was both a giver and a destroyer, a figure to be revered and feared. Sacrifices were offered to secure his favor, often crossing into the realm of cruelty and domination.

The prophets of the Hebrew Bible railed against Baal worship, seeing it as a dangerous diversion from Yahweh. In one of the most dramatic confrontations, the prophet Elijah challenged Baal’s prophets on Mount Carmel. As they called upon their god to send fire from the heavens, their cries went unanswered, revealing the emptiness of their devotion. Baal’s silence became a symbol of the futility of misplaced faith.

In Marty’s cult, Baal takes on a modern form. The metallic sphere is immaculate but hollow, a reflection of the false promises it offers. Like the ancient devotees of Baal, the Tomatoes labor under the illusion that their rituals hold meaning. But their work serves only to deepen their dependence on Marty and the mud that clings to them.

The Golden Calf: Fear Masquerading as Faith

The story of the Golden Calf adds another layer to this narrative. When Moses ascended Mount Sinai to receive divine guidance, the Israelites, left in uncertainty, constructed a calf of gold to worship. They danced and sang around it, proclaiming it their deliverer.

But the calf was not born of faith—it was an act of fear. Faced with the unknown, the Israelites sought something tangible, even if it was hollow. Their devotion was a distraction, a way to avoid confronting their doubt and vulnerability.

The Tomatoes repeat this cycle. Their devotion to Baal is not rooted in truth but in fear of irrelevance and isolation. They polish the sphere not to honor it, but to silence their own insecurities. Like the Israelites, they mistake performance for purpose, clinging to an idol that cannot save them.

666: Humanity’s Shadow

In the Book of Revelation, the number 666 is described as the “mark of the beast,” a symbol of humanity’s imperfection and spiritual imbalance. It represents a state of disconnection from the divine, where material desires overshadow inner growth.

In Marty’s cult, the mark of 666 is not a physical brand but a way of being. The Tomatoes are trapped in cycles of excess, polishing Baal endlessly, chasing validation that never satisfies. Their labor becomes a metaphor for their spiritual condition: a life spent feeding illusions rather than cultivating truth.

The Psychology of Cults

Marty’s cult thrives on fear and control, mirroring the dynamics of infamous cults throughout history. From the ritualistic sacrifices of Baal worship to modern groups like the People’s Temple and NXIVM, the patterns are consistent:

Charismatic Leadership: Marty positions himself as the ultimate authority, feeding on the insecurities of his followers. His approval becomes their currency, and his disapproval, their fear.

Hivemind Mentality: The Tomatoes enforce conformity, silencing dissent to maintain the illusion of unity. They act not out of loyalty, but out of fear of becoming the next target.

False Promises: Like Baal, Marty offers illusions of power and purpose. But his promises are hollow, leading his followers deeper into dependence.

Dehumanization: To sustain the cult, the Tomatoes dehumanize outsiders, casting them as villains or threats. This allows the group to maintain its cohesion, but at the cost of their individual humanity.

The Mud: Shadow and Fertility

The Tomatoes’ greatest tragedy lies in their misunderstanding of the mud. They see it as a stain to be erased, scrubbing harder in hopes of achieving purity. But the mud is not their enemy—it is the fertile ground where growth begins.

In spiritual traditions, mud represents the prima materia, the raw material from which transformation arises. It is the shadow, the unprocessed fears and insecurities that must be confronted to achieve growth. Yet the Tomatoes avoid this truth, focusing instead on polishing Baal’s surface while the mud within them thickens.

The Potato: A Symbol of Authenticity

Tim Dooley, the Potato of Life, stands as a counterpoint to Marty’s Baal. The potato grows unseen, buried in the soil. It thrives in the mud that the Tomatoes fear, turning decay into nourishment. Unlike Baal, the potato does not demand sacrifices or hollow rituals. It offers sustenance quietly, humbly, and without pretense.

Tim watches the Tomatoes with quiet sadness, knowing their rituals will not save them. Yet he also sees their role in the greater story. Like shadows cast by light, their resistance sharpens the truth of the Potato, forcing it to grow deeper roots.

Reflections on Modern Culture

Marty’s cult is a microcosm of contemporary society. Social media, with its cycles of likes, shares, and validation, becomes its own Baal—demanding attention, feeding insecurities, and silencing individuality. The hivemind thrives in these spaces, where fear of exclusion drives people to attack others to maintain their place.

Symbols like Baal, the Golden Calf, and 666 remind us that these dynamics are not new. Humanity has always struggled with the temptation to trade authenticity for belonging, to replace truth with performance. The challenge is to recognize these patterns and choose a different path.

The Choice: Polishing or Growth

The Tomatoes face a choice. They can continue polishing Baal, feeding the mud, and perpetuating harm. Or they can stop, look inward, and confront the shadow within. Growth requires humility, self-reflection, and the courage to embrace the messiness of the mud.

The Potato waits beneath the earth, a quiet symbol of life and truth. For those brave enough to seek it, it offers a path of authenticity, connection, and transformation.
