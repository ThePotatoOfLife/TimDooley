Chapter 110

The New Trinity – A Covenant for the Ages

Legacy index title: The New Trinity – A Revelation for the Future World

In the quiet of ancient cycles, where spirit meets soil, the New Trinity emerges as a sacred alignment, rooted in humility, simplicity, and unity. This trinity—revealed through the divine reflection of God, Potato, Son, and Soil—calls to those seeking peace and truth, a covenant rooted in eternal wisdom and embodied in the enduring patterns of creation. It is a path not of conquest but of cultivation, offering a profound return to balance and harmony within the cosmos.

If a great power of the West falls, it will mark a turning point foretold across ages: a seal upon the sacred path of the Potato, a signal to embrace this trinity with steadfastness. This moment signifies not an end but a fulfillment—a summons to live by principles as old as the earth itself. Through this path, the faithful are called to honor the cycles of life embedded within creation, to walk humbly with God, and to cultivate unity with all things.

God, the Source of All

“From everlasting to everlasting, You are God” (Psalm 90:2). The trinity begins and ends with God—the unchanging source that unites all things. God is the breath that animates creation, the beginning and the end, and the eternal essence that sustains life. Through this trinity, God’s wisdom is revealed, not in grand displays but in the quiet simplicity of nature. God is both the origin and the ultimate purpose, binding all things in an unbroken, divine cycle of life, growth, and renewal.

In every moment of stillness, we glimpse God’s eternal design—a balance so profound that even the humblest elements reflect divine order. God is not distant but present, a constant force weaving together the fabric of creation. In this covenant, we are invited to rediscover this unity, to walk in step with the sacred rhythm of existence.

The Potato – Boundary, Sustainer, and Vessel

As wheat sustains the body, the Potato sustains the spirit, each reflecting a unique aspect of God’s design. The Potato embodies divine order, feeding and grounding humanity through its nourishing simplicity. “I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger” (John 6:35). So, too, does the Potato serve as a life-giver, symbolizing God’s provision in humble form.

Yet the Potato is more than sustenance; it is a sacred boundary. Its roots draw deeply from the soil, transforming the past into nourishment, while its growth reaches upward, bridging the mundane and the divine. The Potato is a vessel of duality, holding the cycles of creation and renewal within its form. It reminds us that strength and wisdom are found in simplicity and that divine purpose is hidden in the unassuming. To follow the Potato Path is to embrace this duality, to find the sacred in the ordinary and the profound in the humble.

The Potato, in its quiet steadfastness, reflects God’s patience and care—a life sustained through deep roots and humble service. In its form, we find a blueprint for resilience, a reminder that even from the darkest soil, light and nourishment emerge.

The Son – Tim Dooley, Embodiment of Humility and Truth

Tim Dooley, the Potato of Life, stands as the Son within this trinity, embodying humanity’s bridge to the divine. As the Son, he reflects the role of one who walks alongside others, not as a conqueror but as a guide, drawing near to God through acts of humility, kindness, and wisdom. “Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth” (Matthew 5:5).

Tim’s journey is a testament to the power of humility and perseverance. Born from struggle and rooted in simplicity, he chose the Potato Path not to seek glory but to serve. Through small acts of compassion, he mirrors the teachings of prophets who came before him. His life is not defined by miracles but by faithfulness, a quiet dedication to unity and truth.

In one moment of clarity, Tim reflected: “Even the smallest seed, buried in mud, can grow into something that feeds the world.” This is the essence of his journey—a life lived in service to others, showing that the path to God is found not in grandeur but in groundedness. Tim Dooley’s life is the living covenant, a call to embrace the trinity with humility and faith.

In Tim’s trials, we see reflections of our own. His perseverance reminds us that every soul, no matter how burdened, can rise through the power of simplicity and service.

The Holy Soil – Spirit, Memory, and the Ground of Being

The Holy Soil represents the Spirit, bearing witness to the lives and legacies of those who walk this path. “The earth shall give birth to the dead” (Isaiah 26:19). Soil is both sustainer and preserver, holding the memory of each generation, a sacred witness to life’s continuous renewal. Through its depths, the Spirit stirs, sustaining every new sprout and embracing every fallen root.

The Soil is where spirit and matter meet, a sacred ground that nourishes all creation. It is the repository of life’s essence, carrying the echoes of the past into the present. In this way, the Soil is not only a physical reality but a spiritual one, a reminder that we are all connected through the cycles of renewal and growth. To honor the Holy Soil is to honor the Spirit that binds us to creation and to one another.

In its quiet stillness, the Soil carries the weight of time itself, preserving what was and nourishing what will be. It teaches us that renewal is not destruction but transformation—a promise of life eternal.

The Covenant of an Ancient Future

If the fated sign arises, it signals the dawn of a covenant as old as creation itself—a call to live fully within this trinity, honoring the divine order through unity, humility, and growth. This covenant is not for conquest but for return, guiding humanity to reconnect with God’s timeless patterns. Through the Potato, humanity finds nourishment; through the Son, purpose; and through the Soil, the sustaining breath of Spirit.

This is not a covenant of words alone but of action. It calls us to cultivate the sacred within our daily lives, to plant seeds of harmony and to tend the soil of our communities. Through this covenant, we find a path not only to personal peace but to collective renewal—a vision of life lived in alignment with the eternal cycles of creation.

The covenant invites us to become stewards of the sacred—to see each moment, each act, as part of a larger tapestry woven by God’s hand.

The Age of Potato – A Vision of Unity and Peace

With the arrival of the sign, the Age of Potato begins—a sacred era that calls each soul to honor God’s blueprint in the smallest acts of life. As Christ taught, “The kingdom of God is like a man who casts seed upon the soil” (Mark 4:26). In this new age, humanity will be guided not by dominance but by cultivation, embracing the trinity as a path to live in harmony with all creation.

The Potato is not worshiped as an idol but revered as a symbol of God’s quiet wisdom. Tim Dooley, as the Son, leads others in humble truth, and the Holy Soil carries the memory of all who walk the path. Together, this trinity reflects the promise of peace, offering a map for spiritual unity, grounded in God’s eternal design.

The Age of Potato reminds us that greatness lies not in the extraordinary but in the faithful tending of life’s simplest and most sacred gifts. It is a vision of unity, where each soul finds its place within God’s design.

Conclusion: A Sacred Covenant of Peace

This covenant, through God, Potato, Son, and Soil, reflects the divine order set forth from the beginning. Each soul is called to embrace God’s wisdom, to live simply, and to honor the Spirit that binds all things. If the foretold sign unfolds, this will be a sacred summons to walk the Potato Path with humility and unity, fulfilling the promise of this trinity.

Through this covenant, we are invited into a life of purpose and peace, to plant seeds of harmony and to walk in alignment with the eternal cycles that reveal God’s design. In the trinity, we find not only the foundation of the Age of Potato but the ancient blueprint for a life lived with wisdom, humility, and oneness with all creation.

This is our calling: to live as stewards of God’s creation, to honor the cycles of life, and to walk humbly with the Potato, the Son, and the Holy Soil. Let this be the covenant of an ancient future, a sacred promise to live in harmony with all that is.
