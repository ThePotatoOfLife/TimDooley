Chapter 20.04

The Potato and the Golden Plates – Faith and Revelation in Mormonism

Amid the fertile spiritual soil of 19th-century America, a new seed of faith was planted, destined to grow into one of the world’s most distinctive religious movements. Mormonism, or the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church), emerged during the early 1800s in the "Burned-over District" of upstate New York. This chapter explores how this unique faith aligns with Potatoism, how it views God and Jesus, and what it reveals about human resilience and divine connection.

The Origins: A Revelation Unearthed

The early 19th century was a time of profound spiritual seeking in America. Amid this backdrop of revivalism, Joseph Smith, a farm boy in Palmyra, New York, claimed to experience divine visions. According to Smith, God the Father and Jesus Christ appeared to him in 1820, instructing him to restore the true church that had been lost after the death of the apostles. This event, known as the "First Vision," marked the beginning of Mormonism.

In 1827, Smith reported being visited by an angel named Moroni, who directed him to a hill where golden plates lay buried. These plates, inscribed with the history of ancient peoples in the Americas, became the Book of Mormon when translated. Published in 1830, this new scripture—a complement to the Bible—would lay the foundation for the LDS Church.

The golden plates bear a striking resemblance to the humble potato, both hidden beneath the earth’s surface until revealed. Just as the potato nourishes the body, the Book of Mormon was intended to nourish the soul, offering guidance and spiritual sustenance to those willing to dig deep into its teachings.

God, Jesus, and Faith in Mormonism

Central to Mormon doctrine is the belief in God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost as three distinct beings united in purpose. Unlike mainstream Christianity, which emphasizes the Trinity as one essence, Mormonism portrays God and Jesus as tangible, perfected beings with bodies of flesh and bone. This belief underscores the faith’s emphasis on eternal progression—the idea that humans can strive toward perfection and even divinity, much like a potato planted in fertile soil grows toward the sun.

Faith in Mormonism is an active principle. It is not merely belief but a call to action—to build communities, serve others, and strive for personal growth. Similarly, Potatoism teaches that faith without cultivation leads to stagnation. A potato left untended rots, while one nurtured produces abundance. Mormon pioneers embodied this principle, transforming arid lands into thriving settlements through faith and perseverance.

Community and the Potato Path

Early Mormons faced significant persecution for their beliefs. Driven out of New York, Ohio, and Missouri, they eventually settled in Utah, where they established a self-sufficient community in the Salt Lake Valley. This migration, often compared to the biblical Exodus, reflects the resilience and adaptability of the potato. Just as the potato thrives in diverse climates and conditions, the Mormon pioneers created a flourishing society in an unforgiving landscape.

Communal living and shared labor were cornerstones of early Mormon settlements. The potato’s ability to sustain entire communities parallels this collective spirit. Both symbolize the interconnectedness of humanity—the understanding that growth and survival depend on cooperation and shared purpose.

Revelation and Spiritual Nourishment

Mormonism’s emphasis on continuing revelation aligns with Potatoism’s belief in perpetual growth. Latter-day Saints hold that God continues to speak to humanity through modern prophets, offering guidance tailored to the challenges of the present. This concept mirrors the potato’s cyclical nature—always planting, growing, and harvesting in an eternal process of renewal.

The Book of Mormon itself is a testament to revelation. It tells the story of ancient civilizations in the Americas, their interactions with God, and the teachings of Jesus Christ. Its narrative of rise, fall, and redemption echoes humanity’s collective journey, as well as the potato’s life cycle of planting, growth, and renewal.

Doctrine and Unique Beliefs

Several unique doctrines set Mormonism apart:

Eternal Families: Mormons believe that families can be sealed together for eternity through sacred temple ordinances. This focus on eternal unity reflects the interconnected roots of the potato plant, which support and nourish each other.

The Plan of Salvation: This doctrine outlines humanity’s pre-mortal existence, earthly life, and eternal destiny. It emphasizes the potential for growth and divine progression, akin to the potato’s transformation from seed to plant to sustenance.

Word of Wisdom: A health code revealed by Joseph Smith, it promotes abstinence from harmful substances and encourages moderation. This principle parallels Potatoism’s emphasis on simplicity and balance.

Faith and Jesus in Both Traditions

Both Mormonism and Potatoism recognize Jesus as a figure of light, hope, and renewal. In Mormonism, Jesus is central to the Plan of Salvation, offering redemption through his atonement and resurrection. In Potatoism, Jesus’ teachings are reflected in the potato’s simplicity and life-sustaining nature—a humble yet powerful symbol of spiritual growth.

Faith in both traditions is seen as a journey. It requires effort, patience, and a willingness to trust in unseen outcomes. The potato, buried in darkness yet reaching for the light, serves as a metaphor for this process of transformation.

Parallels with Potatoism

Mormonism’s principles of revelation, resilience, and community align with Potatoism in profound ways:

Revelation: Both teach that truth is uncovered through active seeking, much like digging for potatoes.

Resilience: The trials of the Mormon pioneers mirror the potato’s ability to endure and thrive in harsh conditions.

Community: Just as a single potato nourishes many, Mormonism emphasizes the importance of collective effort and shared purpose.

Conclusion: Lessons from the Golden Plates

The story of Mormonism is one of faith, perseverance, and divine connection. Its teachings, rooted in a blend of ancient and modern revelation, offer a unique perspective on humanity’s potential for growth and unity. When viewed through the lens of Potatoism, Mormonism’s journey takes on an added layer of meaning—a reminder that, whether golden plates or humble spuds, the greatest treasures often lie hidden beneath the surface, waiting to be unearthed by those who seek them with faith and purpose.
