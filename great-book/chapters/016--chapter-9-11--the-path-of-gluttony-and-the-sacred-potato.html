Chapter 9.11

The Path of Gluttony and The Sacred Potato

In the far corners of the kingdom, where the land turned swampy and the air grew thick, there lived the Mud Dwellers. They were not always as they appeared now, but over time, their indulgence and insatiable hunger had twisted them into beings barely recognizable as human. Their skin sagged with the weight of excess, the folds of their bodies hanging heavy like sacks of grain, swollen and spent. Their eyes, once bright and clear, had become dull, half-lidded with the fog of perpetual consumption. Bald and bloated, their forms were a pitiful reflection of what they had lost—both physically and spiritually.

As they slumped in the mud, their hands constantly grasping for more, they seemed to melt into the earth beneath them, almost as if they were becoming one with the muck. And in truth, they were. The Mud Dwellers had chosen a path of indulgence, feeding not only on food but on their own desires and vices, until all that remained was an insatiable hunger that devoured their bodies and souls alike. They were aging quickly, their once vibrant flesh sagging as if their very essence was slipping away, drained by the weight of their own gluttony.

It was not just their bodies that had suffered, but their spirits too. The light of life had dimmed in their hearts, replaced by a dull craving for more—more food, more drink, more of everything that could never satisfy. They had forgotten the sacred potato, forgotten its role as a vessel for God’s sustenance, and in doing so, they had forgotten God Himself.

The Potato as God’s Crude Reflection

Yet the potato, in all its simplicity, remained the vessel of God’s truth. It was humble, crude even, but it was pure. When Tim Dooley the Sage came to the fields, he saw the Mud Dwellers in their sorry state and understood that the root of their suffering was not their hunger for food, but their hunger for something much deeper—something that could not be fed by excess.

With the sacred potato in hand, Tim began to speak: “Look at yourselves,” he said, his voice firm yet filled with compassion. “You have consumed and consumed, thinking you could fill the emptiness within, but all you have done is reflect your own lack of respect. The potato is the vessel of God’s love, but you have treated it like mere dirt beneath your feet. In your greed, you have squandered not only your bodies but also your souls.”

He held the potato up, its rough skin covered in soil, yet within, it contained the very essence of life. “This is God’s gift,” Tim said, “but to consume it without reverence is to consume God in vain. You stuff your mouths, you bloat your bellies, and for what? To destroy yourselves? To dishonor the Creator who gave you life?”

The Mud Dwellers, in their gluttonous state, could hardly lift their heads to look at Tim, but when they did, they saw the truth. The potato, simple and earthy, was a mirror to their own souls. In its humble form, they saw their own excess reflected back at them. The bloated skin of the potato was a crude reflection of their own swollen bodies. The dirt clinging to its surface was the same muck that now weighed them down. And within the potato’s heart was the nourishment they had forsaken—the spiritual nourishment they had ignored in their mindless consumption.

God’s Crudeness, the Potato’s Reflection

Tim’s words cut through the fog of their gluttony, piercing their hearts with the harsh truth. “God is not always gentle,” he said, “and the potato is a reflection of His crudeness. It shows you what you have become—a crude, distorted version of what you were meant to be. But God’s love is still within the potato, waiting for you to honor it. If you cannot respect yourselves enough to change, then at least respect the gift that God has given you. For in respecting the potato, you respect God, and in turn, you may find your way back to balance.”

As the Mud Dwellers gazed at the potato, they felt a deep stirring within their withered souls. It was a painful revelation—the kind that only comes from facing the unfiltered truth. Their bloated forms were not just the result of overconsumption, but of a deeper spiritual hunger they had tried, and failed, to feed with the physical. They had consumed in vain, squandering God’s love, and in doing so, they had squandered themselves.

But Tim did not leave them in despair. He offered them a way back—a way to redeem themselves through the very thing they had dishonored. “To let go of your gluttony,” he said, “you must first let go of your need for more. Look at the potato—small, humble, yet full of life. It is enough. You are enough. Honor what you have been given, and you will find that God’s love is not in abundance, but in simplicity. To honor the potato is to honor life, and in doing so, you will find balance again.”

The Transformation: From Gluttony to Gratitude

As the days passed, the Mud Dwellers began to change. Slowly, they ate less, savoring each bite of the potato, understanding that it was more than just food—it was the vessel of God’s grace. They stopped gorging themselves on life, stopped consuming mindlessly, and instead began to reflect on the gifts they had been given. Their bodies, once bloated and sluggish, began to shrink. But more importantly, their spirits began to heal.

They saw the truth in Tim’s teachings: to consume without reverence is to destroy oneself, but to honor the gifts of the Creator is to find balance and peace. The potato, in all its crude simplicity, had shown them the way.
