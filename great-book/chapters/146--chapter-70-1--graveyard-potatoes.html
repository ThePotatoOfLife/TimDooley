Chapter 70.1

Graveyard Potatoes

Legacy index title: Graveyard Potatoes ( Tims Secret Recipe )

A dish for those who ponder the cycle of life and death, where mud and flesh meet in solemn harmony.

Ingredients

The Buried Foundation

4 large baking potatoes

Olive oil (for the ritual anointment)

The Muddy Shroud (Meat Sauce)

2 onions, sliced (make them cry—tears are part of the sacrifice)

3 cloves garlic, minced (for the whisper of spirits)

500g ground beef (honor the cow, for its journey ends with you)

2 tbsp flour (to bind the story together)

500ml water (or enough to thicken fate itself)

1 beef bouillon cube or tallow (the cow’s final gift)

Salt & pepper (to season the past)

Brown caramel food coloring (to summon the graveyard mud aesthetic)

The Final Resting Touches (Garnishes & Variations)

Choose wisely—each garnish tells a different tale:

For those who embrace contrast (Sweet & Savory):

Raisins & fried onions → A daring balance, like life and death in a single bite. (Pineapple on pizza-tier choice, but it has its believers.)

For the seekers of richness and indulgence:

Parmesan or cheddar → The bones of the dish, melted into the grave for deeper comfort.

For the believers in freshness & depth:

Parsley → A whisper of life among the ruins, a natural herby lift.

For those who crave sharpness & acidic contrast:

Side pickles OR pickled beets → A reminder that not all is lost to the grave—there is still bite, still defiance in the aftertaste. (Pick one, for both at once is lunacy.)

Instructions

Step 1: Preparing the Grave

Wash and peel the potatoes, stripping them of their earthly skin.

Massage them gently in olive oil, preparing them for the pyre.

Lay them out like stones upon the baking tray.

Roast at 200°C (392°F) for one hour, until their core is soft, yet their form holds.

Step 2: Conjuring the Mud (Meat Sauce)

Slice the onions and let them weep. Their suffering sweetens them.

Sauté them in a pan until they surrender, turning golden and soft.

Add the minced garlic—let its aroma haunt the dish.

Bring in the ground beef, breaking it apart as it browns. Take a moment to thank the cow.

Once the meat is fully cooked, scatter the flour and stir—it will bind the spirits together.

Pour in the water, stirring patiently as the sauce thickens.

Enrich it with bouillon or tallow, deepening the flavor like the weight of time.

Season with salt, pepper, and a touch of brown caramel food coloring—for a graveyard must be muddy.

Step 3: The Burial

Unearth the baked potatoes from their slumber.

Split them open, revealing their softened core.

Pour the rich, dark, muddy sauce over them.

Step 4: Resurrection (Final Garnish & Choice)

Choose your final touch, but choose wisely:

Sweet & Savory? Raisins & fried onions.

Rich & Comforting? Parmesan or cheddar.

Fresh & Earthy? Parsley.

Sharp & Defiant? Pickles or pickled beets (but never both!).

Final Words:

Eat with reverence. Reflect on what has passed, on the suffering that led to this moment. Let each bite ground you in the fleeting nature of existence, yet also in the comfort of the now.

Rest in flavor.
