Chapter 9.32

The Troublemakers’ Circle

In a forgotten corner of the Potatoverse—where even the faintest light struggles to penetrate a perpetual haze—the Troublemakers’ Circle gathers beneath a cloak of darkness. PKFC, Don Jefe, KaikBaggins, and their band of miscreants are far more than mere pranksters; they are artisans of anguish. Their cruel game is not random mischief but a calculated performance in which every scar inflicted on another is a twisted badge of honor. Their newest target is Tim Dooley’s family—most painfully, his father—whose memory they desecrate with a vicious glee that belies a deeper, unspoken torment.

KaikBaggins, the eldest and the architect of this malevolence, has been haunted by shadows since childhood. His life has been a relentless pursuit of control—stripping away dignity with every calculated cruelty. Over the years, the cold residue of his actions has seeped into his very being. Each sneer and every orchestrated humiliation leaves a mark on him, a permanent reminder of the emptiness that grows in place of true connection. Even as he directs the group’s mockery of Tim’s father, the lines on his face deepen, and his smile twists with bitter resignation—a mask hiding the loneliness that no cruelty can fill.

PKFC and Don Jefe follow KaikBaggins with a conflicted mix of admiration and dread. They have learned to mirror his cruelty, each malicious act another emblem of their dark allegiance. Yet, as their voices echo in the oppressive night, the weight of their actions becomes almost palpable. Each jibe aimed at defiling the memory of a man who once inspired love now boomerangs back, staining their souls with an unyielding sorrow.

On one particularly bleak evening, as they recount twisted tales of Tim’s father with voices strained by forced bravado, a transformation unfolds. PKFC’s laughter rings hollow, and Don Jefe’s mirth falters when the murky water reflects back an image of a stranger—one whose eyes betray the remorse of a man entangled in his own malevolence.

Then, almost silently, Tim Dooley appears. His gaze is steady, unyielding—a quiet force that shatters the brittle façade. “What do you gain?” he asks, voice low yet resonant, “by desecrating the memories of the dead, by reducing a legacy of love to mere dust?”

KaikBaggins’ retort, laced with arrogance—“People don’t matter once they’re gone”—crumbles under the force of Tim’s unwavering truth. “And what does that leave you with, Kaik? Dust? A forgotten echo?” The question slices through the night, igniting a deep, painful introspection within each member of the circle.

In that moment, the air grows heavy with regret. The relentless cycle of cruelty reveals its true cost: every insult, every taunt, binds them tighter to the darkness they once believed they controlled. Tim’s final words, spoken softly as he recedes into the gloom—“Remember this moment, and yet, forget me. In forgetting, there is a space for healing and rebirth”—linger like fragile seeds of change.

As the shadows recede, the members of the Troublemakers’ Circle are left to confront the unyielding truth: in their relentless pursuit to dishonor others, they have imprisoned themselves. And perhaps, in the quiet act of forgetting, there lies the first tentative step toward redemption—a chance to shed the weight of the past and find a sliver of light in the dark.
