Termite and the Potato Under the Lawnmower

In one of the Great Book's smallest complete stories, Termite tries to grow a physical potato for the Potatoverse. Then somebody runs a lawnmower through the plant.

Hear the full story

Termite begins with one patch of soil and a ridiculous amount of hope. He wants to grow a potato in the name of the Potatoverse—not a diagram, not a metaphor, an actual potato. He plants it, covers it carefully and keeps returning to the garden.

The plant answers slowly. A sprout appears. Green leaves rise. Termite waters it, watches the weather and protects it from the sun. For a while the whole philosophy has been reduced to the oldest possible experiment: put something alive in the ground and see whether care works.

Then the lawnmower comes through.

The green top disappears in one violent pass. What had looked like progress becomes an empty patch with clippings scattered over it. Termite stands there looking at what is left and has to decide whether the destruction he can see is the whole event.

He kneels and starts digging.

Under the ruined leaves his hand eventually finds something firm. He pulls out a small potato, still dirty and imperfect, but intact beneath the damage above ground. In the book this becomes the first physical Potatoverse potato: the visible plant was cut down; the thing it had been making underground survived.

News travels through the literary world and Tim comes to the garden. He kneels beside Termite and treats the little potato as if its scale has nothing to do with its importance. Termite answers more quietly: he says he only did what the soil asked and that the accomplishment belongs as much to the earth as to him.

The potato is replanted. Termite goes back to tending the garden. The chapter ends without turning him into a king or prophet. He remains the person who kept watering one plant, watched it apparently get destroyed, and had the sense to check under the soil before declaring it dead.
