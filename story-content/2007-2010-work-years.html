SIDE STORY2007

Six Months in the Deep Freezer

Before the internet theology there are six months of night shifts, forklifts and cold.

Hear the full story

After dental reconstruction and an attempt at college, theory feels too far from anything he can touch. He leaves for practical work. First comes painting and decorating: empty apartments, visible change, rooms becoming different because somebody actually did the work.

Then he takes the better-paying job. For roughly six months he works nights in a meatpacking plant, driving forklifts, moving boxes and tubs, cutting and sorting portions, sending meat toward packaging, transport and deep freezers.

The cold is part of the job, but the smell is what the book remembers most vividly. It gets into clothes, hair and skin. The commute is long. Sundays pay better. On the repetitive nights he starts measuring the work in minutes, hours, breaks and money, turning the shift into a private arithmetic of endurance.

Nothing supernatural happens. That is why the episode matters. Long before one hundred thousand hours becomes a mythic number, there is already a young man getting through a freezing night by counting time.

SIDE STORY2008

Corporate Cosplay in Copenhagen

At twenty-one he travels roughly 250 kilometres for an IT consultant opportunity, gets hired almost immediately, and begins commuting into Copenhagen in a suit with a briefcase.

Hear the full story

The book remembers the morning rhythm precisely enough to make the year visible: an 8:40 subway, then the final walk into work. Suit. Briefcase. Computers and phones. Free Coca-Cola, chocolate milk, snacks and movie tickets. The language calls it "corporate cosplay with real pay."

Outside the office the city remains open. There are bars, neon streets, friends, dating and then long returns into Dota and World of Warcraft. Copenhagen is crowded and constantly audible, but the remembered feeling is strangely solitary—everybody close together and still sealed inside their own movement.

Then the year turns. A prized big-screen television is stolen. Shortly afterward the job ends abruptly. The experiment in metropolitan corporate life collapses almost as quickly as it began, and he goes home.

The later Potatoverse will build enormous theories about technology and institutions. Here technology is still much smaller: a job, a commute, games after work, an expensive television, and the unpleasant discovery that a life can disappear because somebody else says Monday will no longer look like last Monday.

Source note

Great Book autobiographical Chapter 24 stratum. The theft and firing are project-biography claims; this Story does not add motives or perpetrators beyond what that source says.

SIDE STORY2010

The Colorblind Boss and the Cubicle

Graphic design was supposed to combine art, technology and communication. The internship manages to make all three feel dead.

Hear the full story

By 2010 he is studying graphic design and, for a while, life feels more coherent. Then an internship takes him away from home and puts him in a cubicle doing work the later biography remembers as painfully dull.

The detail that survives is almost too perfect: the boss is colorblind. A student who wanted an education in visual language finds himself doing boring visual work for somebody whose own perception of color is limited. Whether or not that irony felt funny at the time, the future described by the internship feels like dread rather than arrival.

So he quits.

The decision does not magically solve the next year. In fact, the chronology moves toward loss of direction and the 2011 collapse. But this smaller failure matters because it shows another recurring pattern before any Potato terminology exists: when the structure that is supposed to be the path starts feeling like a cage, he leaves the structure before he knows the replacement.

Great Book autobiographical Chapter 24 stratum. Relationship material surrounding the move is intentionally omitted here because it is not necessary to tell the work/education episode.
