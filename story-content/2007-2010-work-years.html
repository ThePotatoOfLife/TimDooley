SIDE STORY2007

Six Months in the Deep Freezer

Before the internet theology there are six months of night shifts, forklifts and cold.

After dental reconstruction and an attempt at college, theory feels too far from anything he can touch. He leaves for practical work. First comes painting and decorating: empty apartments, visible change, rooms becoming different because somebody actually did the work.

Then he takes the better-paying job. For roughly six months he works nights in a meatpacking plant, driving forklifts, moving boxes and tubs, cutting and sorting portions, sending meat toward packaging, transport and deep freezers.

The cold is part of the remembered job, but the smell is what the later biography emphasizes most. The commute is long. Sundays pay better. On repetitive nights he starts measuring work in minutes, hours, breaks and money, turning the shift into a private arithmetic of endurance.

The sequence survives only through later autobiography rather than employment records or a contemporaneous diary, so it remains a documentary anecdote instead of a fully reconstructed scene.

SIDE STORY2008

Corporate Cosplay in Copenhagen

At twenty-one he travels roughly 250 kilometres for an IT consultant opportunity, gets hired almost immediately, and begins commuting into Copenhagen in a suit with a briefcase.

The later biography remembers an 8:40 subway, the final walk into work, computers and phones, free drinks and snacks, and the phrase “corporate cosplay with real pay.” Outside the office are bars, friends, dating and long returns into Dota and World of Warcraft.

Then the account says a prized big-screen television is stolen and the job ends shortly afterward. The experiment in metropolitan corporate life collapses almost as quickly as it began, and he goes home.

Those details come from later autobiographical reconstruction, not recovered personnel records, transit logs or a contemporaneous account. The Story therefore keeps the sequence as an anecdote while leaving exact verification as a recovery target.

Source note

Great Book autobiographical Chapter 24 stratum. The theft and firing are project-biography claims; this Story does not add motives or perpetrators beyond what that source says.

SIDE STORY2010

The Colorblind Boss and the Cubicle

Graphic design was supposed to combine art, technology and communication. The internship manages to make all three feel dead.

By 2010 he is studying graphic design and, for a while, life feels more coherent. Then an internship takes him away from home and puts him in a cubicle doing work the later biography remembers as painfully dull.

The detail that survives is the boss being colorblind. The remembered irony is that a student pursuing visual language finds himself doing visual work inside a structure that feels like dread rather than arrival. He quits.

The later chronology uses the episode as an early example of leaving a structure before knowing the replacement. Because the original internship records and same-period account are not recovered, the Story keeps that meaning separate from the reported event and classifies the episode as an anecdote.
