The Death of Potato Is the Birth of Many

The Great Potato reaches the end of its life in the Garden and discovers that breaking apart is how its story continues.

Hear the full story

The Great Potato sits in the heart of the Garden, old enough that its skin has become a record of weather, rot, survival and seasons. It knows the end is near. The other vegetables gather around it, not because a battle is coming, but because something is about to be given away.

The Potato begins to crack. Roots push out through the fractures. Pieces of golden flesh break loose and fall into the soil, and the book turns each fallen piece into a new small potato. They are not identical copies. Some are smooth, some rough, some large, some content to remain close to the roots. The original does not preserve itself by remaining whole.

Before the last piece falls, the Great Potato says that it must dissolve and be broken so its children can carry the story forward. Then the original disappears into the soil. What remains is warmth, richer ground and a Garden suddenly full of descendants.

The days afterward are the actual ending. The Garden grows. The descendants begin their own lives. The old Potato survives as fragments, memory, soil and nourishment rather than as one unchanged body.

Source note

Authored Great Book fiction, Chapter 69. This is a literary Potatoverse story, not a historical event. The chapter explicitly narrates cracking, multiplication, dissolution into soil and descendants carrying the story onward.
