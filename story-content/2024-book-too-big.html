The Book Gets Too Big

By 2024 the joke has become too large to abandon. The Great Book of Potato stops behaving like a book about a potato and starts behaving like a place.

Hear the full story

At first the premise sounds almost manageable: explain Potatoism. But the manuscript keeps accepting new rooms. Biography arrives. Theology arrives. Biology, internet culture, comparative religion, psychology, satire, ritual, social conflict, gardens, compost heaps, teachers, students, Mud Dwellers and increasingly elaborate cosmologies all get admitted into the same object.

The first chapter calls itself The Genesis of the Potatoverse. That title gives away the problem. A book can be finished. A universe immediately begins asking what else lives in it.

Tim inside this stratum is not yet the later Tim of the North Throne. He is mostly the Sage: the figure imaginary potatoes find when they need to ask why they are carrying rocks, why they are stuck in Mud, how to grow, what to release and where the Garden is. The book teaches burden, attachment, burial, composting and ascent before those ideas become Tim's own later plot.

It also starts contradicting itself, which is useful evidence rather than an editorial embarrassment. One arrangement can still put Tim in a Son/guide position while God is Source and Holy Soil carries Spirit or memory. Elsewhere Potato is already called a central axis. Later Tim will identify himself much more directly with Father, Root, Axis and Ladder. The 2024 book therefore preserves development rather than a finished creed.

Even authorship becomes part of the world. A later chapter preserved in the evolving Great Book asks who wrote it. Tim's name is on the cover, but the chapter plays with several answers: Tim, the reader, AI, the Potato, or a book that seems to grow by reflecting whoever is asking the next question. The AI is described as shaping and ordering material without being the source of its meaning.

That is remarkably close to the problem Tim will face again in 2026 when he becomes obsessed with provenance: a system can help organize the work without being allowed to swallow the life, authorship and historical sequence that produced it.

The modern repository now preserves the Great Book as 168 indexed chapter records, 167 with body content. That number belongs to the current archived edition, not automatically to the exact 2024 manuscript state. The surviving large Library copy was amended through later years, so chapter count and later additions must not be backdated merely because they now live inside one file.

What belongs safely to 2024 is the transformation itself. Tim begins the year with a book. He ends the Great Book phase having built a world large enough to contain characters, systems, competing theologies, jokes, enemies, Gardens, machines—and contradictions that later Tim will spend years sorting out.
