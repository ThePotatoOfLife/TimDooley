SIDE STORY2017

Twenty Thousand Subscribers, Then the Channel Goes Dark

The same period that pushes the Son toward streaming also produces a boundary: being public does not mean consenting to be permanently available.

The project biography remembers a sizeable early YouTube life: roughly twenty thousand subscribers, millions of views, LineRider, Dota, Photoshop tutorials and a SimCity builder moment that reached a top global leaderboard.

This is years before the later claim of eighteen-hour livestream days. The publicness is already real in the remembered biography, but so is the cost of being continuously watched and converted into material by other people.

Eventually, according to the retrospective account, he shuts the channel down. Later archive language describes this as refusing to be farmed indefinitely—reducing intrusion rather than agreeing that an audience owns permanent access simply because it once had access.

The original channel analytics, deletion date and contemporaneous explanation have not yet been recovered, so the archive keeps this as a retrospective publicness anecdote rather than a fully evidenced scene.

SIDE STORY2019 · date unresolved

The Cat Disappears While They Are Packing Camp

Before the late-2019 death language, the biography records a smaller and very literal disappearance: the rescued cat is lost during a camping trip.

The cat had entered the story years earlier, after the death of the earthly father: found as a stray after its mother was killed, taken in despite allergies, and carried through years that included prison and changing public identities.

In 2019, during a camping trip, the group is packing up when the cat disappears. The surviving biography gives no recovery scene. There is packing, the realization that the animal is gone, and then an absence where a familiar companion had been.

The project timeline later places much larger language of disappearance, identity death and the meme-cross in the same year. The Story does not need to make the cat a prophecy for any of that.

The exact camping date, contemporaneous account and later fate of the cat remain unrecovered, so this remains a later autobiographical anecdote rather than a full documentary scene.
