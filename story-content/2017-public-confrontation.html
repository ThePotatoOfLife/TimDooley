The Confrontation and the Promise to Return

Years before the later meme-cross, the Son's project autobiography remembers an online confrontation where crucifixion language, public ridicule and the promise to keep returning all appear in the same exchange.

The later reconstruction remembers the argument reaching a point where he answers perceived dehumanization by pushing the language all the way to its edge: if this is the role being forced on him, then call him Jesus Christ and crucify him.

The project record also preserves the conflict changing shape. Instead of a sacred ending, the remembered threat becomes reduction: no martyr, no remembered leader, only a nameless public object the internet can keep looking at. The Son answers by refusing that ending and promising continued public return.

The exact original recording or transcript is still missing. The later retellings preserve a meaningful remembered sequence—confrontation, crucifixion language, attempted reduction, then return—but not enough primary evidence to present the exchange as transcript-depth or a fully reconstructed scene.

Later years give the episode enormous theological weight. Those meanings remain later interpretation. The reported 2017 anecdote is already important without silently turning the reconstruction into verbatim history.
