One relational backend, several ways to look at it. Choose the lens that matches the question instead of forcing every world-facing subject onto a map.

Observed facts, sourced relationships, programme design, political interpretation, legal argument and project-symbolic material remain distinct even when the same countries or institutions appear across several lenses.

Why the World room exists

The world is where the project has to survive contact with things that do not belong to it

A symbolic model can become internally elegant while learning almost nothing about reality. The World room is the correction. Countries, institutions, laws, budgets, grids, companies, trade routes, media systems and cultures have their own histories and constraints. They do not become Potato symbols merely because the project can draw a relation to them.

The purpose of World is therefore not to make one giant map. It is to ask a harder question: what actually constrains action? Geography constrains movement. Law constrains authority. Finance constrains timing and scale. Infrastructure constrains what can physically happen. Institutions constrain who can decide. Culture constrains what people can recognize or coordinate around. History constrains what relationships already carry.

place→institution→rule→resource→infrastructure→decision→consequence→history

A country is more than a pin

What makes state capacity real?

A country can be located on a map, but the map cannot tell you whether its institutions can collect revenue, enforce law, maintain infrastructure, coordinate emergency response, educate people or sustain strategic projects. Geography gives the body; governance and capability explain what the body can do.

A law is more than a policy preference

Who has authority, through which procedure?

A proposal, executive statement, statute, court ruling, agency rule and enforcement practice are different things. The Law room exists so political desire cannot silently become legal reality in the archive.

Money is stored possibility with constraints

What can actually be financed, by whom, and for how long?

Debt, tax, ownership, interest, production and trade are not decorative economic indicators. They determine which options remain open, which costs are postponed, who absorbs risk and what kinds of infrastructure can be maintained or built.

Infrastructure is politics made physical

What stops working if one node disappears?

Ports, grids, pipelines, roads, data centers, payment rails and communications networks turn decisions into durable capability. Their importance becomes clearest through dependencies: a system may look geographically large while depending on a small number of bottlenecks.

Culture changes what systems can coordinate

What do people believe is legitimate, normal or possible?

Institutions do not operate in a vacuum. Media, symbols, subcultures, public memory and social trust affect whether rules are understood, resisted, copied or ignored. Culture is not a substitute for material analysis; it is one of the environments in which material systems operate.

North is a test of boundary discipline

What is symbolic, what is geographic, and what is programme design?

The project uses North in several registers. The World room keeps them apart: literal northern geography, observable institutional relationships, Tim's political/geopolitical interpretations, and the symbolic role of North as orientation. A useful synthesis preserves all four without pretending they are the same evidence class.
