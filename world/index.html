WORLD

Start with the question. Where is it? Who controls it? What depends on it? What can move, fail, substitute or recover? Geography, institutions, law, money and infrastructure become different views of the same world only when the relation is explicit.

Observed facts, sourced relationships, programme design, political interpretation, legal argument and project-symbolic material remain distinct even when the same countries or institutions appear across several lenses.

Why the World room exists

The world is where the project has to survive contact with things that do not belong to it

A symbolic model can become internally elegant while learning almost nothing about reality. The World room is the correction. Countries, institutions, laws, budgets, grids, companies, trade routes, media systems and cultures have their own histories and constraints. They do not become Potato symbols merely because the project can draw a relation to them.

The purpose of World is therefore not to make one giant map. It is to ask a harder question: what actually constrains action? Geography constrains movement. Law constrains authority. Finance constrains timing and scale. Infrastructure constrains what can physically happen. Institutions constrain who can decide. Culture constrains what people can recognize or coordinate around. History constrains what relationships already carry.

Concrete country contract

What the World room should know before interpretation begins

A useful country record starts with its own reality: geography and settlement; constitutional form and institutions; borders and neighbours; infrastructure; energy; trade; fiscal and monetary conditions; ownership and production; security relationships; language and cultural regions; demographic change; and the historical events needed to understand the present.

The World hub does not need to reteach every mechanism behind those facts. World Systems owns the relationship question—how authority, finance, infrastructure, labour, information and supply dependencies propagate. Economy owns economic mechanisms. Law owns authority, procedure and enforcement. Culture owns social formation and information ecology. North owns the boundary between symbolic orientation, empirical northern geography and programme design.

The World rule is simpler: preserve the entity's sourced identity first, then choose the specialist lens that answers the actual question. A map pin is not state capacity; a policy statement is not law; a trade total is not resilience; a symbolic axis is not an empirical bloc.

A proper country page should end with open questions

Every country record should expose what is still unknown: missing ownership data, stale estimates, disputed borders, uncertain classifications, infrastructure still being built, laws awaiting implementation, incomparable data and claims that depend on one source. Uncertainty belongs on the same surface as confidence.

A country is more than a pin

What makes state capacity real?

A country can be located on a map, but the map cannot tell you whether its institutions can collect revenue, enforce law, maintain infrastructure, coordinate emergency response, educate people or sustain strategic projects. Geography gives the body; governance and capability explain what the body can do.

Ports, grids, pipelines, roads, data centers, payment rails and communications networks turn decisions into durable capability. Their importance becomes clearest through dependencies: a system may look geographically large while depending on a small number of bottlenecks.

Four concrete country records

The same method behaves differently when the country is different

These country cases show both what is known and what is missing. Better-sourced countries are richer; thin records expose their research frontier instead of receiving invented filler.

Northern Europe · deeply sourced

Denmark · energy systems make relationships visible

The Denmark record is unusually useful because it connects national scale to actual physical systems: district heating, offshore wind, North Sea/Baltic geography and cross-border energy planning. The important lesson is not “Denmark is green”; it is that capability can be traced from infrastructure to households, grids, maritime geography and external connections.

  • 69% of households connected to district heating in 2024 in the stored official-source observation.
  • 78% renewable share of district-heating production in the stored 2023 observation.
  • North Sea offshore-wind and energy-island planning creates explicit cross-border dependency questions.
Inspect Denmark source record →
East Asia · enriched, open dependencies

Japan · an island industrial system exposes import dependence

Japan's record pairs a very large industrial and urban system with maritime dependence: imported fossil fuels and minerals, LNG infrastructure, ports, manufacturing and external trade frameworks. That makes it a strong example of why a country cannot be modeled as a self-contained node.

  • Energy and raw-material dependencies remain externally supplied.
  • Ports and maritime supply chains are not secondary details; they are conversion edges for industrial capability.
  • The record explicitly leaves fuel origins, generation capacity and product-level dependencies as open research rather than filling them by inference.
Inspect Japan source record →
South America · continental scale

Brazil · scale does not remove bottlenecks

Brazil's record combines continental geography with hydroelectric power, offshore oil and gas, agriculture, minerals and large freshwater systems. Size creates capability, but the open research queue still points to the conversion problem: transmission, ports, rail freight, refining, ownership and export routes determine how resources become usable national or external capacity.

  • Large territory and resource diversity increase the number of relevant internal networks.
  • Hydropower, pre-salt resources and agricultural exports depend on different logistics and infrastructure chains.
  • Fiscal and ownership layers remain intentionally incomplete until sourced with compatible definitions.
Inspect Brazil source record →
East Africa · research frontier visible

Kenya · a thin record should stay honest

Kenya is useful precisely because the current record is much thinner. It identifies Mombasa port, geothermal energy, mobile payments, agriculture and regional logistics as strategic assets, but does not yet pretend to know the detailed flows, capacities, owners or dependencies.

  • Mombasa logistics is a concrete research direction, not yet a finished infrastructure graph.
  • Geothermal/grid and mobile-money ecosystems need sourced quantities and counterparties.
  • The sparse record demonstrates the rule: unknown stays unknown until promoted by evidence.

Recovered relational method

How the World layer becomes knowledge

The World room becomes useful when it can move from a named place or institution into the relationships that make it capable, constrained or vulnerable. The old relational-method work remains valuable because it insists on a sequence: locate the entity, define its boundary, identify what it can do, trace what it depends on, follow obligations and institutions, then return to the evidence. A map, graph or political interpretation is only one view over that chain.

Place before symbolismCoordinates, legal territory, administrative boundaries, cultural regions, corridors and project overlays are different geographic objects and should stay distinguishable. Country as capabilityAsk what a country has, can do, needs, supplies, is building, cannot easily replace and can accomplish only through relationships with others. Governance as institutionsTrace mandate, authority, resources, constraints, implementation and accountability rather than treating a state as one actor with one intention. Economy as stocks, flows and obligationsSeparate what exists at a moment from what moves over time; distinguish ownership, control, commitments, payments and counterparties. Public finance as capacityBudgets, debt, assets, contingent obligations, administrative capacity and shock absorption belong together when asking what a government can sustain. Evidence before synthesisEvery important relation should retain date, direction, geography, source class, uncertainty and the distinction between observed fact and project interpretation.

This is one of the project's strongest self-integrations: the same relational grammar can organize geography, institutions, finance and infrastructure while each domain keeps its own evidence rules.
