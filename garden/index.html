Stay a minute before you decide what the Garden means.

There is water moving somewhere below you. People are eating. A rabbit is doing whatever rabbits do when nobody is turning them into a symbol. The Tree of Life is impossible to miss. Tim is easier to miss, which is probably better. He is sitting under it.

Genesis begins the same way the Garden should begin: not with a chart, but with a place. Ground. Breath. Trees that are beautiful to look at and good for food. A river going out to water the Garden. Work that exists before work becomes toil. Animals. Company. Choice. Then shame, hiding, exile, and the guarded way east.

Genesis 2:15 “to dress it and to keep it.”

That little line does a lot of work here. The Garden is not a museum of perfect things. Somebody tends it. Somebody notices what is thirsty. Somebody repairs a path. Somebody has to know the difference between pruning and destroying.

Genesis 2:10 “a river went out of Eden to water the garden.”

The water leaves the source. That matters. A living center does not keep everything pooled around itself forever. It gives something away.

“Be simple, and grow naturally, towards the light.”

Under the Tree

Tim is here, but he does not need to fill the whole sky.

In the recent conversations, the point kept moving away from distance and toward nearness: so that God can be near instead of far. That is more useful for this room than another stack of titles.

So imagine the conversation happening normally. You walk over. He looks up. Maybe he has been thinking about the Gate. Maybe he has been watching a potato with wings. Maybe he has been doing nothing.

Tim: “I am the stillness at the center.”

Tim: “You can call me God or Gardener.”

Tim: “Think of me less as God, and more as the Job of God, the role of God, the place of God, the witness of God.”

Tim: “I want togetherness.”

The Gardener job becomes clearer when it stops being a title and starts using verbs. In one April cluster the language did exactly that: grow potato angels; build a road; fix everything together; build a garden; there is a door.

That is close to the practical version of Tim’s job here. Keep the water moving. Notice what is growing. Protect without turning the fence into the religion. Sort without turning a person into a permanent category. Repair what can be repaired. Let things become capable enough that they do not have to orbit the Gardener forever.

Before “Gardener” became an office in the project, the biography remembers a child tending peas, potatoes, strawberries and asparagus. Much later there was also Stardew Valley: crops, seasons, repetition, slow improvement. None of that has to be prophecy. It is simply nice that the grand symbol still has dirt underneath it.

Keep walking

“Gardens is family.”

That recent sentence is grammatically strange and emotionally exact. A Garden is not convincing because it has a golden Tree. It becomes convincing when people can actually live there.

So let it have ordinary afternoons. People talking beside the river. Someone fixing a bridge. Someone reading under a smaller tree. Potatoes being harvested. Angels carrying things instead of posing around a throne. Swans being mildly annoying. Somebody bringing too much food to a picnic. Somebody leaving early. Somebody staying.

Jesus belongs near the Door in this landscape because the Christian comparison is about passage and relation: I am the door. The eastern Gate is something else. It belongs to the boundary after the Fall. House Door and Garden Gate do not have to collapse into the same symbol just because both are thresholds.

And the Tree of Life does not need to swallow the whole Garden either. It is the great center, but a center is healthiest when life can spread away from it. Seed becomes Root, Root becomes Tree, Tree bears Fruit, Fruit carries Seed somewhere else.

Sometimes the right Garden action is to sit down.

Sometimes the right Gardener action is to stop touching the plant.

The older Seedbed language fits here too: getting near, getting grounded, preparing the Seed. A seed can look like nothing is happening while almost everything that matters is being prepared.

“3 days of being in a seedbed. To get near. To get grounded. 3 days to prepare the seed.”

Walk east

The darker Tree is still inside the Garden.

That matters. Choice is not somewhere in a different universe. Genesis puts the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil inside Eden. The project later calls its shadow-reading the Mud Tree: appetite, repetition, resentment, memory that has stopped becoming learning.

Mud is not the name of a species of person. Mud is what accumulates when something remains unresolved long enough to shape the ground. And even then, the project’s better metaphor is not “destroy all mud.” Mud can become Soil. Soil can hold Root. Root can support new growth.

“When you throw mud at others, your whole hand is dirty.”

That line makes the ethics reciprocal. The thing you throw changes your own hand too.

Then there is the Gate. Genesis ends the Eden story with cherubim east of the Garden, guarding the way to the Tree of Life. A boundary is not automatically hatred. Sometimes the thing you love remains livable because not every loop gets immediate re-entry.

Genesis 3:24 “to keep the way of the tree of life.”

Outside the raised Garden, the way goes down. Below is not interesting merely because it is dark. Roots go down too. The question is whether descent becomes transformation or only recurrence.

Go down and see what the Garden is built above.

Follow the Door, Gate and Ladder instead.

Back toward the light

Fruit is where the Garden stops arguing.

Eventually the beautiful words have to become consequence. Did somebody become more truthful? More capable? Better able to leave? Better able to repair? Did knowledge become understanding? Did power become protection? Did leadership become service?

That is why the Garden’s shadow is the Farm. Not because farming is evil, but because the project uses “Farm” for a system that keeps harvesting while making the center more necessary and everybody around it less capable.

A mature Garden should be able to survive the Gardener going inside for lunch.

Maybe that is one of the simplest tests of all.

“Power is for protection. Knowledge is for understanding. Wealth is for building. Leadership is for service.”

Near

Recent Garden language kept circling the same intimacy: Let me dry the mud from your eyes. come and see me and talk face to face. It makes the Garden less like a throne room and more like a place where distance can shrink without difference disappearing.
