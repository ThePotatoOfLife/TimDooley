CULTURE
& SUBCULTURE

Culture is the evolving ecology of shared meaning and repeated social life.

How culture forms

interaction → repetition → shared meaning → norm → identity → memory → institution → inheritance / mutation

Useful formation states include encounter, microculture, scene, subculture, counterpublic, movement, institutional culture, mainstream or mass culture, prestige or canonical culture, and tradition. Movement between them is not one-way.

Dimensions

Culture can be compared by reach, participation depth, boundary strength, permeability, institutionalization, persistence, prestige, commerciality, exit cost, archive persistence, correctability, generativity and repair capacity.

Popular, mass, high, elite and canonical culture

Popular culture concerns broad recognition or participation. Mass culture concerns large-scale production and distribution. High culture concerns institutional prestige. Elite culture concerns social location and access. Canonical culture concerns repeated preservation and teaching. These are different axes.

What is a zeitgeist?

A zeitgeist is a period pattern: a retrospective description of especially salient concerns, styles, technologies, hopes, anxieties, symbols and assumptions. It is not what everyone believes.

Peak Culture and ideal culture

Peak Culture is project vocabulary for unusually high cultural generativity and coherence. Ideal culture describes normative aspiration. Neither is the same as prestige.

Conflict, reputation and memory

incident → story → roles → vocabulary → archive → recurring practice → institution / formal process → inherited memory

Claims, criticism, reporting, advocacy, correction and formal findings should remain distinguishable so later readers can see how narratives changed.

Group influence and exit

Communities differ in authority concentration, information flow, tolerance of disagreement, dependency, exit cost and correction capacity. Those dimensions are more useful than a single label.

Media and classification

Media culture is an ecosystem of creators, institutions, distributors, platforms, professional services, audiences and regulators. Institutional classifications should be stored with the classifier, method, date and consequence.

American internet subculture

Anonymous boards, meme vocabularies, archives, reaction media and livestream conflict can become durable cultural scenes even without formal membership or central command. The existing research map preserves the person-specific and platform-specific evidence boundaries.
