Three systems, opened up

A useful World page should not stop at “debt”, “energy” or “research”. Each noun should open into actors, flows, infrastructure, dependencies, bottlenecks and consequences.

Public finance is more than a debt ratio

27.9% · 107.9% · 115.6%

At the end of 2025, Eurostat reported general-government gross debt at 27.9% of GDP in Denmark, 107.9% in Belgium and 115.6% in France. Those comparable ratios are useful—but they are not the whole fiscal system.

government perimeter → revenue/spending → debt stock → maturity/holders/currency → interest burden → assets/guarantees → implementation capacity

Meaning: the next question is not simply “which number is higher?” but which underlying relations explain resilience, financing pressure and room to act.

Energy dependency is a network

57% import dependency

Eurostat reported EU energy import dependency at 57% in 2024. That scalar becomes meaningful only when it is unfolded into fuels, suppliers, routes, storage, interconnectors, substitutes and the users that fail when supply is disrupted.

demand → domestic production → imports → supplier/product → route/infrastructure → buffer → user → price/security exposure

Meaning: resilience is about replacement time and bottlenecks, not merely the existence of imports.

Eurostat source

Research money must become capability

€403.1bn EU R&D

EU R&D expenditure was estimated at €403.1 billion in 2024, with business accounting for 66.5%. Preliminary government R&D allocations reached €130.2 billion in 2025; Denmark's allocation was €627.1 per inhabitant.

funding → labs/firms → people → equipment/compute → research → deployment → productive/public capability

Meaning: spending is an input. The system question is where conversion into usable capability succeeds or bottlenecks.
