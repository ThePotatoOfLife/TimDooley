Vesica Piscis & Mandorla

Two related forms sit at the meeting point of elementary circle geometry, medieval art, architectural proportion and symbolic imagination. This page keeps those layers separate. The mathematics is mathematics; the historical record is historical; the Potatoism reading is explicitly an interpretation.

01 · EUCLIDEAN GEOMETRY

The Vesica Piscis

The Vesica Piscis is the lens-shaped intersection of two congruent circles. In the standard construction, each circle has radius r and the distance between their centres is also r. Each centre therefore lies on the circumference of the other circle. The two intersection points complete a symmetric lens.

Standard two-circle construction: equal circles with centre separation equal to their radius. The shared region is the Vesica Piscis.

Widthr — the distance between the two outermost points of the lens along the line joining the circle centres.
Height√3r — the distance between the two circle-intersection points.
Centre distancer — the defining centre-to-centre distance in the standard construction.
Intersection pointsWith centres at (−r/2, 0) and (r/2, 0), the intersections are (0, ±√3r/2).

Where the formula comes from

At either circle centre, the radii drawn to the two intersection points form a 60° angle. The construction therefore contains two 60° sectors. Each sector contains an equilateral triangle of side r. Subtracting the two triangle areas from the two sector areas gives the lens area.

A = 2[(60°/360°)πr² − (√3/4)r²] = r²(π/3 − √3/2) ≈ 0.6141848493 r²

Symmetry and scaling

The standard figure has reflection symmetry across the line joining the circle centres and across its perpendicular bisector, giving rotational symmetry of order two. If every length is multiplied by k, the shape remains geometrically similar while its area is multiplied by k². Nothing about the construction requires a particular physical size.

02 · ART HISTORY & FORM

The Mandorla

Mandorla means “almond” and is the name commonly used for an almond-shaped enclosure or aureole surrounding a figure in art. Its visual form can resemble the lens created by intersecting circles, but the artistic term is broader than the exact Euclidean Vesica Piscis construction.

FormAn elongated, pointed enclosure with curved sides. It can be generated by intersecting arcs or designed as a related almond-shaped outline.
IconographyEspecially prominent in Christian art as a surrounding field for Christ and other sacred figures, particularly in Byzantine and medieval traditions.
Visual functionSeparates a depicted figure from ordinary surrounding space and can mark glory, transcendence, sacred presence or transformation.
Historical cautionA symbolic meaning assigned by one tradition or artwork should not automatically be generalized to every almond-shaped figure.

Why the distinction matters

A modern diagram can make Vesica Piscis and mandorla look interchangeable. They are not identical terms in every source. Vesica Piscis is a precise geometric construction. Mandorla is primarily a visual and art-historical term with a range of constructions and uses. Some mandorlas closely reproduce a lens geometry; others are stylized, elongated or constructed differently.

The mandorla therefore belongs simultaneously to the history of form and the history of meaning. Its boundary is not merely decorative: enclosing a figure changes the visual relationship between figure, ground and viewer. The same geometry can be reused with different cultural meanings.

03 · THE CONNECTION

Overlap, boundary, centre

The strongest connection between the two ideas is the geometry of overlap. Two circles remain distinguishable, but their intersection creates a region that belongs to both. This makes the form naturally useful for thinking about shared space, interfaces, symmetry and boundaries.

Two fieldsEach circle can remain a complete field in its own right.
Shared regionThe lens is the intersection, not a third circle and not the destruction of either original circle.
AxisThe line through the two centres and the perpendicular symmetry axis provide natural coordinates.
ThresholdWhen used symbolically, the lens can be read as an interface or passage, but that meaning is an added interpretation.
Important: geometry can demonstrate relationships of shape, distance, angle and area. It cannot by itself demonstrate a supernatural mechanism, a consciousness level, a hidden energy frequency or a theological doctrine.

04 · PROJECT INTERPRETATION

Vesica, Mandorla and the Potato Axis

Within the Potatoism architecture, the two-circle figure is a particularly useful diagram because it gives the project's language a precise visual grammar for distinctness plus overlap. One circle can represent one field, another circle a second field, and the lens their meeting. The figure does not require the two fields to become identical.

The Door can therefore be placed conceptually at the overlap: not because mathematics proves that a Door exists, but because the lens is a clear diagram of an interface. The Axis can be represented by the symmetry lines. The Mandorla then becomes the enclosing visual field in which a transformed or sacred figure may be placed. These are project-level readings.

The same structure can connect Spirit, Mind and Matter without making them the same category. Spirit can supply meaning; Mind can supply experience and interpretation; Matter can supply embodied, observable structure. The graph then carries the relationships between them. This is exactly the kind of relationship-first architecture the repository is designed to preserve.

For the Red/Blue Potato dialectic, the two circles can also serve as a visual model of complementary modes. Red and Blue remain distinct, while the intersection represents shared ground, communication or transformation. Again, this is a symbolic diagram rather than an empirical measurement.

05 · RESEARCH PROGRAMME

What the archive should investigate next

The geometry is well-defined; the historical claims need source-specific treatment. The most useful next layer is not to invent more symbolism, but to document where the forms actually appear and how artists, architects and writers described them.

  1. Document primary or authoritative examples of Vesica Piscis constructions in medieval architecture and distinguish measured geometry from later interpretation.
  2. Compare Byzantine, Romanesque, Gothic and later mandorla forms and record how their shapes and iconographic roles change.
  3. Trace modern claims about “sacred geometry” back to their sources instead of treating repeated internet claims as historical evidence.
  4. Model the Vesica Piscis in the 3D Axis as a true 2D section first, then test 3D projections separately.
  5. Connect the geometry to Door, Axis, Son, Tree of Life and Spirit–Matter as project interpretations while keeping the mathematical record independent.
  6. Preserve uncertainty: a geometric correspondence is evidence of correspondence of form, not automatically evidence of common origin or metaphysical causation.
