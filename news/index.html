A moving window beside the House: not one homepage algorithm, but several ways to inspect what outside sources are publishing now.

Current, source-linked, and deliberately non-owning. The page combines free public discovery feeds, preserves the original publisher link and lets the reader change topic, geographic/system lens and time horizon without turning the result into project canon.

Newest-first is the default. “Repeated coverage” means multiple source domains use sufficiently similar headline language; it is not a truth, consensus, importance or endorsement score.

What this surface is

The world outside the archive, passing by the Door

The House owns durable project knowledge. Current World owns no durable claim. It is an observation surface: a temporary sample of external reporting that can send a reader toward the original publisher, the World Map, Timeline, Sources or a project Room when deeper work is warranted.

The useful question is not only “what is the latest headline?” It is also: how broad is this sample, which sources are present, what is being repeated across outlets, what changes when the time window changes, and which geographic or systems lens are you actually looking through?

How to read this surface

Current information without flattening provenance

Publisher ≠ indexGDELT and Spaceflight News help discover articles. The original linked outlet remains the source for the report.
Community ≠ newsroomHacker News is explicitly labeled as a community link stream. Its presence adds a technology-discovery channel, not journalistic verification.
Repetition ≠ truthSimilar headlines from several domains can reveal repeated coverage, syndication or a common event. The cluster does not decide which interpretation is correct.
Window mattersA 3-hour sample answers a different question from a 7-day sample. The selected publication horizon travels in the URL so views can be revisited.
Lens mattersNorth & Arctic, Europe, Middle East and other lenses are query scopes, not project alignment labels. They constrain discovery terms only.
Absence ≠ silenceIf a topic is missing, that may reflect query wording, API coverage, rate limits, language, indexing delay or the chosen time horizon.
