Comparative divinity · functional map

DIVINE
FUNCTIONS

Different gods can perform similar functions without being the same god. This page compares the functions—rule, wisdom, protection, judgment, passage, love, death, transformation, craft, ecstasy—while keeping the religious figures historically distinct.

Compare the job before you compare the identity.

Sovereignty / cosmic order

Rule

This function asks who sets order, maintains a world, or stands at the highest political/cosmic level inside a tradition.

Examples: Zeus in Greek religion; high-god/source traditions; kingship-linked divine roles.

Character test: does authority coordinate life, or merely demand recognition?

Order / truth

Maat is especially useful because she is not simply “a ruler.” In ancient Egyptian religion she embodies truth, justice, balance and proper cosmic order; rulers were expected to uphold that order.

Examples: Maat; law/order functions in other traditions should be compared carefully rather than equated.

Character test: can the ruler be judged by an order larger than personal appetite?

Wisdom / knowledge

Costly wisdom

Odin dramatizes the pursuit of hidden knowledge, poetry, magic and strategic understanding. His myths make knowledge costly rather than merely decorative.

Character test: does knowledge increase discernment—or only the prestige of being the person who knows?

Strategic wisdom

Athena joins wisdom with counsel, civic protection, strategy and craft. Her form of intelligence is not simply abstract contemplation; it becomes competent action.

Character test: can intelligence act without becoming superiority?

Threshold / mediation

Messenger / boundary-crosser

Hermes crosses boundaries between places and roles: messenger, traveler, guide, trader, trickster. He makes mediation itself a divine function.

Character test: can you translate between worlds without falsely making them the same?

Guardian of passage

Anubis protects the dead, oversees embalming and appears in the weighing-of-the-heart judgment field. Passage is not casual: it involves preparation, truth and a threshold.

Character test: can you guard a Door without becoming the reason nobody passes?

Protection / force

Guardian strength

Thor gives a clean image of force used to protect a world and its boundaries.

Character test: is strength proportional to the danger, or does strength need danger in order to feel meaningful?

Strategic protection

Athena complicates the warrior function: protection can depend as much on planning, intelligence and restraint as on raw force.

Character test: can courage wait long enough to become effective?

Love / fertility / restoration

Desire and attraction

Aphrodite centers love, beauty, desire and attraction in Greek religion and myth. This power can bind, inspire, destabilize and reorder priorities.

Character test: can desire recognize another person as more than the object of desire?

Restorative care

Isis brings protection, motherhood, royal continuity and restorative devotion into the Egyptian field surrounding Osiris and Horus.

Character test: can care restore life without turning restoration into permanent ownership?

Death / judgment / return

Death and continued life

Osiris becomes a ruler of the underworld and a major funerary figure associated with death, kingship and continued existence.

Character test: what can die without making the whole self die with it?

Weighing

The Egyptian weighing-of-the-heart scene makes judgment relational: the heart is tested against Maat, while Anubis oversees the weighing and Osiris receives the justified dead.

Character test: what standard weighs the judge?

Transformation / dissolution

Shiva

Shiva's religious meanings exceed the simple label “destroyer”: ascetic, yogic, household, cosmic and transformative functions coexist across Hindu traditions.

Character test: can you end an obsolete form without becoming fascinated by destruction itself?

Potatoist Compost / Forge

Potatoism has its own transformation grammar: what dies can become seed, compost, material or tool—but only if the later state actually gains capacity.

Character test: did the pain become something useful, or did the story merely rename the pain?

Craft / making

Hephaestus

Fire, forge and craft make transformation technical rather than merely mystical. The difficult material becomes an object through skill.

Character test: can injury become craft without craft becoming revenge?

Promethean gift

Prometheus is strongly associated with bringing fire to humanity and suffering punishment for defying Zeus. Later reception often turns him into a symbol of knowledge, technology and rebellious benefaction.

Character test: when does giving power to others become service, and when is “liberation” another form of self-glorification?

Ecstasy / release

Dionysus

Wine, festival, theatre, vegetation and ecstatic religious experience make Dionysus a figure through whom ordinary boundaries can loosen.

Character test: can you experience release without making loss of control your only route to aliveness?

Potato humor

Potatoism's comic register performs a milder version of boundary loosening: the sacred becomes ridiculous enough to remain examinable.

Character test: can your highest idea survive laughter without becoming meaningless?

Trickster / disruption

Loki

Loki occupies a deeply ambivalent trickster position in Norse myth, repeatedly destabilizing relations and crossing expected categories.

Character test: does disruption reveal rigidity, or has disruption itself become the identity?

Hermes again

Hermes shows why trickster and messenger functions overlap: crossing the boundary can produce exchange, translation, theft, invention or deception.

Character test: can cleverness answer to truth?

A pantheon can be read as a distributed model of powers. Character maturity asks whether a human being can integrate those powers without pretending to become the historical gods who carried them.

Potatoist use

Potatoism gains something by refusing to turn every function into Tim. Maat can remain a standard that challenges Tim's Judge role. Anubis can remain a threshold guardian that clarifies Door. Odin can remain an ordeal/wisdom comparator. Isis can expose what Father-heavy language may neglect about restorative, maternal care. Hephaestus can deepen Forge. Dionysus can challenge a system that becomes too controlled.

This produces a more interesting divine field: not a collection of masks Tim wears, but a council of powers capable of questioning Tim.

Source anchors: Metropolitan Museum of Art material on Maat, Anubis, and the Judgment Before Osiris; project comparative-religion records provide additional routing. Character interpretations are Potatoist/philosophical synthesis rather than claims about ancient worshippers.
