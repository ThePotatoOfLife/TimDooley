Culture in motion

A subculture is a system that learns how to reproduce itself

The useful question is not only “what is this group called?” but what keeps it alive: shared objects, language, places, roles, status, boundaries, memory, incentives and transmission. The project now treats culture as a living mechanism rather than a glossary category.

How a subculture forms

Repeated encounter becomes culturally durable when participants develop recognizable signals, norms, roles, memory and a way to teach the pattern to newcomers.

Shared condition→encounter→signal→norm→identity→memory→reproduction

How culture can enter politics

Political effects appear when shared identities, grievances, vocabulary, networks or creators are translated into public claims, mobilization, advocacy, campaigns, institutions, law or policy response. This is one possible route, not an automatic stage.

Identity / grievance→frame→network→mobilization→bridge actor→institution→feedback

Sektur is the social territory

In the project's specialist vocabulary, Sektur describes a semi-bounded territory where recurring roles, conflict and attention relationships become self-reproducing. It is narrower than Culture and is not a universal stage every subculture enters.

Subculture→roles→conflict / attention→archive→recurrence

Swamp is the surrounding environment

Swamp describes the dense overlap among platforms, media, markets, institutions, law, politics, reputation, archives and unresolved incentives. Farm describes an attention process inside that environment; Swamp is not a secret organization.

Culture↔media↔markets↔institutions↔politics / law

Why the Farm persists

A target ecology does not need one central controller. Distributed watchers, archivists, commentators, reactors and searchers can keep a narrative alive because each contribution is cheap while the archive makes the whole system cumulative.

watch→capture→archive→react→redistribute→remember

Where money enters

Not every participant is paid. But attention can become ads, subscriptions, memberships, tips, sponsorships, donations, political fundraising or platform revenue. The important distinction is who captures value from recurrence.

attention→traffic→revenue / status→more production

Who gets to classify whom?

SPLC and ADL are private watchdog/classifier organizations; the FBI is law enforcement. A watchdog designation, an incident map and a criminal finding are different kinds of authority and should never be silently merged.

observation→classification→media / employer / donor consequence→possible legal process

Why visibility is not accountability

Years of public ugliness can persist because moral wrong, platform-rule violation, civil wrong and crime are not the same category. Evidence, jurisdiction, infrastructure and authority may be distributed across different actors.

visible harm≠one legal owner→fragmented response

Same root, different route

Life, Strife, Repair and Return are not labels for people or institutions. They are route hypotheses. The same information, residue, growth or descent can branch differently depending on the operator, the evidence and the downstream consequence.

Run it against reality

The House has started touching real systems

Project operators are analytical lenses, not factual labels for these organizations. The dossiers keep each entity’s own identity and sources, then test whether concepts such as River, Door, Eye, Garden, Fruit and Foundation reveal measurable mechanisms.

The first four dossiers deliberately use very different institutions. Danmarks Nationalbank tests money, payment infrastructure and oversight; Wikimedia Foundation tests knowledge infrastructure, volunteer/governance boundaries and technology investment; CERN tests scientific infrastructure and research throughput; the International Committee of the Red Cross tests humanitarian mandate, access, funding and legal/operational constraint. The point is not to force all four into the same symbolic story. It is to see whether the House can preserve each institution’s own documented identity while asking comparable questions about flow, observation, thresholds, reproduction and consequence.

The cases already carry measurable longitudinal differences rather than only descriptions. The Nationalbank dossier records core Danish payment throughput rising from DKK 732 billion to DKK 838 billion per average banking day from 2024 to 2025, while explicitly warning that throughput measures scale rather than safety or social value. CERN records a more-than-doubling of cited LHC raw-data production between 2023 and 2024, while warning that data volume is not discovery quality. Wikimedia tracks budget and technology-investment changes without treating spending as public value. ICRC tracks contribution and flexible-funding changes while separating financing inputs from humanitarian outcomes.

That distinction is the real test of the machinery: one observable number may describe load, money, capacity or change without becoming a universal score. Every dossier therefore pairs the measurement with a limit, an evidence boundary and a falsifier for the project operator being applied. The backend can grow the case population; the homepage only needs enough of that method to show the reader what the project is actually doing.
