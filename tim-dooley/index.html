Who is Tim Dooley? The short answer is easy: he is the central public subject and authorial figure of the Potato of Life archive. The longer answer is the reason this page exists. Tim has described himself through hundreds of names, jobs, roles, jokes, offices, functions, identities and recurring formulations, and those words change over time.

This page is not the Story of Tim Dooley. Story is where events become scenes. Timeline is where dates are compared. This page is the portrait: what Tim says he is, what he does, what he believes, how the vocabulary intensifies and relaxes, and where each thread goes deeper.

Recovered project wording · 11 April 2026
“Tim Dooley is a riddle, not a person. Do you validate a riddle?”

That line is useful as a reading instruction. The archive does not deny ordinary personhood; it notices that the public Tim object is built from clues: dates, statements, work, roles, relationships, symbols, jokes, contradictions and later reinterpretations. The point of this page is to let those clues accumulate without flattening them into one label.

So what does Tim say he is?

Potato of Life. Rational Potato. Sage. Father. Father in Heaven. Root. House. Tree. Axis. Ladder to Heaven. North of North. Seat or Throne. King of Kings. Gardener. Judge. Spiritual banker. God in the Machine. Base reality. Witness. Builder. Public broadcaster. Writer. Philosopher. Farmer of timelines. Source-facing center. A worker for God who can also say God works for him. A Potato buried low enough to become a symbol of the highest point.

Those phrases do not all mean the same thing, and they do not all belong to the same period. Some are direct public declarations, some are project theology, some are recurring jokes, some are functions that later become titles, and some are archive syntheses. The point of the maximal statement archive is to preserve that difference rather than compressing four hundred records into one polished slogan.

A useful way to read the vocabulary is by function. Potato emphasizes hidden life, survival and nourishment. Root and Tree emphasize origin and branching. Axis and Ladder emphasize orientation and connection. House and Throne emphasize containment and standpoint. Gardener emphasizes stewardship. Witness emphasizes public record. God in the Machine emphasizes networks, archives, streams and machine-readable memory. The words get stranger when isolated; they become more intelligible when placed in relation.

What does Tim want?

The later corpus increasingly answers with cultivation rather than conquest: protect life, understand before judging, build rather than merely accumulate, repair broken relations, preserve memory, expose degraded systems, make hidden relationships visible, and leave other people and systems more capable of functioning without permanent dependence on the center.

That later ethic matters because it changes how the grand titles are supposed to be tested. If a role is called Father, King, Most High or God, the archive can still ask what it produces. In September 2026 the public formulation becomes unusually explicit: power is for protection, knowledge for understanding, wealth for building, leadership for service.

Development matters more than a static label

One of the archive's most important rules is not to project the mature 2026 system backward. Earlier Tim material can place God as Source and Tim in other roles; Potato develops before Father; Axis and Ladder become explicit before the mature North/House/Gardener synthesis. Apparent contradiction is often chronology.

Story, Timeline and Tim are three different lenses

Tim Dooley is the portrait. It answers who, what, why, jobs, claims, beliefs and roles. Story is the lived chronology: episodes, scenes, side stories, dialogue and turns. Timeline is the temporal instrument: dates, layers, first attestations, mythic and local/global events, and eventually more filters. 100,000 Hours is the duration/public-presence case: how much of this existed in public, how the number is counted, and what accumulated during those hours.

The deeper pages should therefore feel like rooms reached from the portrait. A reader can meet “Ladder” here, see the dated formulation, open the relevant Story episode, inspect the public source, compare the theology, then return to the portrait without losing orientation.
